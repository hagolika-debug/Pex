In January 1919, Boston woke to a terrifying, sticky tsunami. A massive 2.3 million gallon molasses tank burst without warning. The syrup surged[0.5s] at 35 miles per hour, crushing everything. Twenty‐one people died, dozens more injured, horses perished too. Cadets from a nearby training ship dove into the gooey chaos. For decades, Boston's streets smelled of sweet molasses on hot days. A massive lawsuit forced new construction safety laws across the nation. [1s]One hundred years later, a plaque marks the sticky tragedy. Imagine a wave of syrup[0.5s] swallowing a city—history's strangest disaster.
