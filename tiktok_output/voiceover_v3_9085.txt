Imagine a tiny nation born from a vague border line.[1s] In 1832, 300 settlers declared independence as the Republic of Indian Stream. They wrote a constitution, complete with a bill of rights.[0.5s] Double taxation tormented them—both the U.S. and British collectors demanded payments. A British sheriff’s arrest sparked an international incident and a skirmish. The U.S. militia occupied the land in late 1835, ending its autonomy.[1s] By 1842, the Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally settled the border. Today, Pittsburg, New Hampshire, holds the memory of that forgotten republic. A hidden slice of history, waiting for curious explorers.[0.8s]
