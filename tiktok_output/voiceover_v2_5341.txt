In the 1880s, miners forged a wild gold city on the Amur. They called it Zheltuga, a “California” far beyond U.S. borders. Russians, Chinese, even a few Europeans gathered, reaching twelve thousand souls.[1s] They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, symbolizing earth and gold. Law was written, punishments brutal[0.5s] – 500 lashes for thieves. A bustling theater, casino, and circus thrived among the mining pits. But Qing China soon demanded the illegal settlement disappear. Chinese troops arrived, burned Zheltuga to ashes, ending the dream.[0.8s] Today, the gold is gone, but the story whispers through history.
