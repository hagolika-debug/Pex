In the 1880s, a gold rush[0.5s] birthed a rogue republic. On the Amur River’s banks, miners built a city of dreams. At its peak, twelve thousand hopeful prospectors from across the world gathered. They elected presidents, levied taxes, and raised a black‐and‐yellow flag. A strict code punished thieves with whips[1s]; even drunkenness earned lashes. Women were forbidden, spawning a hidden red‐light district across the river. In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation; the miners ignored the warning. Chinese troops returned in 1886, razing the settlement to ash[0.8s]. The vanished 'California on the Amur' remains a forgotten tale of ambition.
