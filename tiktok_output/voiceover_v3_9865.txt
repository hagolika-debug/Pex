In 1900, St. Louis streets turned into a battlefield.[1s] Three thousand workers demanded fair wages, safety, and union rights. Company fired them all, hiring armed police and a citizen posse.[0.5s] Violence erupted: stone‐filled tracks, burning cables, gunfire echoing downtown. On June 10, the posse shot three strikers returning from a picnic. Fourteen dead, two hundred wounded; the city trembled in fear.[1s] The strike ended in September, sparking reforms that reshaped city politics. Today, few remember the bloodied rails that forged modern labor rights.
