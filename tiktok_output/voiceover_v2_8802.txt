In 1864, Spain set sail to reclaim its lost empire. They seized the guano‐rich Chincha Islands, Peru’s cash cow. A clash at Talambo turned locals against foreign troops. Spain demanded apology; Peru refused, tensions boiled over. The Battle of Papudo, 1865: Chile captured the Spanish schooner Covadonga. Shocked, Admiral Pareja took his own life aboard the Villa de Madrid.[1s] Spain bombarded Valparaíso, setting the harbor ablaze. May 2, 1866, the fierce Battle of Callao raged. Both sides claimed victory, but Spain’s Pacific ambitions crumbled.[0.8s] The war ended, leaving a legacy of naval modernization in South America.
