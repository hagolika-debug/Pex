In 1872, Huntington, New York, witnessed a grisly tar‐and‐feathering. Educated farmer Charles Kelsey chased love, sparking townwide scandal. His advances offended Julia Smith’s guardian, leading to a deadly trap. On November 4, masked men seized Kelsey, stripped him naked. They drenched him in hot tar, then cruelly feathered his skin.[1s] The mob displayed his tormented form to gathering women, then vanished. [0.8s]Later, his mutilated legs washed ashore, sparking horrified whispers. Huntington earned the nickname ‘Tar Town,’ dividing residents into Tars and Anti‐Tars. The gruesome case faded, but its chilling phrase lives on today.
