In 1903, a silent war erupted in West Virginia's hills. Coal miners, starving for rights, faced armed company enforcers. February 25th, a posse of fifty stormed the tiny village of Stanaford. Gunfire ripped through shacks, echoing through pine‐filled valleys. Three miners died in their beds, bullets tearing through nightclothes. Mother Jones arrived, finding blood‐soaked mattresses and weeping children.[2s] The posse claimed justice, but a coroner's jury called it murder. Today, no marker stands; the hills keep the story hidden.[1.5s] Remember Stanaford, the forgotten spark that ignited America's labor wars. History whispers, waiting for us to listen.[0.8s]
