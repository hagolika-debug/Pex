January 15, 1919: Boston's North End awoke to an impossible roar. A massive steel tank, holding 2.3 million gallons of molasses, suddenly collapsed. The liquid wave surged, thirty‐five miles per hour, drowning streets.[1s] Twenty‐one souls perished, horses flailed, buildings cracked under the syrupy pressure. Cadets from the USS Nantucket rushed in, pulling survivors from the sticky nightmare. Rescuers waded knee‐deep, fighting a viscous tide that clung like quicksand.[0.8s] The city sued the company, sparking America’s first massive class‐action lawsuit. Regulations changed overnight, demanding stronger tanks and proper engineering oversight forever. Even a century later, the sweet scent lingers on hot Boston afternoons.[0.5s]
