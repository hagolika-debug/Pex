In 19th‐century Somerset, a secretive cult thrived behind stone walls. The Agapemonites gathered at the Chapel of the Holy Ghost in Spaxton. Leader Henry James Prince declared himself the living Holy Spirit, commanding absolute obedience. Members were forbidden to leave[1s]; loyalty was enforced with terrifying threats. The wealthy Nottidge sisters surrendered fortunes, believing marriage to followers was divine. Behind closed doors[0.5s], bizarre sexual rites claimed to embody the Holy Spirit. Scandal erupted when locals discovered the rituals, forcing Prince into exile. After Prince's death, John Hugh Smyth‐Piggott proclaimed himself Jesus, sparking riots. The once‐sinister estate now rents as an Airbnb[0.8s], echoing its eerie past.
