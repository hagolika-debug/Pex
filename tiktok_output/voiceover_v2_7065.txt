October 21, 1904, the Russian Baltic Fleet heads into the North Sea. They feared Japanese torpedo boats stalking their path at night. A distant light flickered—was it a trawler or a threat?[1s] Mistook the harmless British fishing boats for Japanese attackers. The fleet unleashed cannon fire[0.5s], lighting up the dark water. Two fishermen died, six injured; a trawler sank beneath the waves. Russian ships even fired on each other, mistaking allies[1s] for enemies. Diplomacy teetered—Britain prepared its navy for war. The incident forced Russia to pay £66,000, ending the crisis.
