In 1184, noblemen gathered in Erfurt for a crucial council. They debated land disputes beneath ancient wooden floors, unaware of danger. Suddenly[0.8s], the floor gave way, plunging nobles into a foul cesspit. Drowning in excrement, some were crushed, others suffocated by stench. King Henry clung[0.5s] to iron rails, escaping the deadly flood. Sixty to a hundred nobles perished, reshaping the empire's power balance. The very dispute they meant to resolve vanished[1s] beneath the toilet. History remembers the Erfurt Latrine Disaster as a macabre cautionary tale.
