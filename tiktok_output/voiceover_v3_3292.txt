January 1919, Boston's North End, a massive sugar tank loomed. The 2.3‐million‐gallon steel tank was built on shaky foundations.[0.8s] On a warm day, the tank burst, releasing a sticky wave.[1s] A 25‐foot wall of syrup surged through streets at 35 mph. Cars flipped, horses drowned, and people were trapped in the brown torrent.[0.5s] Rescue crews waded through syrup, pulling victims from the viscous nightmare. The disaster claimed 21 lives and injured over a hundred. Boston sued the company, sparking America's first massive class‐action lawsuit. Even a century later, the sweet scent still haunts the neighborhood.
