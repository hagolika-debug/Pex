In 1857, Washington’s grand National Hotel became a deadly mystery. President‐elect James Buchanan checked in, unaware of the looming danger. Within days, guests fell sick with fever, vomiting, and delirium. By summer’s end, over three hundred were ill and thirty died. [1s]Rumors spread: poisoned water, political assassination, or foul sewage miasma. Doctors blamed noxious vapors from backed‐up sewage beneath the hotel. [0.8s]The outbreak halted Buchanan’s inauguration speech, leaving the nation shaken. Modern scientists suspect a cholera or dysentery outbreak from contaminated water. The National Hotel vanished in 1942, its story forgotten by most.[0.5s]
