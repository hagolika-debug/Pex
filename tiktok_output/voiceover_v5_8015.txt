Imagine a tiny nation hidden between the US and Canada. A vague 1783 treaty left the headwaters of the Connecticut River disputed. Three hundred settlers paid taxes to both America and Britain, furious. On July 9, 1832, they declared the Republic of Indian Stream. A handwritten constitution created a tiny government for just three hundred people. The New Hampshire sheriff demanded surrender.[1s]The militia marched, but no shots fired. A tiny hardware‐store debt sparked an international crisis between empires. By August 1835, Indian Stream capitulated and rejoined New Hampshire. Today, a marker stands,[0.8s] remembering a three‐year republic that defied borders.
