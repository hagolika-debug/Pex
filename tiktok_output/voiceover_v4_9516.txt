In 1857, Washington's grandest hotel became a death trap. President-elect James Buchanan[0.5s] checked in, unaware of looming disaster. Within days, guests fell ill with fever, vomiting, and delirium. Over thirty died, sparking rumors of poison and political assassination. The city panicked.[1s]Doctors blamed foul sewage fumes. Scientists later called it the mysterious National Hotel disease. Modern analysis suggests contaminated water, not a deliberate toxin. The outbreak faded, the hotel demolished, memory erased. A forgotten scandal that shaped public health and politics forever.[1s]
