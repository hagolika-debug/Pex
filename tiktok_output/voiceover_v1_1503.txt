October 17, 1814, London witnessed a disaster unlike any other. A massive wooden vat at the Horse Shoe Brewery burst open. Over 320,000 gallons of frothy porter cascaded onto the streets. Neighbors scrambled, scooping beer into any container they could find. A wave slammed into tenements, crushing walls and claiming lives.[1s] Eight people perished, including a mother and her daughter drinking tea. Even a wake for a toddler was swallowed by the torrent. The flood lingered, the stench of beer filling alleys for months.[0.8s] The tragedy forced breweries to replace wooden vats with safer concrete ones.
