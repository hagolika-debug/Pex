In 897, Rome staged its strangest trial ever. Pope Stephen VI exhumed his dead predecessor, Pope Formosus. The corpse was propped on a throne, silent and rotting. A deacon answered for the dead, pleading with a silent audience.[1s] Stephen accused Formosus of perjury, usurping the holy seat.[0.5s] The dead pope was stripped, his fingers cut off brutally. The verdict: guilty. All his acts declared null and void. Crowds erupted, chanting for justice as the body floated down the Tiber.[1s] Later that year, the synod was reversed, restoring Formosus's legacy.
