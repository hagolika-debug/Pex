A tiny strip of Italy became a free republic by mistake. Two surveyors chose different streams, leaving a 330‐hectare gap. On February 25, 1441, villagers declared independence[1s], tax‐free. No army, no prisons; trade flowed freely through its borderless streets. In 1574 they planted tobacco, becoming Italy’s first legal growers. 1642 the Pope banned smoking, but Cospaia ignored the curse[0.8s]. Merchants smuggled salt and silk, paying nothing to anyone. 1826 Pope Leo XII forced its surrender[1s]; citizens received a silver coin. Today Cospaia is a hamlet, its flag still fluttering in memory.
