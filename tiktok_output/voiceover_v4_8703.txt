A covert Swedish settlement sprouted along the Delaware River in 1638. Peter Minuit, former Dutch governor, led the expedition for revenge. They landed quietly, hidden among the reeds.[1s]The Dutch never saw them. Fort Christina rose, named after Sweden’s teenage queen, marking America’s first permanent settlement. The colony introduced log cabins, a symbol still echoing across frontier America. Life was harsh.[0.8s]Winter threatened, but Native allies shared food. Governor Johan Printz expanded forts, yet the population never exceeded four hundred. In 1655, Peter Stuyvesant’s fleet arrived; the Swedes surrendered without bloodshed. New Sweden vanished, but its legacy lives in log cabins and cultural festivals.
