In 1883, gold fever sparked a hidden city on the Amur River. Miners called it the 'California of the Far East'. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors from Russia, China, and beyond gathered. They elected a president, drafted statutes, and even raised their own flag[1s]. Hotels, theaters, and a casino thrived, while whiskey fueled nightly brawls[0.5s]. Violators faced brutal whips; the community feared both crime and chaos. In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, but miners ignored the warning[0.8s]. Chinese troops returned in 1886, razed the settlement, and scattered its people. Today, the ghost of Zheltuga whispers a forgotten chapter of gold rushes.
