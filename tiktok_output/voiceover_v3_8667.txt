In 1896, Britain demanded Zanzibar obey its ultimatum. Sultan Khalid refused, raising a tiny navy of old ships. At 9:02 am, the British fleet opened fire.[1s] Zanzibar's defenses collapsed within minutes, surrender inevitable. The ceasefire was signed after only thirty‐eight minutes of combat.[0.5s] Britain installed a pro‐British sultan, reshaping East African politics. The war cost Britain just one fatality, a stark contrast. Today, the 38‐minute conflict reminds us how swiftly empires act.[0.8s]
