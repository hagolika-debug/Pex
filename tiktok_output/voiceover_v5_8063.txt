In 1902, a German steamer sparked a deadly Haitian showdown. The Markomannia, bound for Hamburg, docked near Cap‐Haïtien. Rebel gunboat Crête‐à‐Pierrot boarded, accusing the ship of smuggling weapons. Captain Nansen protested, but the rebels seized the cargo anyway. Germany responded, sending the warship SMS Panther to hunt the rebels. A fierce exchange erupted[1s]; the Haitian ship caught fire. Admiral Killick chose a dramatic end, detonating his own magazines. Four crew perished, sinking Crête‐à‐Pierrot into Haitian legend.[0.5s] The incident faded, but it reshaped Caribbean power dynamics forever.
