In 1925, a stray dog sparked a near‐war in the Balkans. Greek soldiers patrolled near Petrich when the dog crossed into Bulgaria. A Greek trooper chased his pet, stepping onto Bulgarian soil. Bulgarian guards opened fire, killing the Greek soldier instantly.[1s] Greece demanded revenge, sending troops into the disputed valley.[0.5s] Villages fled, casualties rose, tensions threatened a broader Balkan conflict. Bulgaria appealed to the League of Nations for urgent intervention. The League ordered a ceasefire, forcing Greece to withdraw. Thus, a tiny dog averted war, showcasing early collective security.[1.5s]
