February 1942, Los Angeles braced for a nightmarish air attack. Just weeks after Pearl Harbor, fear crackled across the West Coast. At 7:18 pm, anti‐aircraft guns roared as searchlights sliced the sky. Citizens fled in panic, sirens blaring, streets awash with darkness.[1s] Over 1,400 shells exploded, yet no enemy planes were ever seen. A lone weather balloon sparked the frenzy[0.8s], igniting war nerves. Five civilians died—three in crashes, two from heart attacks. The Navy declared it a false alarm, but rumors lingered. Tonight, the sky over L.A. remains quiet[0.5s]—no bombs, only memory.
