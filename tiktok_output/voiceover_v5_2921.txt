In 1887, a remote canyon hid a brutal tragedy. Thirty‐plus Chinese gold miners trekked deep into Oregon's Hells Canyon. They sought 'flour gold'—tiny particles shimmering in the river.[0.5s] But a gang of white horse thieves watched from the cliffs. On May 27, guns cracked, echoing through the canyon walls.[1s] Every miner fell; one escaped only to be chased down. The thieves stole the gold, burned the camp, and vanished. The massacre faded from newspapers, hidden for a century.[0.8s] In 1995, forgotten records resurfaced, finally giving victims a voice.
