In 1904[0.5s], the Russian fleet fired on harmless British trawlers. The Baltic Fleet raced to the Far East, fearing Japanese torpedo boats. Foggy night, searchlights blinded the trawlers, crews imagined enemy vessels. Shots rang out[0.8s]; fishermen screamed while Russian ships bombarded each other. Two fishermen died instantly, several wounded, and a trawler sank. Britain demanded answers[1s]; war seemed inevitable between the powers. An international commission convened, applying Hague rules to settle the dispute. Russia paid £66,000 compensation, and Hull erected a solemn memorial. The tragedy warned navies: paranoia breeds friendly fire and disaster. A night of mistaken fire that almost sparked a global war.
