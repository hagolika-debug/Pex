When war clouds gathered, London's banks shattered in silence.[1s] July 1914: the stock exchange halted, trading floors emptied. Queues of frantic citizens swapped paper for gold sovereigns. The government declared a four‐day bank holiday,[0.5s] stopping all payments. Banks reopened in August, yet fear lingered like a cold fog. International markets mirrored London’s collapse, creating a worldwide financial blackout. Lloyd George and Keynes scrambled, trying to calm the trembling economy. As guns roared,[1s] the crisis faded, swallowed by the war’s chaos. History remembers the war, forgetting the financial storm that almost sank it.
