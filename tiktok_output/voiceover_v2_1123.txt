In 1864 Spain seized Peru's guano islands, sparking a bizarre war. The Chincha Islands, tiny desert outcrops, held the world’s richest fertilizer. Spanish Admiral Pinzón demanded apologies for a mob murder, then threatened force. Peru refused, and Spain landed 400 marines, raising their flag over the cliffs. [1s]The seizure crippled Peru’s economy, as guano powered global agriculture. Chile and Bolivia rallied, declaring war on Spain, fearing colonial resurgence. [0.5s]A daring Chilean squadron captured the Spanish vessel Covadonga, turning tides. Spain bombarded Valparaíso, but global protests forced a humiliating retreat. [2s]The Chincha Islands War faded, yet it reshaped South American naval pride forever.
