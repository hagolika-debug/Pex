In 1883, gold sparked a wild frontier on the Amur River. Russian and Chinese prospectors rushed in, building a makeshift town overnight. They named it Zheltuga, the 'California on the Amur'. At its peak, 12,000 souls from ten nations lived side‐by‐side.[0.5s] Miners elected councils, wrote statutes, and even raised a black‐yellow flag. Gambling halls, theaters, and a circus thrived, while lawlessness lurked.[1s] In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, warning of imminent attack.[0.8s] January 1886, Chinese troops razed Zheltuga, scattering its gold‐hungry refugees. The ghost of the Amur’s wild republic still haunts history books.
