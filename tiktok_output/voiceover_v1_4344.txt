In 1913, California's hop fields turned into a battlefield. Rural Wheatland, Yuba County, sweltering summer, tents cramped with hopeful workers. Durst Ranch promised jobs, but only 1500 spots for 2800 desperate migrants. Workers paid per weight, water miles away, tents scorched,[0.5s] latrines filthy. When wages were slashed, pickers formed a makeshift IWW local demanding fairness. Sheriff's posse fired into the air[1s]; chaos erupted, lawmen fell among the crowd. Four dead, hundreds arrested, many beaten,[0.8s] a chilling reminder of labor oppression. The riot sparked California's first farm labor reforms, echoing in modern movements.
