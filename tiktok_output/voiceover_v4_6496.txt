In 1873, a German prophet ignited a deadly Brazilian uprising. Deep in Rio Grande do Sul,[0.5s] isolated farms hid secret gatherings. Jacobina Mentz Maurer claimed visions, calling herself the new Messiah. She warned of apocalypse.[1s] Followers prepared to purge sixteen families. Muckers set fire to rival homes, sparking a brutal retaliation. Imperial troops descended,[2s] burning Jacobina’s house and killing dozens. Survivors fled into forests, but the massacre ended the rebellion. Today, the Mucker War remains a haunting reminder of fanaticism. Few Brazilians remember this blood‐stained chapter of their past. Unearthed archives urge us to learn from vanished uprisings.
