In the 1880s, a gold rush birthed a hidden republic. Nestled on the Amur River, Zheltuga attracted thousands of miners. Russians, Chinese, and even Americans converged, creating a multicultural frontier. The self‐governed town enforced its own law: theft meant flogging. Murderers faced execution, and the gold flowed like water. By 1885, 12,000 souls lived there, thriving amid chaos. [1s] Then the empires intervened, sending troops to crush the outpost. In 1886, Russian and Chinese forces razed Zheltuga, ending its brief glory. [0.5s] Today, the ruins lie silent, a forgotten California on the Amur.
