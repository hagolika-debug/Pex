In 1883, gold fever birthed a wild frontier on the Amur. Russian and Chinese miners built a makeshift city they called Zheltuga.[0.5s] At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors roamed streets like a gold rush metropolis. They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, declaring independence from the Qing. Lawmakers drafted twenty clauses, punishing thieves with brutal whips.[1s] Women were banned, spawning a hidden red‐light district across the river. In 1885, Qing officials finally discovered the outlaw enclave. Chinese troops stormed Zheltuga, burning the town to ash.[0.8s] The phantom republic vanished, leaving only legends of the Amur's California.
