In 1674, a Dutch treasure ship hid in a remote Shetland inlet. Storm‐tossed, it lost masts and sought shelter among jagged cliffs. Local whispers reached the English navy, prompting a swift, secretive fleet. Three warships and a modest dogger entered Ronas Voe, guns ready. A tense standoff unfolded within the narrow, misty fjord.[1s] The Dutch East Indiaman, Wapen van Rotterdam, could not escape. English cannons roared, shattering hulls; over two hundred sailors perished. The survivors were taken to England as prized war booty. Today, a modest cairn marks the Hollanders’ Graves, a forgotten war echo.[0.8s]
