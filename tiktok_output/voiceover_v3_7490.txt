In 1962, a remote Tanzanian school sparked a bizarre contagion. It began with three teenage girls bursting into uncontrollable laughter. Within days, half the students joined, coughing, crying, and giggling wildly.[1s] Teachers tried discipline, but the fits lasted up to sixteen days. The school closed in March, reopening months later to find new outbreaks. Altogether, 1,000 people across fourteen schools were affected. Psychologists called it mass hysteria, fueled by post‐independence stress.[0.8s] The laughter faded after eighteen months, leaving a strange historical footnote. Today, the story reminds us how emotions can spread like viruses.
