A forgotten nation existed between the US and Canada for three years. The 1783 Treaty of Paris left the border ambiguous around Connecticut River headwaters. Both America and British Canada claimed the same land, sending tax collectors. On July 9, 1832, locals declared independence as the Indian Stream Republic. They drafted a constitution, elected officials, and even a justice of the peace.[0.8s] New Hampshire sheriffs threatened invasion; militia gathered in nearby towns. The militia advanced toward Indian Stream.[1.5s]The tiny republic stood its ground. In August 1835, New Hampshire annexed the territory after a standoff. The border dispute finally settled by the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty.[0.5s] Today, Pittsburg, New Hampshire whispers the story of a lost republic.
