Imagine a tiny nation born between two empires, hidden in New Hampshire. A vague 1783 treaty line left the Connecticut River’s headwaters undefined. 300 settlers faced double taxes, law from both the US and British Canada. On July 9, 1832, they drafted a constitution and declared independence. Their government featured three branches, a bill of rights, and a 41‐man militia. But neighboring authorities kept sending officials to collect taxes and issue warrants. A British sheriff arrested a debtor; settlers raided his house, sparking international incident.[1s] New Hampshire militia occupied the area in November 1835, ending the republic.[0.5s] Today it’s Pittsburg, NH—a quiet town remembering its lost sovereignty.
