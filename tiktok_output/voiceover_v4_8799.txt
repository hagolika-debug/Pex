On a sunny May afternoon, a hidden tragedy erupted in Pearl Harbor. Twenty-nine landing ships, tankers, bristled together, packed with fuel and munitions. At 3:08 p.m., a sudden blast tore through LST‐353, igniting everything.[1s] Flames leapt, gasoline vapors exploded, sending fireballs across the loch. Within minutes, over 200 sailors plunged into the sea, many never resurfaced. The Navy imposed a strict press blackout, silencing the disaster for years.[0.5s] Classified until 1960, the West Loch tragedy remained a hidden scar. The calamity forced the Navy to overhaul ammunition handling forever.[0.8s] Today, a modest plaque marks the site, honoring the forgotten heroes.
