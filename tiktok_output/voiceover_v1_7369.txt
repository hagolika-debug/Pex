In 1825, Sunderland’s North Sands beach became a battlefield of blood. Seafaring workers protested wage cuts, blocking ships from leaving the River Wear. Local magistrates called in the 3rd Light Dragoons to crush the strike. Soldiers marched onto the sand, ordering the crowd to disperse peacefully. Instead, they opened fire, sending bullets into families, women, and children. At least seven souls fell, while dozens more were wounded beyond repair. [2s]The tragedy was reported across Britain, yet faded from memory within years. Even Sir Robert Peel, Home Secretary, received the grim news, but took no action. [1s]Today, a plaque marks the sand, reminding us of forgotten sacrifice. Remember North Sands, where the cost of justice was paid in blood.
