In 1814[1s], London was swallowed by a tidal wave of beer. The Meux & Co Horse Shoe Brewery stored millions of gallons in towering wooden vats. A rusted iron hoop slipped, and the massive vat finally gave way. A 15‐foot wall of porter crashed through brick, flooding the slums. Eight souls perished[0.8s]—children, mothers, mourners—caught in the frothy torrent. Crowds flocked, paying a penny to glimpse the beer‐soaked ruins. The disaster bankrupted Meux, yet forced the industry to abandon wooden tanks. Today, the site hosts a theater[1s], but the memory bubbles beneath the streets. A beer tsunami—proof that history can be stranger than fiction.
