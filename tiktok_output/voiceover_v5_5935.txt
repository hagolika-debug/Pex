In 1897, Pennsylvania's anthracite fields turned deadly for hopeful immigrants. They demanded fair wages and safe conditions after years of exploitation. On September 10, a peaceful march gathered near Lattimer village. Sheriff James Martin and armed deputies blocked the crowd, tensions rose. Orders shouted.[1s] Guns opened, spraying the unarmed strikers. Nineteen men fell, many shot in the back while fleeing. The crowd dispersed.[0.8s] Authorities claimed self‐defense for protecting townsfolk. Trials ended in acquittals, the miners' deaths erased from headlines. Today, a lone boulder remembers the forgotten sacrifice at Lattimer.
