In 1905, a hidden tragedy unfolded in Kenya's highlands. The British East Africa Protectorate aimed to control restless Kipsigis tribes. When Kipsigis raided Maasai, stealing cattle and families, tension erupted.[1s] Colonial officials demanded the return of captives, but negotiations stalled. Major Richard Pope‐Hennessy launched a two‐column expedition with Maxim guns. On June 2, soldiers opened fire, killing over a thousand souls.[0.8s] The forces seized twenty thousand cattle, paving the way for white settlers. History erased the massacre, yet today voices demand its truth.[1.2s] Remember the Sotik tragedy, a silent scar on Kenya's past.
