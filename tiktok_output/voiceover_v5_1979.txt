In 1839, a bizarre border dispute sparked a honey-fueled standoff. A misaligned survey misplaced Missouri’s border by miles, confusing everyone. A Missouri farmer cut down three bee trees, stealing honey illegally. Iowa courts tried him in absentia, fining him just $1.50. Both governors issued fiery proclamations, demanding loyalty and threatening arrests. Missouri sheriff entered the disputed zone, surrounded by 1,200 angry Iowans. Militias gathered, armed with flintlocks, pitchforks, and whiskey-fueled anger. [2s]But no shots were fired, only tense stares. Winter hardships forced both sides to stand down, sending the case to Washington. In 1849, the Supreme Court finally defined the boundary, ending the honey war.
