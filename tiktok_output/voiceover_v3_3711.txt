In the 1880s, a hidden gold rush birthed a rebel republic. Nestled on the Amur River's Chinese bank, miners declared independence. They called it Zheltuga, the 'California of the Far East'. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived without law[1s], except their own. A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered over bustling streets, saloons, and a circus. Women were banned[0.5s], so a hidden red‐light district blossomed across the river. The Qing dynasty finally intervened, ordering evacuation within eight days. When soldiers returned, they razed the town[1.2s], erasing its golden dream. Today, only faded maps remember Zheltuga's brief, glittering existence.
