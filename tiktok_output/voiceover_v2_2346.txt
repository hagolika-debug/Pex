Deep in the 1880s, a gold rush birthed a hidden republic. On the Amur River, miners built a lawless city called Zheltuga.[0.5s] At its peak, 12,000 prospectors lived there, a multicultural Gold California. Gold glittered everywhere.[1s]But lawlessness reigned in the streets. They voted, taxed, even built a theater and a casino. Women were banned, yet a hidden red‐light district thrived.[0.8s] The Qing dynasty finally noticed, demanding evacuation within eight days. When soldiers arrived, the settlement burned, disappearing like a phantom. Today, Zheltuga lives only in faded photographs and legends.
