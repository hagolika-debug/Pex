In the remote woods of northern New Hampshire, borders blurred. The 1783 Treaty of Paris left the land's exact boundary undefined. Both America and British Canada claimed the same 200‐square‐mile forest. Settlers paid double taxes, faced two courts, and lived in legal limbo. On July 9, 1832, they gathered and proclaimed independence.[1s]The Republic was born. Their constitution guaranteed elections, juries, and a militia for protection. Loggers, farmers, and traders built homes, schools, and a modest town hall. A constable’s arrest of a Canadian deputy sparked war.[0.8s]The republic fell. Today, a historic marker commemorates the three‐year experiment in self‐governance.
