In July 1518, Strasbourg's streets buzzed with ordinary life. Then Frau Troffea stepped out, dancing uncontrollably, unable to stop. Within days, dozens more joined, their feet moving without reason. City leaders ordered musicians, believing more music would cure the frenzy.[1s] At its peak, four hundred townsfolk danced until they collapsed.[1s] Many succumbed to exhaustion, their bodies broken by endless motion. Historians argue ergot‐poisoned bread or mass psychogenic stress caused it.[0.8s] By early September, the dancing faded, leaving a silent, bewildered town. The 1518 plague remains the last documented case of such mania.
