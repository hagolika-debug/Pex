A hidden gold rush birthed a rogue republic on the Amur. In 1883, miners flooded the riverbanks of Manchuria seeking fortune. Twelve thousand prospectors built a chaotic city of tents and saloons. They elected leaders, taxed gold, and raised a black‐yellow flag. Strict codes punished thieves with brutal whips.[1s] Casinos, theaters, and bustling markets thrived amidst glittering riverside gold. Qing officials finally discovered the settlement and demanded evacuation.[0.8s] In early 1886, Chinese troops razed the town, ending the republic.[1.2s] Legends of Zheltuga whisper still, a forgotten gold fever.
