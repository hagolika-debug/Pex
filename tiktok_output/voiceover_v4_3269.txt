In the 1880s, a hidden gold rush blossomed on the Amur. Russian and Chinese prospectors built a makeshift city, calling it Zheltuga. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived there, defying borders. They forged their own flag, law[0.5s], and even a bustling casino. Women were banned, yet a secretive red‐light district thrived nearby. Gold flowed, but harsh whips punished theft and drunken chaos. In 1885, Qing forces demanded evacuation, delivering a chilling ultimatum. [2s]The settlement burned, its dream reduced to ash. [1s]Today, Zheltuga remains a forgotten 'California on the Amur'.
