In 1910, the British navy targeted a thriving Gulf trade hub. Dubai, a free port, attracted merchants and clandestine arms dealers alike. The British suspected gun‐running and demanded a house search on December 20. When the Sheikh delayed, tension rose, and the navy prepared for action. [1s]At dawn, 100 marines landed, confronting armed townsfolk on the streets. Gunfire erupted; the British responded with HMS Hyacinth’s six‐inch shells. [0.8s]Explosions ripped through the town, killing thirty‐seven civilians. The aftermath forced the Sheikh to surrender rifles and pay reparations. [1.2s]Today, Dubai thrives, yet this violent chapter remains largely forgotten.
