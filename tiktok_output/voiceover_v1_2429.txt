In the remote Amur River, gold sparked an impossible republic. Between 1883 and 1886, miners built a lawless city of 12,000. They called it Zheltuga, the ‘California on the Amur’. Rivals vanished.[1s]The settlers forged their own flag. A black‐and‐yellow banner symbolized both land and glittering gold. Justice was brutal.[0.8s]Whips cracked, lives ended. A makeshift court met under the Eagle Field, dispensing swift punishment. The settlement thrived: theaters, casinos, even a circus performed nightly. Then, empire intervened.[1.5s]Qing troops razed the dream. By 1886, Zheltuga vanished, leaving legends of frontier law.
