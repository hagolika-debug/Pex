In 1883, gold fever sparked a wild frontier on the Amur River. Russian and Chinese prospectors built Zheltuga, the 'California of the Far East'. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived under a black‐and‐yellow flag. They forged their own laws, courts, and even a bustling casino. Women were forbidden; a hidden red‐light district blossomed across the river. In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, but miners ignored the warning. [1s]The Chinese army returned in January 1886, torching the gold town. Survivors scattered, yet the legend of Zheltuga lives in forgotten maps.
