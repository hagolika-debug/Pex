A tiny, unrecognized nation sprang up in New England’s tangled border. Between New Hampshire and Lower Canada, the 1783 Treaty left a vague line. Both sides taxed the same settlers, sparking outrage and a push for independence. On July 9, 1832, they declared independence and drafted a constitution.[1s] A council of five governed, with Luther Parker as justice of the peace. New Hampshire’s sheriff summoned militia; towns rallied, tension rose dramatically.[0.8s] Facing an ultimatum, the assembly capitulated and joined New Hampshire on August 5. The border dispute finally settled by the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty. Today, a historic marker in Pittsburg reminds us of this lost republic. Imagine a nation so small, it vanished without a trace.
