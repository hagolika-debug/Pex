In 897, Rome held a trial unlike any other. Pope Stephen VI exhumed dead Pope Formosus from his tomb. The corpse was placed on a throne inside the grand basilica. He faced charges of perjury, illegal papacy, and holding multiple sees. A deacon answered for the dead, while Stephen declared judgment. [1s]The corpse’s fingers were severed, his papal robes stripped. Finally, the body was cast into the Tiber, drifting through Rome. [0.8s]Angry crowds erupted, leading to Stephen’s imprisonment and eventual strangulation. Later popes annulled the trial, restoring Formosus’s name and legacy. A macabre reminder that even death can become a battlefield for power.
