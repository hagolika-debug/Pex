In 1518, Strasbourg's streets turned into a frantic dance floor. It began with a lone woman, Frau Troffea, moving without music. Within days, dozens joined, compelled to sway, step, and spin. City doctors called it a 'natural disease,'[1s] prescribing more dancing. They cleared a market, hired musicians, built a stage for endless motion. The crowd grew, exhaustion set in, some collapsed[0.8s], others died. Authorities finally banned music, sending the worst to Saint Vitus' shrine. Historians still debate ergot poisoning[0.5s] versus mass hysteria as cause. A reminder that fear and stress can make entire cities dance. The 1518 dancing plague remains one of history’s strangest, documented mysteries.
