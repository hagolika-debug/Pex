In 1184, nobles gathered for a royal court in Erfurt. They sat atop a wooden floor above a massive cesspit. The floor, weakened by rot, couldn't bear their combined weight. With a terrifying crack, the floor gave way.[1s] Hundreds plunged into darkness, landing in foul, bubbling sewage. Around sixty nobles drowned, their titles lost to stench. King Henry VI and the archbishop survived, hanging on a stone ledge.[0.5s] The tragedy shocked the empire, becoming a grim legend for centuries. Even today, the Erfurt latrine disaster remains one of history's dirtiest deaths.[2s]
