In the 1830s, a tiny nation vanished between the US and Canada. A vague treaty left a strip of land unclaimed, sparking chaos. Around 300 pioneers lived there, paying taxes to two governments. On July 9, 1832, they declared independence as the Republic of Indian Stream. They drafted a constitution, elected officials, and even a justice of the peace. Both US sheriffs and British magistrates demanded taxes, leading to double‐tax nightmares.[1s] In 1835, New Hampshire militia marched,[0.8s] forcing the Republic to surrender. The land finally settled by the Webster‐Ashburton Treaty of 1842. Today, the town of Pittsburg holds a marker remembering the lost nation.
