In 1878, a bustling Thames ferry met a tragic fate. The SS Princess Alice left Sheerness, packed with working‐class families. At night near Woolwich[0.5s], she collided with the massive cargo ship Bywell Castle. Water rushed in, drowning hundreds as heavy Victorian dresses weighed them down. The Thames, already polluted[1s], turned the river into a deadly toxic brew. Rescue boats fought currents, but many bodies drifted far downstream. Inquiries blamed the Princess Alice's crew, prompting stricter safety laws. The tragedy spurred public swimming pools across Kent, teaching women to swim. Today, a silent memorial in Woolwich reminds us of those lost[1s].
