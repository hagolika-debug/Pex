In Philadelphia, a quiet maid became science’s hidden heroine. Harriet Cole, a hospital cleaner, died of tuberculosis at thirty‐five. She allegedly willed her body to Dr. Weaver—records remain uncertain.[1s] Weaver spent five months dissecting every nerve, mounting them in glass. It was the first full nervous system displayed outside a corpse.[0.8s] The masterpiece earned a blue‐ribbon award at Chicago’s 1893 World’s Fair. For decades, Harriet taught medical students, appearing in textbooks worldwide. Today she sits behind glass, a reminder of forgotten lives.[1.2s] What stories do unmarked bodies whisper behind museum walls?
