In 1902, a tiny Caribbean port sparked an international naval showdown.[1s] The Haitian rebel gunboat Crête-à-Pierrot seized the German steamer Markomannia. They suspected the ship carried weapons for rival government forces. German captain and consul protested, demanding the vessel continue unhindered. Kaiser’s navy dispatched the cruiser SMS Panther to enforce German rights.[0.5s] Admiral Killick abandoned crew, then blew up his own ship’s magazines. Flames engulfed the rebel vessel, sealing a dramatic end.[0.8s] The U.S. State Department quietly approved Germany’s decisive, yet controversial, action. The incident lingered in Haitian memory, even appearing on a 1943 stamp.
