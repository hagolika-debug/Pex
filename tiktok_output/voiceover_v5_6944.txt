In 1832, a tiny frontier declared itself a sovereign nation. Nestled between New Hampshire and Canada[0.5s], the land was called Indian Stream. The Treaty of Paris left the border ambiguous, sparking a jurisdictional nightmare. Both nations sent tax collectors, double‐taxing three hundred settlers. Fed up, locals formed a constitution on July 9[0.8s], 1832. They elected officials, drafted laws, and even printed their own money. Peace seemed possible[1s]—until New Hampshire’s sheriff demanded militia. A standoff erupted, but the Republic surrendered within weeks. In 1835, the land finally joined New Hampshire, ending its brief independence.
