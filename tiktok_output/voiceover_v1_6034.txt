In 1900, a forgotten massacre unfolded on Russia's Far East frontier. Blagoveshchensk, a bustling river town, teetered on the Amur's edge. Amid the Boxer Rebellion, Russian officials feared Chinese insurgents nearby. They ordered every Chinese resident to evacuate across the river—no boats. Cossacks herded them to the bank, then opened fire as they fled.[1s] Hundreds plunged into the icy Amur, many never resurfaced. The river ran with bodies for days, a silent horror.[0.8s] News was censored, the tragedy vanished from textbooks for decades. Remembering Blagoveshchensk reminds us: history’s darkest chapters demand light.[2s]
