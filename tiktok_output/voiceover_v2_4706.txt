Deep in Siberia, a gold rush birthed a fleeting republic. Between 1883 and 1886, miners built a city on the Amur. Dubbed 'California on the Amur', it swelled to twelve thousand souls. Russians, Chinese, even Koreans lived side by side, speaking a hybrid pidgin. They elected leaders, raised taxes, and built a hospital and theater. But the Qing Empire soon noticed.[1.5s] They could not ignore the illegal gold haven. In 1885, Chinese troops demanded evacuation; miners fled into the frozen wilderness. The settlement burned to ashes.[2s] By 1886, Zheltuga vanished forever. Today, only stories remain of the 'International California' that defied borders. A fleeting dream of lawlessness, gold, and multicultural chaos—forgotten yet fascinating.
