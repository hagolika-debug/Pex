Deep in the 1880s Amur River, a gold fever ignited. Rogue miners from Russia and China built their own lawless city.[0.5s] At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived like a wild frontier.[1s] They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, calling it the Zheltuga Republic. A makeshift court met in the town square, doling out whips.[0.8s] Women were forbidden; a hidden red‐light district thrived across the river. The Qing dynasty finally intervened, demanding evacuation in eight days.[1.2s] In early 1886, Chinese troops burned the town, erasing its memory. Today, the Amur’s banks whisper of a forgotten gold empire.
