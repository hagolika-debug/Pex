July 1920, the Yangtze River simmered with unrest. American merchant ship Alice Dollar suddenly came under rebel fire. USS Monocacy arrived[0.5s], guns ready, to escort the beleaguered vessel. The freighter sped downstream, dodging bursts that lit the night. Next day, rebels[0.8s] struck again near Chongqing, four miles away. Monocacy unleashed rapid cannon fire, forcing attackers to retreat.[1s] Over six hundred rounds rattled the river, yet only fifteen holes appeared. The U.S. consul demanded an apology; China obliged without reparations. The clash intensified gunboat diplomacy, shaping foreign policy in China. A forgotten flash of fire, echoing through diplomatic history.
