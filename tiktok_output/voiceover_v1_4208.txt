In 1902, Sarawak's White Rajah sent troops down the Batang Lupar. Their goal: crush Iban rebels who defied tax and relocation orders. Vyner Brooke, the Rajah’s son, led two English officers on this march. Soon after camp, cholera erupted, killing men before they reached the enemy.[1s] Within days, thousands fell ill; over two thousand died in silence. Bodies floated downstream, shallow graves filled, some tossed from boats into the river. International papers screamed headlines: ‘Punitive Expedition Attacked by Cholera.’[0.8s] The surviving force pressed on, but the disease vanished as mysteriously as it arrived.[1.2s] Today, Sarawak remembers the tragedy with memorials and the haunting tidal bore.
