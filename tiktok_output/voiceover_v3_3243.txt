In 1184, Erfurt's nobles gathered for a royal council. The floor above a hidden cesspit was rotten beyond repair. When the assembly stood, the beams gave way dramatically. They tumbled through wood, crashing onto a foul sewage pit. Nobles screamed as darkness swallowed them helplessly.[1s] The stench was unbearable. Around sixty highborn men perished, their bodies lost in filth. Only the king and archbishop survived, clinging to a stone alcove. The tragedy vanished from history, whispered only in local legend.[0.8s] Yet it shocked medieval Europe. Today we remember Erfurt’s latrine disaster—a gruesome reminder of fragile foundations.
