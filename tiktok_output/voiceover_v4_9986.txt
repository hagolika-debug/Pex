In early 1900s Kentucky, tobacco farmers faced a ruthless monopoly. The American Tobacco Company slashed prices, pushing growers toward ruin. Farmers formed the Planters' Protective Association, hoping to fight back.[1s] But fear grew. Night Riders emerged, cloaked in darkness, riding silently through the hills. December 1906, Princeton burned as masked men detonated tobacco warehouses.[2s] Flames lit the night. The raids spread to Hopkinsville and Russellville, terrorizing entire counties. Law enforcement finally intervened, but the damage to communities was irreversible. The conflict ended when the state guard arrested the Night Riders, restoring order. The Supreme Court later broke the monopoly, reshaping America's tobacco industry. Today, the Black Patch War reminds us how greed can ignite violent resistance.
