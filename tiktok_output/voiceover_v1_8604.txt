In 1832, a tiny borderland declared itself a sovereign nation. Three hundred settlers were fed up with double taxes from two governments. They drafted a constitution, naming it the Republic of Indian Stream. Luther Parker became justice of the peace, guiding the fledgling state. For three years, they held elections, minted their own stamps. [2s] But neighboring New Hampshire demanded control, sending militia troops. A sheriff's ultimatum forced the assembly to surrender peacefully. [1s] By August 1835, the republic was annexed, disappearing forever. Today, Pittsburg, NH marks the spot where a micro‐nation once thrived.
