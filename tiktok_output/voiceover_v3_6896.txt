In 1775, the Baltic city of Tartu burned to ash. A careless spark in a backyard ignited a disaster. Strong winds fanned flames across wooden streets, devouring everything. The river's bridges became tinder.[1s]Water turned to ash. Nearly two hundred wooden houses vanished, stone walls crumbled. Only 160 structures survived, a fraction of the former city. Catherine the Great sent 25,000 rubles.[0.8s]Hope flickered. The fire forced new stone building laws, reshaping Tartu forever. Today, only a handful of pre‐fire walls whisper the city's past.
