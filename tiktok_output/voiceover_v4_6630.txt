In 1887, Ohio farmers launched a daring war against a stagnant reservoir. Built in 1840, Six Mile Reservoir once fed canals, now a mosquito breeding ground. The foul smell and malaria plagued nearby farms, sparking residents' fury. In March, they tried cutting the dike, but icy night forced retreat.[1s] Governor Foraker declared the militia would protect state property.[0.5s] On April 25, 200‐400 men dynamited locks, draining the reservoir's lifeblood. Only casualty: nineteen‐year‐old Private Reeves, who accidentally shot himself. They raised a flag reading 'No Compromise!'—a motto still used today. A forgotten rebellion reminds us ordinary citizens can reshape history.[2s]
