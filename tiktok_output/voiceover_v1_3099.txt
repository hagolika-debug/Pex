In the wild forests of New Hampshire, borders vanished. A vague 1783 treaty left a land claimed by both America and Britain. Three hundred settlers grew tired of paying double taxes.[0.5s] On July 9, 1832, they declared independence, forming a tiny republic. They drafted a constitution, elected officials, and named it Indian Stream. But neighboring sheriffs soon threatened them with force.[1s]The standoff began. Militia marched, yet no shots fired; the dispute escalated peacefully. A trivial hardware‐store debt sparked an international incident. By 1835, the republic surrendered, becoming part of New Hampshire. Today, a marker stands, reminding us of borders that once breathed.
