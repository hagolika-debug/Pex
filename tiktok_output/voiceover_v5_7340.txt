In 1904, a Russian fleet mistook fishermen for enemy warships.[1s] Foggy North Sea, tension high during the Russo‐Japanese War. The Baltic Fleet illuminated tiny trawlers with searchlights, then opened fire. Two British fishermen died, a trawler sank, others were wounded.[0.5s] Confusion sparked Russian ships shooting at each other, fearing Japanese torpedo boats.[0.7s] Britain demanded answers; war seemed imminent between two empires. An international commission forced Russia to pay £66,000 compensation. Hull erected a memorial, reminding us of mistaken aggression. A night of flashlights almost sparked a world war—history’s hidden flashpoint.
