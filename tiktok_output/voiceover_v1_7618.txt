In 1814, London’s streets were about to be flooded with beer. Meux & Co’s massive wooden vat held over a quarter‐million gallons. A slipped iron hoop went unnoticed, weakening the towering container.[0.5s] At 4:30 pm, the vat burst,[1s] unleashing a frothy tidal wave. The 15‐foot wave crashed through St Giles, destroying homes instantly. Eight neighbors, including mourners at a wake, drowned in porter.[0.8s] Spectators paid to view the ruined brewery, turning tragedy into spectacle. The disaster forced brewers to abandon wooden vats for safer concrete tanks. Today, the site is the Dominion Theatre, but the beer tsunami lives on in legend.
