In 1814, London’s Horse Shoe Brewery became an accidental tsunami.[1s] A 22‐foot wooden vat burst, unleashing hundreds of thousands of gallons. The roaring wave of porter crashed into St Giles’ cramped slums. Eight people died, including children at a funeral wake. Spectators paid to watch the smashed vats[0.5s], turning tragedy into spectacle. The disaster bankrupted Meux & Co, yet they survived after a tax rebate. Afterwards, breweries abandoned massive wooden vats for safer concrete tanks. Today, the site houses the Dominion Theatre, a reminder of foam fury. Imagine a tidal wave of beer[0.8s] flooding your street—history’s wildest spill.
