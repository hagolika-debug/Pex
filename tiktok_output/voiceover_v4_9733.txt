In 1809, a mischievous bet turned a quiet London street chaotic.[1s] The challenger, playwright Theodore Hook, wagered he could make any house famous. He sent four thousand false orders to tradesmen, doctors, and clergy. At dawn, chimney sweeps arrived, thinking they were summoned. Soon, coal carts[0.5s], cake bakers, and even undertakers lined the street. The Governor of the Bank, the Lord Mayor, and the Duke appeared. Crowds clogged Berners Street; horses struggled, pianos toppled[1.5s], chaos reigned. Hook revealed the prank, vanished to the countryside, avoiding retaliation.
