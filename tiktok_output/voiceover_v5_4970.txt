In 1835, Ohio and Michigan clashed over a narrow strip of land. A mistaken map placed the Maumee River mouth inside Ohio's borders. Both governments passed laws, threatening hefty fines for crossing the disputed line. Militia troops gathered, muskets ready, but actual combat remained elusive. At Phillips Corners, shots rang out—only into the air, no bloodshed. A lone stabbing left a sheriff's deputy wounded—the war’s only serious injury.[1s] President Andrew Jackson dispatched envoys, pushing both sides toward a compromise. The Frostbitten Convention traded Toledo for Michigan’s Upper Peninsula, rich in iron.[0.5s] Today, the rivalry lives on in football, echoing that forgotten war.
