In 1883, gold sparked a hidden empire on the Amur. Russian and Chinese miners built Zheltuga, a makeshift California in Siberia. At its peak[1s], twelve thousand prospectors lived under a black‐and‐yellow flag. They formed their own laws, courts, and even a bustling theater. Women were banned[0.5s], yet a secret vice economy thrived across the river. In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, giving settlers only eight days. When the deadline passed[2s], Chinese troops returned, razed the town, and vanished. The gold rush ended, but the legend of Zheltuga lives on. A forgotten republic, a fleeting glitter of lawlessness and hope.
