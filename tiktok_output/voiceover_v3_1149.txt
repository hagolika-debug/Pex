Boston's streets turned sweet nightmare on a cold January morning. January 15, 1919, a massive molasses tank burst unexpectedly. Two and a half million gallons of thick syrup surged outward. A 58‐foot wall of goo crashed, crushing everything.[1s]Silence echoed. The wave reached fifteen feet high, racing through two city blocks.[0.5s] Twenty‐one people died, dozens more injured, horses drowned in sticky chaos. Cleanup took weeks; the sour scent lingered for generations.[0.8s]Unforgettable. Court battles lasted years, forcing stricter tank standards nationwide. Today, the Great Molasses Flood remains a sticky reminder of negligence.
