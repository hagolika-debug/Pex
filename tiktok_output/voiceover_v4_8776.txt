In 1441, a tiny Italian strip became a republic by accident. Surveyors chose different rivers, leaving a five‐hundred‐meter no‐man’s land.[1s] Local peasants declared freedom, paying no taxes to anyone. Cospaia thrived as a duty‐free haven for smugglers and traders. When tobacco arrived, the republic ignored papal bans, flourishing. No army, courts, or taxes—just a council of elders ruling. Three centuries passed, untouched...[2s]until the papacy finally intervened. In 1826, Pope Leo XII split Cospaia, ending its autonomy. Today, a festival remembers the forgotten republic’s brief, bold freedom.[1s]
