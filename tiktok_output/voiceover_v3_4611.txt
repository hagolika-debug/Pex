In the early 1800s, a tiny nation rose between the US and Canada. It was called the Republic of Indian Stream, lasting only three years. The cause? Confusing 1783 Treaty language left the land unclaimed. Both Britain and America sent tax collectors, double‐taxing the locals. Fed up, settlers drafted a constitution on July 9, 1832.[1s] They elected officials, collected their own taxes, and even minted coins. In 1835, New Hampshire militia threatened invasion,[0.5s] demanding surrender. The Republic capitulated, becoming part of New Hampshire on August 5. The dispute lingered until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally settled it. Today, the story reminds us how borders can birth fleeting nations.[0.8s]
