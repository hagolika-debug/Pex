In the misty woods of New Hampshire, settlers declared independence. They called it the Indian Stream Republic, a tiny nation. Between 1829 and 1836, they drafted a constitution and elected officials. Taxes were collected, schools built, and a 41‐man militia guarded borders[1s]. Both the United States and British Canada claimed the land, demanding taxes. In 1835, a cross‐border arrest sparked a brief armed conflict[0.8s]. New Hampshire militia marched in, ending the Republic’s short‐lived sovereignty. By May 1836, residents accepted New Hampshire’s authority, dissolving the state. Today, a historic marker whispers the tale of a forgotten border nation[1.5s].
