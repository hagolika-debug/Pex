Imagine a city drowned in sweet, sticky syrup.[1s] On January 15, 1919, Boston's North End faced disaster. A 50‐foot steel tank held 2.3 million gallons of molasses. When it burst,[0.5s] a 35‐mph wave surged through the streets. Buildings shattered, a streetcar lifted, horses flailed in the chaos. Twenty‐one people died, dozens more were badly injured. Cadets, Red Cross nurses, and volunteers waded through the gooey mess.[0.8s] The tragedy reshaped safety laws and left a lingering sweet scent. Today, a plaque marks the spot where molasses once roared. History's sticky secrets remind us: even sweets can turn deadly.
