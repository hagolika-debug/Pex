In 1910, Dubai thrived as a bustling Gulf free port. Yet a hidden gun‐running network fed conflict across Persia and Arabia. British warship HMS Hyacinth sailed into Dubai's waters, demanding inspections. At dawn, marines stormed a trader's house, sparks igniting street battles. The crowd fired first; Hyacinth answered with thunderous six‐inch shells.[1s] Thirty‐seven townspeople died, while four British sailors fell. British imposed heavy reparations, demanding rifles and a new telegraph office. The bombardment silenced the market, but whispers of resentment lingered.[0.8s] Decades later, the incident shaped British policy, lingering in UAE memory.[1.2s]
