London, 1867: a daring prison break plot erupts into disaster. Fenian Brotherhood packed gunpowder into a beer cask[0.5s], aiming for freedom. They miscalculated the charge, causing a massive blast that shattered walls. Hundreds injured, three killed instantly as bricks rained down. The Times called it a ‘crime of unexampled atrocity’.[1s] The city mourned. Public outrage surged, prompting the prime minister to ban demonstrations. The failed rescue became the first Irish bomb on English soil. Only one man, Michael Barrett, was executed[2s], ending public hangings in England. Today, the shattered wall is a forgotten reminder of radical desperation.
