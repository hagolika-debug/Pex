In 1272, a stolen cow sparked a six‐year war. A peasant in Ciney was caught stealing a prized heifer. He was fined, but a corrupt bailiff executed him anyway.[1s] His lord demanded revenge, pulling Namur into the conflict. King Philip III of France stepped in to mediate peace. Battles raged across Condroz, leaving towns burned and fields barren.[0.8s] The war ended in 1278, restoring the status quo ante bellum. Today, the 'Cow War' lives on in local legends and road signs.
