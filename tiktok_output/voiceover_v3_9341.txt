In the early 1830s, a tiny borderland declared independence.[0.8s] Three hundred settlers, double taxes, and two empires clashed. They named it the Republic of Indian Stream, writing its own constitution. A small river gave this micro‐nation its identity.[1s] For three years, they elected officials and ran a modest government. But the New Hampshire sheriff demanded surrender, sparking a standoff.[0.5s] Militia arrived, yet no shots were fired; the republic fell peacefully.[1s] The dispute lingered until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally settled it. Today, a lone historic marker reminds us of this vanished nation.
