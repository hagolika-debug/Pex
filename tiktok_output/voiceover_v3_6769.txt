In 1830, a ragtag band of convicts sailed into forbidden Japan. They hijacked the brig Cyprus[0.5s], escaping Tasmania's brutal penal colony. The ship limped to Mugi Cove, Shikoku, seeking water and firewood. Japanese samurai, hidden as fishermen, approached, wary of the foreign flag. The crew pleaded in sign language[1s], eyes pleading for aid. Samurai fired warning cannon, the ship rocked but refused to flee. After days of stalemate, the convicts finally repaired sails and escaped. Historians dismissed the tale[0.5s] until 2017 archival discovery proved it real. The Cyprus became the first Australian‐built ship to set foot in Japan. A pirate’s desperate voyage rewrote maritime history, hidden for nearly two centuries.
