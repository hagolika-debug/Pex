In 1859, two nations eyed a tiny island in the Pacific. The Oregon Treaty left the boundary vague, sparking a silent rivalry. A farmer shot a roaming pig, demanding payment for his ruined potatoes. British officials threatened arrest; Americans called for troops, raising tensions. Both sides landed soldiers, cannons aimed, yet no shots fired.[1s] President Buchanan sent General Winfield Scott; negotiations kept war from erupting. The island hosted both flags side by side, a strange peace. In 1872, Germany arbitrated; the United States kept the islands. Today, the Pig War reminds us how tiny sparks can ignite global tension.[0.8s]
