In 1910, Dubai was a bustling free port, thriving trade. But beneath the market stalls, a secret arms trade[0.5s] fed regional conflicts. British officials, fearing rebellion, demanded to search a local merchant’s house. When the search revealed hidden rifles, tensions erupted into gunfire. The crowd surged, stones flew,[1s] and the British responded with artillery. HMS Hyacinth unleashed six‐inch shells, tearing through Dubai’s narrow streets. Thirty‐seven townspeople died,[0.8s] leaving a scar on the city’s memory. The British withdrew, but their demands reshaped Dubai’s political landscape forever. A forgotten bombardment that sparked modern Dubai’s path to global prominence.
