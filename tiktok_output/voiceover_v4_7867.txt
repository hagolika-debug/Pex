In 1918, U.S. soldiers marched into the Arctic wilderness. President Wilson sent troops to protect Allied stockpiles in Arkhangelsk. They became known as the Polar Bears, fighting Bolshevik forces. Harsh winter froze rivers, turning battlefields into icy death traps.[1s] Disease ravaged the camp; Spanish flu claimed dozens of lives.[0.5s] In 1919, the troops withdrew, leaving a frozen legacy.[0.8s] The world forgot them, but their graves stand in Michigan. Today, the Polar Bear Memorial reminds us of this hidden war.[1s]
