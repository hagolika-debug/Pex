1932, Western Australia faced an unexpected enemy: flightless, towering emus. Farmers blamed the birds for ruining wheat harvests during the Great Depression. They begged the government for machine‐guns, promising food and shelter. In November, soldiers arrived with Lewis guns, ready for a ‘war’. First burst: emus scattered like tiny tanks[0.5s], bullets missed their marks. Major Meredith joked the birds were better soldiers than his men.[1s] After weeks of futile shooting, the army withdrew, humbled by feathers. The ‘Emu War’ became a legend of nature outsmarting warfare.[0.8s]
