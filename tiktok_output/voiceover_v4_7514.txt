In the remote New Hampshire woods, a tiny nation emerged. A vague 1783 treaty left two empires fighting over one stream.[0.5s] Settlers paid double taxes, caught between American sheriffs and British officers. Fed up, they declared independence on July 9, 1832. They drafted a constitution, elected leaders, and named it Indian Stream.[1s] A hardware‐store debt sparked a sheriff’s ultimatum, threatening militia invasion. Without a shot fired, the militia entered, ending the republic.[1s] The dispute finally settled by the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty. Today, a lone historical marker whispers the story of this forgotten nation.
