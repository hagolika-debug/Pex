In the 1880s, a golden oasis sprang on the Amur River.[1s] Russian and Chinese prospectors illegally settled, forming a wild frontier town. They called it the 'California on the Amur', a glittering lawless haven. At its peak, 12,000 miners from dozens of nations roamed its streets.[0.5s] Miners elected a council, drafted statutes, and raised a black‐yellow flag. Without women, a secret brothel district thrived across the river. In 1885 Qing officials demanded evacuation[1s]; the miners fled. January 1886 Chinese troops razed the settlement, ending the republic. The ghost town vanished, but its story echoes as a forgotten gold rush.
