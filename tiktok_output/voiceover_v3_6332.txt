In 1913, the Bogd Khanate launched a daring invasion into Inner Mongolia. They attacked from five directions, hoping to free their southern brethren. At first, Mongol troops swept across the steppes like a storm. But supply lines crumbled, and winter froze their dwindling provisions. Russian diplomats warned Moscow, urging a halt to the reckless campaign.[1s] Facing mounting pressure, Chinese forces launched a fierce counterattack. Mongol soldiers retreated, abandoning the territories they had briefly claimed. The conflict ended with a 1915 treaty, restoring Chinese control. Today, few remember this forgotten war of the steppes.[2s]
