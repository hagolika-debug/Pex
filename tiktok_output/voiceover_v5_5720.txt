April 1887, a French inspector vanished near the Franco‐German border. Guillaume Schnaebelé was seized by disguised German agents on a quiet road. [1s]He protested, claiming diplomatic immunity, but the Germans ignored him. The arrest ignited a feverish French press, demanding war against Germany. [0.8s]Bismarck intervened, releasing Schnaebelé to avoid a continent‐wide conflict. Paris officials debated an ultimatum, but fear of another war prevailed. The crisis faded, yet it exposed fragile peace and secret espionage. Today, a small bridge in Pagny‐sur‐Moselle bears his forgotten name. [1.5s]A reminder that a single arrest once teetered Europe on the brink.
