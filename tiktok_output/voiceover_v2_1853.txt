On October 17, 1814, a London street faced an unexpected wave. The massive wooden vat at Meux's Horse Shoe Brewery finally gave way. A snapped iron ring[0.5s] triggered a 320,000‐gallon beer tsunami. The roaring wave smashed walls, flooding the cramped St Giles slums. Neighbors scrambled, scooping beer into buckets, some drinking it blindly. [2s]Eight lives were lost, including a mother and her daughter. A funeral wake turned tragedy as mourners drowned in the frothy flood. [1s]The disaster forced breweries to replace wooden vats with concrete. Today, the Dominion Theatre stands where beer once roared across streets.
