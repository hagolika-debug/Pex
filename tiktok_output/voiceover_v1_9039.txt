In 1962, a girls' school in Tanganyika became the epicenter. Students erupted into uncontrollable laughter during a quiet lesson.[1s] The giggles spread like wildfire to nearby villages within days. Victims couldn't stop laughing, some even fainted from exhaustion. Doctors called it a mass psychogenic illness, baffling experts.[0.8s] The epidemic lasted months, affecting hundreds of teenagers and teachers. Classes were halted, and the community grappled with daily chaos. Psychologists suggested stress and cultural pressures fueled the outbreak. Today, the laughter epidemic remains a forgotten chapter of history.[1.2s]
