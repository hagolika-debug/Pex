In 1835, Ohio and Michigan nearly clashed over a tiny strip. The disputed land stretched 468 square miles, known as the Toledo Strip. A faulty map misplaced Lake Michigan’s southern tip, sparking the conflict. Both sides raised militias[0.8s], but only one bloodshed ever occurred. A Michigan militiaman stabbed an Ohio surveyor— the war’s sole injury. President Andrew Jackson[1s] declared the strip part of Ohio, ending the standoff. Ohio kept Toledo, while Michigan received the Upper Peninsula as compensation. The Upper Peninsula later yielded massive copper and iron wealth for Michigan. A forgotten border dispute that shaped two states’ destinies.
