December 13, 1867, a massive explosion shattered Clerkenwell’s narrow lanes. Fenians aimed to free two imprisoned comrades with a daring prison break.[0.5s] They overloaded a barrel of gunpowder, causing a blast far beyond expectations. Houses collapsed, windows shattered, and dozens were killed or maimed instantly.[1s] Among the dead: a 47‐year‐old bricklayer, a seamstress, and a seven‐year‐old girl. Prime Minister banned public demonstrations, fearing further Fenian violence. Curious onlookers paid sixpence to view the ruins, turning tragedy into spectacle. The community raised thousands of pounds, feeding families and rebuilding shattered homes.[0.8s] It marked England’s first Irish bomb attack and the last public execution of a Fenian.
