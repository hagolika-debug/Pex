In the early 1900s, Kentucky tobacco farmers faced a ruthless monopoly. The American Tobacco Company bought out rivals, slashing prices to near zero. Farmers created the Planters' Protective Association, hoping to force fair prices. When negotiations failed, masked Night Riders[0.5s] formed the Silent Brigade. December 1906, they stormed Princeton, Kentucky, setting tobacco warehouses ablaze. Flames roared, smoke choked the sky.[1s] The town trembled in terror. State militia intervened, arresting leaders and ending the night terror. The war forced the monopoly's breakup, reshaping America's tobacco industry. Today, the Night Riders' legacy warns us about power and resistance.
