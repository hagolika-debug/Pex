In 1889, a tiny Caribbean rock became a battlefield.[0.5s] Navassa Island, a guano mining outpost owned by the United States. Only 115 black laborers lived there, under harsh white overseers. On September 14, they rose, demanding dignity and fair wages. [2s]Shots rang out, four supervisors fell dead. The U.S. Navy landed, capturing the rebels without mercy. Forty‐three were tried; three sentenced to death, later spared. [1s]The case reached the Supreme Court, testing the Guano Islands Act. Today, Navassa is a quiet wildlife refuge, its revolt forgotten.
