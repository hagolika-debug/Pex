In 1910, British warship HMS Hyacinth stormed tiny Dubai. The mission: seize suspected gun‐running caches hidden in merchant homes. British marines landed at dawn, demanding the ruler open a locked cellar. The Sheikh hesitated,[1s] buying precious minutes for townsfolk to arm themselves. Shots erupted; civilians fired from rooftops, turning streets into a battlefield. HMS Hyacinth unleashed six‐inch shells,[0.8s] shaking adobe walls and lives. Thirty‐seven locals perished, while four British sailors died in the chaos. The aftermath forced Dubai's ruler to surrender rifles and pay hefty reparations. A forgotten clash that reshaped Gulf politics,[1.2s] hidden in dusty archives.
