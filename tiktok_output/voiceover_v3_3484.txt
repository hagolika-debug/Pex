In 1441 a cartographer’s slip created a nation out of nowhere. A thin strip, five hundred meters wide, lay between Florence and the Papacy. Villagers seized the chance, declared independence.[1s] No taxes, no rulers. A council of elders ran the republic from a modest church hall. In 1574 they cultivated tobacco, becoming Italy’s first free‐trade hub. When the Pope banned smoking, Cospaia laughed.[0.8s] Smugglers thrived. For 385 years the tiny state survived, unnoticed on any official map. In 1826 Pope Leo XII forced its surrender; coins marked the goodbye. Today Cospaia is a quiet hamlet, but its story endures.[1.2s]
