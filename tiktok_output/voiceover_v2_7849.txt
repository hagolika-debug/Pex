September 1922, New York streets simmered under a scorching summer heat.[0.5s] Men wore straw boater hats until September fifteen—then felt hats took over. Teenagers took the rule personally, hunting down anyone still sporting straw. It all ignited on September thirteenth.[1s] Youths ripped hats from factory workers. The fight spilled onto the Manhattan Bridge, grinding traffic to a halt. Police arrived, but teenagers outnumbered them, snatching hats and smashing them. By night, hat shops stayed open, selling felt replacements.[0.8s] Business boomed. Eight days of chaos ended with dozens injured, but no fatalities. A bizarre fashion feud reminds us how small customs can spark riots.
