In 1900, a hidden tragedy unfolded on Russia’s Amur River. Thousands of Chinese residents were forced to flee across icy waters. [1s]Orders came from Lieutenant‐General Gribskiy, demanding expulsion. Cossacks herded families toward the riverbank under gunfire. [0.5s]Many tried to swim, but cold currents swallowed them. Those who survived were shot on the opposite shore. The massacre claimed over three thousand lives, erased from textbooks. [1.2s]Centuries later, descendants still mourn the silent river. Why does this brutal chapter stay buried? Remembering hidden horrors shapes a more honest future.
