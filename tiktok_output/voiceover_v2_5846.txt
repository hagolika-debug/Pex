October 1904, the Russian Baltic Fleet sails toward the Far East. Fog rolls over the North Sea; fishermen haul in their nets. Russian lookouts spot tiny lights, convinced they're Japanese torpedo boats. [0.5s]Admiral Rozhestvensky orders the fleet to open fire. [1s]Shells slam into wooden trawlers; men scream as boats burn. Russian ships mistakenly fire on each other in the darkness. [2s]The attack kills two British fishermen, injures six more. Britain threatens war; an international commission steps in. Russia pays £66,000 compensation, and the world barely remembers.
