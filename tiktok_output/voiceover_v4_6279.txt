In 1910, Brazil’s navy faced a fiery, unexpected rebellion. Black sailors, fed up with brutal whippings, seized the dreadnoughts.[0.5s] Leader João Cândido, later called the Black Admiral, demanded justice. The mutineers threatened Rio, but chose to fire over the city.[1s] Government hesitated, fearing destruction of its brand‐new battleships. After tense negotiations, Congress granted amnesty, ending corporal punishment. The ships were later disarmed, their power quietly stripped away.[0.8s] Many rebels faced exile, imprisonment, and harsh Amazon labor camps. The Revolt of the Lash reshaped Brazil’s navy and sparked human rights debate.
