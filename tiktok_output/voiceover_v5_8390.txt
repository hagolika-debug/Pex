In 1919, Boston faced a sweet disaster that shocked the city. A massive 2.5‐million‐gallon molasses tank loomed over the North End. On a chilly January afternoon[0.5s], the steel walls finally gave way. A wave of sticky syrup roared, 15‐feet high, crushing everything. People screamed, horses whinnied, chaos erupted.[1s] The city drowned. Twenty‐one souls perished, many suffocated by the thick, cold syrup. Rescue crews struggled as the molasses hardened, turning streets into cement. [0.5s] Even years later, Boston's air still smelled sweetly of molasses. Victims sued, forcing stricter building codes and corporate accountability. A sticky tragedy that reminds us: even harmless liquids can become lethal.
