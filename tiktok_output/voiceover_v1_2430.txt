In 1872, after a brutal war, Paraguay begged for settlers. London financiers sold a dream: fertile fields for impoverished families. They were called Lincolnshire Farmers, though most came from London’s slums. Eight hundred souls boarded steamships, hoping for a new life.[1s] Arriving at Itapé, they faced swamp, disease, and starvation.[0.5s] Children cried for clean water while snakes coiled in the wells. After months of suffering, the British consul intervened, arranging rescue.[0.8s] Hundreds trekked to Rosario, Argentina, where they finally found safety. The failed colony left a haunting reminder of greed and broken promises.
