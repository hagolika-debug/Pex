In 1739, Portsmouth hid a chilling secret beneath its streets. Winter's cold grip covered a well where tragedy began. A newborn girl floated in darkness.[1s]The town held its breath. Eyes turned to Sarah Simpson, a widowed mother in poverty. She led constables to a river grave, claiming stillbirth. Colonial law demanded death for hidden bastards; the court agreed. Snow fell heavily.[0.8s]The gallows rose, awaiting two condemned women. On December 27, both women hanged, crowds shivering in silence. History remembers.[1.2s]A silent warning echoing through centuries.
