In August 1915, New York buzzed with a shocking espionage scandal. German attaché Heinrich Albert lost a leather briefcase on a crowded train. Inside lay secret documents exposing German money schemes and sabotage plans. British agents, led by Victor Voska, orchestrated the theft to cripple Germany. The papers vanished, then reappeared in the New York World,[0.5s] causing uproar. President Wilson’s cabinet debated suppression, fearing war‐time panic.[1s] But the story leaked, revealing hidden British influence in America. The briefcase never resurfaced; its contents remain a mystery today. A century later[0.8s], historians still debate who truly stole the papers.
