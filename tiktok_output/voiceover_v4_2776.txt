Imagine a wave[1s] of sweet syrup swallowing a city. January 15, 1919, Boston's North End faced an unimaginable disaster. A 50‐foot steel tank held 2.3 million gallons of molasses. Warmer weather expanded the thick liquid, cracking the weak rivets. At 12:30 pm, the tank burst,[0.5s] releasing a 35‐mph wave. Streets turned waist‐deep in brown syrup,[1s] crushing horses and people alike. Twenty‐one lives lost, over a hundred injured, the city stunned. The disaster sparked the first major US class‐action lawsuit. Even a century later,[0.8s] locals swear they can still smell molasses.
