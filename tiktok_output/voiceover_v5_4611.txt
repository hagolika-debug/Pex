In 1638, Sweden secretly settled the Delaware Valley.[0.5s] Peter Minuit, former Dutch director, guided the venture.[1s] The Kalmar Nyckel sailed with 25 hopeful Swedes and Finns. They founded Fort Christina, the first European settlement in Delaware.[0.8s] Swedish log cabins introduced a building style that defined the frontier. For 17 years the colony thrived peacefully alongside Native tribes. In 1655, Peter Stuyvesant's fleet forced the Swedes to surrender.[1s] The land became New Netherland, but Swedish culture lingered. Today, Wilmington's Old Swedes' Church still whispers that hidden chapter.[0.5s]
