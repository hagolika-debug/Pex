A tiny logging town vanished in a single night. December 23, 1918 – relentless rain pounded the Snoqualmie valley. A newly built masonry dam held back a swelling lake.[0.5s] Water seeped, pressure built, then the dam cracked. A roar echoed through the hills.[1.5s] Boxley Creek exploded into a 150‐foot river, racing toward Edgewick. Night watchman Charles Moore blared a whistle, waking the town. Families fled in nightclothes, clutching only what they could. The mill collapsed, but miraculously every resident survived.[0.8s] Edgewick never rebuilt; the creek is now called Christmas Creek.
