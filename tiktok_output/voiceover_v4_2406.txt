In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced a courtroom. Pope Stephen VI ordered Formosus' corpse exhumed.[0.5s] He placed it on a throne. The silent dead was accused of perjury, illegal papacy, and greed. The trial began.[1s] The crowd gasped at the macabre spectacle. A deacon answered for the pope, while bishops whispered in disbelief. Stephen VI cut off Formosus' three blessing fingers, declaring his acts void. The corpse was stripped, dressed as a layman, then cast into the Tiber.[0.8s] Public outrage erupted; Stephen was imprisoned, strangled, and the synod later annulled.
