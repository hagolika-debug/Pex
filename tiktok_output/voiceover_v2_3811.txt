In the early 1800s, a forgotten nation sprang up in New Hampshire. A vague 1783 treaty left the border undefined[0.8s], creating a lawless wilderness. 300 settlers faced double taxes[0.5s] from both the United States and British Canada. In July 1832, they drafted a constitution and declared independence[0.7s]. Their government featured a bill of rights, three branches, and even a militia. Skirmishes erupted when a British sheriff arrested a local over a debt[1s]. The New Hampshire militia finally occupied the territory, ending the republic. The 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally settled the border[0.5s], absorbing the land. Today, Pittsburg, NH, holds markers remembering the short‐lived Republic of Indian Stream. A tiny chapter of history showing borders can create entire nations[1s].
