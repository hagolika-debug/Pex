In the 1830s, two states almost went to war over a strip. The Toledo Strip—just eight miles wide—held fertile farms and a crucial river mouth. A faulty map placed Lake Michigan’s southern tip far south, confusing boundary lines. Ohio’s Governor Robert Lucas and Michigan’s 24‐year‐old 'Boy Governor' Stevens Mason faced off.[0.5s] Both sides raised hundreds of armed militiamen, camping along the Maumee River. At Phillips Corners, militia exchanged fire—only shots into the air,[1s] no casualties. The only serious injury? A penknife wound to a Michigan sheriff’s deputy. Congress traded Michigan’s Upper Peninsula for the disputed strip, ending the war. Michigan gained copper and iron mines, turning the ‘worthless’ land into riches. Today, the Toledo War fuels the fierce Ohio‐Michigan rivalry—on and off the football field.[0.8s]
