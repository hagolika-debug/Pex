In 1830, a rag‐tag crew of convicts stormed a ship. They hijacked the British brig Cyprus while bound for Tasmania’s penal colony. Led by William Swallow, they escaped, sailing across the unforgiving Pacific. After months at sea, they reached Japan’s isolated coast of Mugi.[1s] Samurai guards, cloaked as fishermen, inspected the foreign vessel. [1s] The convicts begged for water, firewood, and spare sails. Japanese officials, bound by isolationist edicts, ordered cannon fire. Under relentless bombardment, the Cyprus struggled, yet refused to surrender. [0.5s] The daring escape marked the first Australian ship ever to set foot in Japan.
