1518 Strasbourg became a deadly ballroom[0.5s], where hundreds danced nonstop. It started with Frau Troffea, a lone woman swaying on a street. Within days, dozens more joined, twirling through cobblestones without rest. Physicians diagnosed 'overheated blood' and prescribed more dancing as treatment. The council built a stage, hired musicians, forcing the frenzy to continue. Instead of curing, the music amplified hysteria, and more fell dead. They danced till collapse[1s], some dying from sheer exhaustion. Historians argue ergot poisoning versus collective psychogenic illness. A chilling lesson: fear can turn a city into a fatal festival.
