1184, Erfurt’s nobles gathered[0.5s], unaware of a deadly pit below. They met to resolve a bitter land dispute between archbishop and landgrave. The ancient wooden floor, weakened by time, could not bear their weight. Suddenly[1s], the floor gave way, plunging nobles into a foul abyss. They fell into a massive cesspit, filled with stagnant, toxic waste. Sixty to a hundred aristocrats drowned, were crushed, or suffocated by fumes[0.8s]. Miraculously, King Henry and Archbishop Conrad clung to iron rails, surviving. The tragedy didn’t halt Henry’s rise; he became Holy Roman Emperor. History remembers the Erfurt Latrine Disaster as a grim reminder of fragile power.
