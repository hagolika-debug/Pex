Imagine a wave of syrup crushing a city. January 15, 1919, Boston's North End faced disaster.[0.8s] A massive 2.3‐million‐gallon molasses tank burst unexpectedly. A 25‐foot sticky wave surged at 35 mph, flattening everything.[1s] Twenty‐one people died, horses flailed, streets turned brown. Cadets from USS Nantucket plunged into the syrup, rescuing victims.[0.5s] The sweet smell lingered for decades; a plaque now commemorates. New building codes emerged, preventing future tank catastrophes. A sugary tsunami still haunts Boston—history’s strangest disaster.
