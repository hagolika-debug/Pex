In 1832, a tiny kingdom sprang up on the U.S.-Canada border. A confusing treaty left the land untaxed,[0.8s] sparking double‐taxation fury. Hundreds of settlers voted, drafted a constitution, and declared independence. They elected a justice, set up courts, and called themselves Indian Stream. Both American sheriffs and British officials demanded taxes, threatening arrests. Militia gathered.[1s] The border standoff grew dangerously tense. In August 1835, New Hampshire troops forced the republic to surrender. The dispute lingered until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty settled the border. Today, a historic marker in Pittsburg whispers of this forgotten nation. Imagine living in a country that existed for just three years.
