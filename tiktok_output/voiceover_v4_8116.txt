April 27, 1865, the Sultana steamboat exploded on the Mississippi. Overloaded with over 2,000 Union soldiers, far exceeding its 376‐person limit. A patched boiler, ignored warnings, set the stage for disaster. The blast lit the night sky[1s], killing nearly 1,800 souls. The tragedy vanished as headlines screamed Lincoln’s assassination and Booth’s death. Survivors clung to debris; bodies floated downstream for weeks. Captain Speed was convicted, then exonerated, disappearing into legal obscurity. Today, monuments stand, reminding us of the forgotten Sultana victims. History glorifies battles, not this tragedy.[2s]Remember the Sultana.
