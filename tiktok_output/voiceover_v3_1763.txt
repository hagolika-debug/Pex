In 1883, glittering gold sparked a hidden boom on the Amur. Russian and Chinese prospectors built a lawless town, calling it Zheltuga. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived without women, creating a strange society. They forged their own flag—black and yellow, symbolizing land and gold.[1s] A crude court met at Eagle Field[0.5s], meting out brutal punishments. No women were allowed—except a few families—so a red‐light district blossomed across the river. In 1885, Qing forces demanded evacuation, but miners ignored the warning.[0.8s] By January 1886, Chinese soldiers razed Zheltuga, erasing its golden dream. The ghost of the Amur’s California vanished, but its story lingers in forgotten maps.
