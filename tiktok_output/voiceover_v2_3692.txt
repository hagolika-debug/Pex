In 1957, a hidden conflict ignited on Africa's Atlantic coast. Spain's enclave Ifni, a relic of empire, became the flashpoint. Moroccan nationalists, fresh from independence, demanded the return of their land.[0.5s] On November 23, 1957, guerrillas stormed villages near Sidi Ifni. Spain deployed elite Legionnaires, airlifting troops into desert dunes.[1s] The siege lasted months, but the city held thanks to naval supplies. International eyes ignored the clash, dubbing it the Forgotten War. By June 1958, peace arrived, yet scars remained on the coastline. The Treaty of Angra de Cintra finally ceded Ifni to Morocco in 1969. A quiet legacy: a once‐war‐torn town now rests in peaceful silence.[0.8s]
