In 1755, a tiny Moravian village faced a vampire panic. They blamed elderly healer Marianna Saligerin, claiming she rose from the grave. Night after night, villagers heard weight on chests[0.5s] and phantom figures. Empress Maria Theresa ordered physicians to investigate the undead rumors. Doctors unearthed twenty‐nine corpses; many showed no decay. The manor court sentenced them to fire[1s] Nineteen bodies turned to ash. Maria Theresa issued a decree banning clergy from vampire trials. Today, scientists explain the bodies' preservation as cold soil and illness[0.8s]. A forgotten vampire fire still haunts history's shadows.
