On Georgia’s St. Simons Island, a silent tragedy unfolded in 1803. Seventy‐five Igbo captives arrived aboard the slave ship Wanderer, bound for bondage. During the voyage they seized control, drowning their captors in a desperate revolt. The ship grounded at Dunbar Creek, where the rebels marched onto the marsh. Led by their chief, they chose death over slavery, walking into the water. Their final steps echoed across the swamp.[1s] The water swallowed hope. Only thirteen bodies were recovered; the rest vanished beneath the murky tide. Centuries later, descendants honor the site as a sacred ground of resistance. Igbo Landing remains a whispered story of courage,[2s] echoing through forgotten histories.
