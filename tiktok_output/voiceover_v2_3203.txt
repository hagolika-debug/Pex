In July 1518, Strasbourg’s streets filled with frantic dancers. It started with Frau Troffea, who could not stop moving. Within days, over thirty neighbors joined, spiraling into a deadly mania. Authorities believed dancing would cure them, so they hired musicians.[0.8s] The city opened guildhalls, turning streets into endless dance floors. Hundreds danced for weeks, some collapsing from exhaustion.[1s]The tragedy grew. Estimates say up to four hundred people succumbed to the fever. Modern scholars blame ergot‐contaminated rye or mass psychogenic stress.[0.5s] The dancing plague faded as mysteriously as it began, leaving legends.
