In 1900, a peaceful Native village faced a sudden, brutal eviction. Sheriff Fred Ming led deputies, igniting homes with torches at dawn. Nineteen families watched their lives turn to ash, [2s] unheard cries echo. Only St. Mary's Church survived, its walls bearing silent testimony. Land speculator John W. McGinn claimed ownership, citing false tax titles. The Burn‐Out sparked a legal battle that lasted decades.[1s] Justice delayed. [1.5s] Today, descendants remember the fire, seeking recognition and restitution. The event remains hidden in textbooks, a dark chapter of American history. Remember Burt Lake Burn‐Out, and ask: how many more stories lie forgotten?
