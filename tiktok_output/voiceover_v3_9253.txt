In 1900, Konitz, Prussia, a tragedy sparked deadly hysteria. Nineteen‐year‐old student Ernst Winter vanished after dinner, never returning home. His dismembered body parts emerged from the lake, chilling the town. Antisemitic pamphlets blamed Jews, invoking ancient blood‐libel myths.[1s] Riots erupted: torches lit, synagogues attacked, police struggled.[0.5s] Two Jewish men arrested, despite forensic evidence proving innocence.[0.8s] Trials dragged on; libel convictions fell on anti‐Jewish editors. By 1903, Jewish residents fell 28%, community shattered. The Konitz affair reminds us how fear fuels injustice.
