In the early 1800s, a tiny borderland declared independence. Settlers taxed by both US and British Canada grew furious. July 9, 1832: The Republic of Indian Stream was born.[1s] They drafted a constitution with 13 articles, echoing the U.S. Luther Parker became their first representative, negotiating with both nations. [0.5s] Skirmishes erupted, but the republic held its ground.[1s] By 1835, U.S. militia occupied the territory, ending autonomy. The 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally drew the line at Hall Stream. Today, plaques whisper this forgotten micro‐nation’s daring story across borders.
