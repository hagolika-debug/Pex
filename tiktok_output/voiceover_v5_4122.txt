In August 1930, the Amazon jungle heard a thunderous roar. Villagers near the Curuçá River reported fireball streaking across the sky. The bright explosion illuminated the canopy, shaking trees for minutes. Witnesses described a deafening boom[1s] followed by a lingering, sulfuric smell. A lone friar, Fedele d'Alviano, journeyed five days later to investigate. He collected terrified testimonies[0.5s], sending a detailed letter to the Vatican Observatory. Decades passed unnoticed until 1995, when astronomer Mark Bailey uncovered the report. Scientists later examined satellite images, searching for an impact crater[1s] that never existed. The Curuçá event remains a mysterious Amazonian fireball, echoing Tunguska’s secret.
