In 1880, California’s wheat fields turned into a deadly battlefield. The Southern Pacific promised cheap land,[0.8s] then raised prices astronomically. Angry farmers formed the Settlers League to fight the railroad’s greed. May 11th, a community rally turned into a tense showdown. Shots rang out.[1s] Chaos erupted as settlers and marshals exchanged fire. Within seconds, seven men lay dead, the field stained crimson. The railroad’s gunman, Walter Crow, was later ambushed and killed. Frank Norris turned the tragedy into his novel, cementing its myth. Today, a historic marker stands,[1s] reminding us of broken promises.
