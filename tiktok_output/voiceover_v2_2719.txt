April 24, 1865, a Mississippi steamboat overloaded with Union POWs set sail. The vessel, Sultana, was legally limited to 376 passengers, but carried over 2,300. Just five days after Lincoln's assassination, tragedy struck on the river's dark waters. At 2 a.m., a faulty boiler exploded, tearing the hull apart.[1s] The blast sent scorching steam into cabins, killing passengers instantly. Rescue attempts were hampered by the raging current and night darkness.[0.8s] Over 1,800 souls perished, making Sultana the deadliest U.S. maritime disaster. The tragedy was buried by war news, remaining almost forgotten for a century.[1.2s] Today, Marion, Arkansas honors the victims with a museum and annual ceremony. Remember Sultana, a silent reminder of greed, loss, and forgotten heroes.
