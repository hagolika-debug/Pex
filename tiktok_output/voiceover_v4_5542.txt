In 1921, a quiet Tuscan town faced a storm of fascist terror. Italy’s post‐war unrest, known as the Biennio Rosso, fueled violent clashes. Local fascist Squadristi, led by Dino Castellani, organized a punitive expedition. On July 24, two trucks rolled into Roccastrada, weapons glinting in dusk. They torched homes, fired indiscriminately[1s], leaving ten civilians dead. A stray bullet killed a squadrist, sparking a brutal second assault. Carabinieri watched, powerless[0.5s], while fascists celebrated their terror. Authorities later escorted the attackers away, no immediate arrests were made. Decades later[1s], trials finally condemned Castellani, but the town’s scar remains.
