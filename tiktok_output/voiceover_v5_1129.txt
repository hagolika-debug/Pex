In 1915, a quiet island erupted into chaos. British Indian soldiers stationed in Singapore, restless and hungry. Rumors swirled they'd be sent to fight fellow Muslims abroad.[1s] On February fifteen, sepoys seized rifles and unleashed deadly fire. Thirty‐six soldiers and civilians fell as panic swept the streets. Allied ships from Japan, France, and Russia stormed the harbor. Within two days mutineers were captured; forty‐seven faced firing squads.[1s] The crisis birthed Singapore’s Special Branch, tightening colonial surveillance. A forgotten uprising, echoing empire’s tangled loyalties and unrest.[0.8s]
