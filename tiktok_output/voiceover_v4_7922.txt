Imagine a tiny nation hidden between two empires, lasting only three years. A vague 1783 treaty left the border undefined, sparking endless confusion. Settlers paid double taxes to America and Britain, feeling crushed by both. On July 9, 1832,[1s] they declared independence, drafting a constitution for themselves. Three hundred pioneers elected officials, formed courts, and named the Republic of Indian Stream. New Hampshire's sheriff demanded surrender[0.5s]; militia arrived, but no shots fired. A petty hardware-store debt almost ignited war between the United States and Britain.[1.2s] The 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally fixed the border, ending the republic. Today a historic marker stands, whispering the story of this forgotten frontier.
