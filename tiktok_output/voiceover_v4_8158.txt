In 1924, a farmer in Auvergne unearthed strange, ancient-looking artifacts. Amateur archaeologist Antonin Morlet claimed he'd found a prehistoric tomb. Among the finds were clay tablets bearing mysterious alphabetic symbols.[0.5s] French newspapers splashed the story across front pages, igniting nationwide curiosity. Scholars argued: genuine Neolithic relics or modern forgery?[1s]The debate roared. A 1927 international commission arrived, dressed in blue overalls, not suits. Their report declared the site ‘not ancient’ and the hoax exposed. The scandal faded, but Glozel artifacts remain on display, puzzling visitors. Even today, experts debate Glozel’s mystery.[0.8s]History whispers.
