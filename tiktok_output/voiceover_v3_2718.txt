In the 1880s, a golden city rose on a remote river. Dubbed the 'California on the Amur', miners from Russia and China converged.[0.8s] At its height, twelve thousand prospectors lived in the lawless frontier town. They elected a president, raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, and collected taxes. Violence ruled; punishments included lashes with a spiked whip.[1s]Fear reigned. Women were banned; a hidden red‐light district sprang across the river. In 1886, Qing troops stormed the settlement, burning it to ashes.[0.5s] The Zheltuga Republic vanished, but its legend lives in forgotten maps.
