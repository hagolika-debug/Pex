In early 1900s Kentucky, tobacco farmers faced a ruthless monopoly. The American Tobacco Company bought their crop for pennies, crushing livelihoods. Farmers formed the Planters' Protective Association, vowing to withhold tobacco. When boycotts failed, they turned to the Night Riders[1s], masked vigilantes. December 1906, Princeton was seized; warehouses ignited, tobacco vanished in flames. The riders shouted 'stay indoors' as they torched buildings[0.5s], silencing resistance. Authorities responded, deploying the Kentucky State Guard to confront the masked insurgents. By 1909, the militia’s raids broke the Night Riders' power. The monopoly fell after the 1911 Supreme Court decision[1s], ending the war. The Black Patch War remains a forgotten chapter of American labor resistance.
