In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced a courtroom. Pope Stephen VI ordered Formosus' body exhumed[0.5s] for a trial. The corpse sat on a throne, a macabre spectacle. Deacons whispered accusations; the dead couldn't defend himself.[2s] Stephen cut off Formosus' blessing fingers, symbolically silencing him. The corpse was stripped, dressed as a layman, then buried.[1s] The public erupted, demanding justice for the desecrated pope. Later synods nullified the trial, banning future corpse prosecutions.
