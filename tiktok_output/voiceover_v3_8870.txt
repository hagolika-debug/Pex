In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced a courtroom. Pope Stephen VI ordered Formosus’s corpse exhumed for judgment. The body was propped on a throne, silence filled the hall. Clergy shouted accusations, demanding the dead answer his sins.[2s] He was found guilty, his papal vestments stripped away. Three fingers of his right hand were cut off. The corpse was tossed into the Tiber, floating towards oblivion.[1s] Public outrage erupted, leading to Stephen’s own violent downfall. Later, new popes nullified the trial, erasing its memory. A macabre chapter reminding us: power can resurrect even death.
