One spring morning[0.5s], the sky opened—dropping raw meat onto Kentucky fields. March 3, 1876, Bath County's Crouch farm became the strangest weather station. Grandma Rebecca Crouch shouted, ‘It’s snowing!’ as meat clattered on her porch. Pieces ranged from hand‐sized strips to half‐dollar circles, covering two acres. Neighbors, a butcher, and a doctor tasted the odd flesh[1s], none could name it. Some blamed sky‐born algae; others whispered about vulture vomit. The leading theory:[0.8s] a dust devil lifted decaying deer meat, then scattered it. Scientists still debate, but Guinness crowned it the world’s first ‘meat rain.’ Today, the story drifts through odd‐history lists, a reminder of nature’s bizarre sense of humor.
