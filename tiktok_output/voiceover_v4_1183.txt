In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced a courtroom showdown. Pope Stephen VI exhumed Formosus' corpse and placed it on a throne. The lifeless body answered accusations like a silent witness.[1s] No one could respond. Charges included perjury, illegal papal election, and holding multiple bishoprics. Stephen ordered Formosus' fingers cut off, symbolically ending his blessing power. Stripped of papal robes, the body was cast into the Tiber.[0.8s] Darkness swallowed it. Public outrage erupted; Romans stormed the palace, demanding justice for the dead. Stephen VI was imprisoned, then strangled in his cell.[1s] Power turned deadly. The Cadaver Synod was later nullified, banning corpse trials forever.
