A hidden gold city blossomed on the Amur river, unknown to history. In 1883 miners struck gold, creating the 'California of the East' [0.5s]—a wild frontier. Within months, twelve thousand prospectors from across continents converged there. They elected presidents, wrote statutes, and even raised a black‐yellow flag. Violence ruled, but punishments were brutal.[1s]One lash meant death. Chinese coolies, Russian deserters, and European traders shared taverns and theatres. In 1886 Qing forces stormed the settlement, burning it to ashes. Survivors fled, but the legend of Zheltuga still glitters today. Imagine a lawless gold republic disappearing overnight.[0.8s]History’s secret lost.
