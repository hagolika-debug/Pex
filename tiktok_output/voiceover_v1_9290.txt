In 1883, gold glittered along the Amur, birthing a wild city. Russian and Chinese prospectors built Zheltuga, the 'California on the Amur'. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived without laws, only gold.[1s] A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered, symbolizing wealth and danger. Miners voted in councils, crafting a twenty‐clause statute for justice. Women were banned, spawning a hidden red‐light district across the river.[0.8s] In 1885, Qing officials finally discovered the outlawed gold rush. The Chinese army razed Zheltuga in January 1886, erasing its legend. Today, only stories remain of the vanished frontier republic.[1.2s]
