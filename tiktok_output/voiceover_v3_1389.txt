In 1873, a remote Brazilian valley erupted in chaos. German settlers, fleeing persecution, built farms along the Sinos River. A mystic woman, Jacobina Mentz, claimed divine visions. Her followers, called Muckers, clashed with conservative Spotters. In May 1874, Jacobina announced the world’s end[1s] and urged bloodshed. Muckers burned 14 houses, killing ten, including children. The army surrounded Jacobina’s homestead[0.5s]; flames consumed the night. Dozens perished, Jacobina’s mouth slit, her body vanished into myth. Today, the Mucker War remains a haunting footnote in Brazilian history.[1s]
