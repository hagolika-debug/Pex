In 1914, a governor declared martial law on a tiny town. Copperfield, Oregon, barely eighty souls, thriving on saloons and gambling.[0.8s] Residents petitioned Governor Oswald West, demanding an end to Sunday drinking. He sent Fern Hobbs, a 30‐year‐old secretary, to enforce.[1s] Militiamen and Hobbs arrived by train, townsfolk greeted them with bunting. Hobbs handed resignation papers to the council; they refused to sign. Governor’s proclamation sealed the saloons, militia guarded each doorway.[0.5s] Owners sued; courts issued injunction, but West ignored it. By 1915 the ghost town burned, its saloons never reopened.
