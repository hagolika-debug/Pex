In 1925, a quiet Oregon town turned hostile. Toledo's timber mill hired Japanese workers for night shifts. Local businessmen formed a league to keep them out. A mob of fifty men[1s], backed by two hundred voices, arrived. They forced twenty‐seven Japanese, four Filipinos, one Korean onto trucks. The victims were driven[0.5s] fifty miles to Corvallis depot. A year later, a lawsuit sued the mob leaders. The court ruled for the workers[1s], awarding $2,500 damages. This case set a precedent for civil rights protections.
