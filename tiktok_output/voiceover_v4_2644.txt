In the far‐east, a secret gold fever ignited a floating kingdom.[0.5s] 1883, miners struck gold along the Albazikha River, birthing Zheltuga. Russians, Chinese, even a few Europeans rushed to claim the glitter. They built a bustling town with hotels, theaters, even a casino. No women allowed,[1s] so a hidden red‐light district flourished across the river. Law was harsh: lashes for drunkenness, whips for thieves, death for traitors. By 1885, the population peaked at twelve thousand, a multicultural gold rush. Empress Cixi demanded its removal; Qing troops finally razed the town. Zheltuga vanished, leaving only legends of a forgotten frontier republic.[1s]
