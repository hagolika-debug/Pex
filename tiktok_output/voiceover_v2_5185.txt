A forgotten nation sprang up between the US and Canada.[1s] A vague border in the 1783 Treaty left locals confused. On July 9, 1832, settlers proclaimed the Indian Stream Republic.[0.5s] They drafted a constitution, elected officials, and taxed themselves. Both American and British sheriffs demanded taxes, sparking tension. New Hampshire militia marched, but the Republic resisted peacefully.[1.2s] By August 1835, the tiny nation surrendered to New Hampshire. The dispute lingered until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty settled borders. Today, a historic marker reminds us of this lost republic. Imagine a country that existed for just three years—forgotten, but real.
