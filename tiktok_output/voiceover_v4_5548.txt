In 1883, gold glittered on the remote Amur River banks. Prospectors from Russia and China surged, creating a bustling frontier town. [0.5s]They named it Zheltuga, the 'California on the Amur'. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived under a black‐and‐yellow flag. [1s]Law was strict: thieves received five hundred lashes, violators vanished. Women were banned, yet a hidden sex trade thrived in secret. [0.8s]When Qing forces finally arrived, they razed the settlement to ashes. Survivors scattered: some returned to Russia, others mined elsewhere in Amur. Today, Zheltuga lives only in faded photographs and whispered legends.
