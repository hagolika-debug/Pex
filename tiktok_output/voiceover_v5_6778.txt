In 1272, a petty dispute sparked a six‐year war. A peasant stole a cow during a jousting tournament in Ciney. Officials mistakenly[0.5s] executed the thief, enraging his lord and neighbors. Namur, Brabant, and Luxembourg rallied, invading neighboring lands for revenge. In April 1276, 2,400 men clashed[0.8s] at Ciney’s fields. Thousands died; even the count mocked the loss, saying three gentlemen. The city burned, its church engulfed in flames.[1s] Survivors vanished. King Philip III intervened, restoring borders and ending the absurd conflict. Today, the ‘Route de la Vache’ commemorates this bizarre medieval war.
