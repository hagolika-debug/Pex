In 1184, nobles gathered for a royal court... [1s] ...and tragedy struck. King Henry VI presided, unaware the wooden floor was rotting beneath. Hundreds of armored nobles crowded the upper chamber, adding unbearable weight. Suddenly, the beams gave way—[0.8s]—the floor crashed into darkness. They fell into a massive cesspit, [0.5s] the stench of death rising. Some nobles drowned instantly, others were crushed by falling timber. King Henry escaped, perched on a stone alcove that held firm. The disaster claimed about sixty lives, reshaping regional power forever. Legends say the nobles’ screams echo beneath Erfurt’s streets to this day. A grim reminder: even the mighty can fall through the floor of history.
