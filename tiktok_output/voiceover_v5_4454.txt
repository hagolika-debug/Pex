In 1859 a tiny island sparked a near‐war.[0.8s] The Oregon Treaty set the border, but left a watery loophole. San Juan Island sat between Britain and America, both claiming sovereignty. A farmer shot a roaming pig that was ruining his potatoes.[1s] The pig’s owner demanded £100, refusing the farmer’s modest offer. American troops landed, British warships appeared, tension flared dramatically. Admiral Baynes ordered restraint, preventing bloodshed over the pork.[0.5s] Negotiations cooled the crisis; the island stayed American after arbitration. Today tourists visit San Juan, unaware of the dead pig’s legacy.
