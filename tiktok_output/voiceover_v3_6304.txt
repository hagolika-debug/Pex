In 1903, a hidden war erupted in West Virginia's coal hills. Striking miners demanded fair wages, confronting ruthless mine owners and corporations. Deputy Marshal Cunningham was tasked with serving injunctions to the defiant miners.[0.5s] A posse of five hundred armed citizens assembled, but the strike persisted. At dawn, fifty marshals and detectives stormed Stanaford's tiny village.[1s] Gunfire roared; over a thousand bullets shattered the quiet Appalachian morning.[1s] Three miners fell in their beds, while mothers wept in silence. Mother Jones arrived, witnessing blood‐stained shacks and shattered hopes. The battle faded, unmarked, its story buried beneath forested hills. Remember Stanaford, a silent testament to the fight for workers' rights.
