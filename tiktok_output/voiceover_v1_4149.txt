In 1932, Australian farmers faced an unexpected enemy: towering emus. A drought forced thousands to migrate across wheat fields, destroying crops. The government sent soldiers with machine guns, thinking they could control the flock. But the birds scattered, outrunning bullets and even dodging gun jams.[1s] Three days of fire resulted in only thirty emus dead, a humiliating loss. The media mocked the 'Emu War', declaring the birds the true victors. [0.5s]The soldiers withdrew, leaving the fields still overrun by feathered invaders. Farmers later built massive fences, the only defense that finally worked. Today, the Emu War remains a quirky reminder of nature’s stubborn resilience.
