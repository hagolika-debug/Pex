In June 1730, a British slaver set sail from Guinea. Ninety-six captives cramped below deck, shackled and barely breathing. At dawn, five enslaved men slipped free of iron chains.[0.5s] They stormed the deck, seized weapons, and killed three crew members. A desperate bomb threat forced the crew to surrender.[1s] Without navigation skills, they turned the vessel around toward Africa. Days later, the Little George reached Sierra Leone's river mouth. Both captives and crew abandoned ship, later rescued by another slaver. The revolt left a rare record of enslaved resistance on the high seas.[1.2s]
