In 1883, gold glittered along the Amur, birthing a wild city. Rogue miners from Russia and China formed the Zheltuga Republic. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived like a frontier California. They erected a black‐and‐yellow flag,[0.5s] declaring independence on foreign soil. Lawmakers wrote twenty clauses, enforcing brutal punishments for even minor theft. Gold flowed,[1s] yet disease and scurvy claimed many miners’ lives. In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, but miners ignored the warning. A Chinese army arrived in 1886[0.8s], razing the settlement to ashes. Today, Zheltuga remains a forgotten gold rush, echoing lost frontier dreams.
