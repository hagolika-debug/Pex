In September 1902, the Pacific Northwest erupted in flames.[0.5s] It started when boys tried burning a hornet's nest.[1s] Within hours, the blaze raced 30 miles across the gorge. Ash fell on Portland; streetlights glowed at noon in Seattle. At least 38 lives lost, dozens more injured in the inferno. Damages topped $12 million then—equivalent to nearly half a billion today.[1s] The disaster sparked new forest management laws across Washington and Oregon. Now the Yacolt Burn State Forest stands as a reminder. Remember history’s flames, protect our woods before they ignite again.[1.5s]
