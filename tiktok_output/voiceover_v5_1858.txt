Off the California coast, the Farallon Islands teemed with seabird colonies. In the 1850s, entrepreneurs saw gold in gull and murre eggs[0.5s]. The Pacific Egg Company claimed exclusive rights, sparking fierce competition. Independent fishermen and rival firms defied the monopoly, risking violent clashes. June 1863, armed men landed, guns barked[1s], blood stained the rocks. One shooter fell on each side; the island echoed with mournful cries. The Egg Company won, but federal orders soon ended private egging. Today, the Farallons protect wildlife, a silent reminder of the Egg War.
