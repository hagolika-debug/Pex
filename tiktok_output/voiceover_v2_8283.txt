Paris, 1730s:[0.5s] apprentice printers toiled under a master's demanding rule. Their masters cherished cats, feeding them better than the apprentices. Starved apprentices watched cats lounge, sparking bitter jealousy. They plotted a gruesome joke: capture and execute the felines. A mock courtroom formed.[1s]Cats stood accused of witchcraft. Verdict: death by hanging.[0.8s]Apprentices cheered, masters shivered. The massacre echoed, hinting early worker rebellion. Historians view it as a rare glimpse into 18th‐century dissent. Today, the tale inspires books, lectures, and quirky museum exhibits. Imagine fighting oppression with cats—what would you sacrifice?
