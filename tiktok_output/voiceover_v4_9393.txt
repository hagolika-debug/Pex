In 1883, miners struck gold along the Amur’s remote banks. Russians and Chinese rushed in, forming a lawless boomtown. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived like a frontier city. They elected a president, raised taxes, and built a hospital.[1s] Women were banned, spawning a notorious red‐light district across the river. The settlement flew a black‐and‐yellow flag, symbolizing soil and gold. In 1885, Qing forces demanded evacuation, but miners ignored the order.[0.5s] Finally, a Chinese army razed Zheltuga in early 1886, ending the dream. Today, the ghost of 'California on the Amur' whispers through forgotten archives.[1.2s]
