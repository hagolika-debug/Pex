In 1832, a tiny borderland declared itself a nation. The Republic of Indian Stream sat between New Hampshire and British Canada. Conflicting treaty language left the area unclaimed by either side. Locals grew tired of double taxation, so they drafted their own constitution.[0.5s] July 9, 1832, the assembly proclaimed independence amid hopeful cheers. A tiny militia guarded the border, but larger forces soon approached. Negotiations stalled, and tensions rose.[1s]The sheriff demanded surrender. By August 5, 1835, New Hampshire annexed the fledgling republic. The border dispute lingered.[0.5s]Until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty settled it.
