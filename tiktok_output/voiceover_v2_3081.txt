In 1839, two US states almost went to war[0.5s] over honey. A faulty 1816 border line sparked a bitter disagreement. Missouri tried collecting taxes in the contested strip; settlers rebelled. Sheriff Gregory was chased away[1s], then arrested when he returned. Missouri cut down bee‐hive trees, stealing sweet frontier treasure. Both sides mobilized militias, armed with muskets and farm tools. Men faced each other across fields[0.8s], but no shots rang out. In 1849, the Supreme Court upheld the original survey line. Today the border still follows the crooked line, a honey reminder.
