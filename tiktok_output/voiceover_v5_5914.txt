In 1859, a single pig sparked an international standoff. The San Juan Islands sat between America’s Washington Territory and British Vancouver Island.[0.5s] American farmer Lyman Cutlar shot the pig rooting in his potato garden. The pig’s owner demanded $100, the farmer offered just $10. Britain threatened arrest; the U.S. dispatched troops, and Britain sent warships. Both sides amassed cannons, yet no shots were ever fired.[1s] President Buchanan sent Winfield Scott, who negotiated a peaceful resolution. Germany’s Kaiser Wilhelm I arbitrated, awarding the islands to the United States. The Pig War left no casualties, but a quirky reminder of border disputes.[0.8s] All because a pig ate potatoes—history’s most bizarre trigger.
