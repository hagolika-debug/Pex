In 1912, Tasmania's darkest hour ignited deep underground. On October 12, a pump house fire swallowed the North Mount Lyell mine. Lack of alarms meant miners raced through smoke, shouting warnings. Forty‐two souls perished[1s], but brave men stayed behind to save others. The SS Loongana raced across Bass Strait, delivering rescue gear in record time[0.8s]. A miner’s note pinned to timber begged his wife for forgiveness. Rescuers descended, hearing a tin‐clad note pleading for food[0.5s]. After four days, the final survivors emerged, exhausted but alive. Today, the tragedy lives on in songs and memorials.
