In the wild Amur wilderness, miners forged a hidden gold kingdom. 1883, gold glint sparked a rush of Russian and Chinese prospectors. They declared the Zheltuga Republic—an unrecognized, law‐making proto‐state. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived in bustling streets. Women were banned[1s], yet a thriving underground market flourished. They taxed gold, built hospitals, theaters, even a casino dubbed Monte Carlo. Qing officials finally discovered the colony and demanded evacuation. Chinese soldiers razed Zheltuga, [0.8s] burned its gold dream. The ghost of this 'California on the Amur' still haunts history.
