A British trader's fatal stroll sparked war in feudal Japan.[1s] 1862, the Tōkaidō road buzzed with samurai and merchants. Charles Richardson and friends ignored warnings, riding toward Kawasaki temple. A massive Satsuma retinue thundered past, demanding reverence. Richardson rode between the lord's litter and guards, inciting fury. Swords slashed; Richardson fell, mortally wounded by samurai. The death echoed across Yokohama, demanding retribution.[0.8s] Britain demanded compensation; Japan faced a looming naval showdown. In 1863, British ships bombarded Kagoshima, reshaping foreign policy.[0.5s]
