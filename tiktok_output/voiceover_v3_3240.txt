In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced a courtroom. Pope Stephen VI ordered Formosus' corpse exhumed from a tomb. The body was propped on a throne,[0.8s] silent witnesses watching. A deacon answered for the dead, reading charges of betrayal. Formosus was accused of perjury, illegal papacy, and multiple sees. The dead pope was found guilty,[1s] his fingers cut off. His body stripped, dressed as a layman, then thrown into the Tiber. Romans rioted, deposed Stephen, who later died in prison.[0.5s] The Cadaver Synod remains history's strangest trial, warning against political vengeance.
