October 21, 1904—Russia’s Baltic Fleet sails toward the Pacific. Fearful of Japanese torpedo boats, they scanned the dark North Sea. A lone trawler's lights looked like enemy vessels. Orders shouted, guns primed, tension crackled across decks.[1s] Searchlights blinded crews as Russian cannons ripped through humble fishing boats. Two fishermen died, the sea turned red.[0.8s] Russian ships mistakenly fired on each other, mistaking allies for foes. Britain demanded answers;[1s] the world watched, fearing a war between empires. Russia paid £66,000, and a memorial still stands in Hull.
