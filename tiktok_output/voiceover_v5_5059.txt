In the early 1830s, a tiny nation emerged on the US‐Canada border. The Republic of Indian Stream claimed independence to escape double taxation. Boundaries were murky.[1s] The 1783 Treaty of Paris left a gray line. Three neighboring states argued over the headwaters of the Connecticut River. Citizens drafted a constitution, electing Luther Parker as justice. Militia from New Hampshire marched in, demanding surrender. A showdown loomed.[0.5s] Yet the tiny republic chose peace. By August 1835, Indian Stream was annexed back to New Hampshire. The border dispute finally settled by the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty.[1s] Today, the story lives on as a forgotten slice of history.
