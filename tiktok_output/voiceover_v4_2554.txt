In 1962, a tiny school by Lake Victoria sparked chaos.[0.5s] Three girls burst into uncontrollable laughter, bewildering teachers. The giggles spread like wildfire, infecting 95 of 159 pupils. Within weeks, neighboring villages heard echoing chortles across the fields. Students couldn't study; teachers shut the school down.[1s] The laughter lingered. The epidemic reached 14 schools, affecting nearly a thousand people. Scientists called it mass hysteria, linking stress after independence. [2s] By late 1963, the laughter faded, leaving whispered warnings. Today, the Tanganyika laughter epidemic reminds us how emotions can spread.
