In November 1912, a teen’s confession ignited Portland’s darkest scandal.[1s] Police uncovered a hidden gay subculture thriving in city parks and YMCAs. Roughly seventy men faced charges ranging from indecent acts to sodomy. Three were convicted, but the Oregon Supreme Court reversed them all.[0.8s] The scandal sparked a moral panic, fueling eugenics legislation the following year. Legislators broadened sodomy laws, tripling maximum sentences to fifteen years.[1.2s] A controversial sterilization bill passed, only to be repealed by voters.[0.5s] Prominent figures like attorney Edward McAllister were dragged into the spotlight. Decades later, the scandal’s legacy reminds us how fear can rewrite history.
