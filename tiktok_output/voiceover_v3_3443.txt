In April 1885, Panama became the stage for an unlikely showdown.[1s] The 1846 Mallarino‐Bidlack Treaty forced the U.S. to protect transit. Colombia's civil war drained troops, leaving Panama vulnerable to rebellion. America dispatched its North Atlantic Squadron, forming its first Marine brigade.[0.8s] USS Shenandoah rolled into Panama City on April 7, signaling power. Chile countered, sending the armored cruiser Esmeralda to block U.S. ambitions. The two navies faced off, each warning of annexation threats.[1s] By month’s end, rebels fell and foreign forces withdrew peacefully. The crisis spurred U.S. Marine reforms, shaping future expeditionary doctrine.
