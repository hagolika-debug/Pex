In 1919, Boston's North End faced an unimaginable sweet disaster. A massive steel tank held 2.5 million gallons of molasses[0.5s]. At noon, the tank burst[0.8s], unleashing a viscous tidal wave. Molasses surged fifteen feet high, drowning streets, horses, and people. The sticky deluge trapped 21 victims[1s], while the scent lingered forever. Rescue crews battled a syrupy battlefield, their boots stuck in goo. Legal battles followed, forcing the company to pay a million dollars. Today, the tragedy reminds engineers to respect structural safety. Boston still smells faintly sweet on hot summer evenings.
