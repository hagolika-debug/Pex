In 1859, a single pig sparked an international standoff. American farmer Lyman Cutlar shot the pig rooting in his garden. The pig belonged to a Hudson's Bay Company rancher, igniting anger.[0.5s] British officials demanded $100 compensation, the Americans balked. Both nations sent troops to San Juan Island, guns aimed.[1s] General Harney dispatched 66 soldiers; Britain countered with five warships. Negotiations stalled, but no shots were fired—just a lot of bluster.[0.8s] The standoff lasted months, ending with joint occupation. Finally, arbitration awarded the islands to the U.S., all because of a pig.
