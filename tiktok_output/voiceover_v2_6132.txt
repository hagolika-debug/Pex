In 1919, Boston faced a disaster no one imagined. A massive steel tank, holding 2.3 million gallons, burst. Molasses surged through the North End, a 15‐foot sticky wave. Residents were trapped, suffocated, and swept away by the sweet torrent. The city was silent.[2s]The streets smelled of sugar. Twenty‐one people died, dozens more suffered injuries. Investigators blamed shoddy steel— the same used on the Titanic. Justice was delayed.[1s]Court awarded $630,000 to victims. The disaster reshaped building codes, preventing future sweet catastrophes.
