In 1741, the Dutch East India Company coveted India's pepper. King Marthanda Varma refused to bow, sparking a deadly clash. The Dutch blockaded the coast, aiming to starve Travancore.[0.5s] Fishermen sabotaged Dutch ships, using hidden reefs and night raids. A fake cannon line fooled the enemy.[1s]The Dutch hesitated. A single cannonball hit a powder barrel, causing massive explosion. The stockade shattered.[0.8s]Dutch troops surrendered within days. Captured commander De Lannoy became Travancore's chief admiral, modernizing the army. This victory forced the Dutch out of South India permanently. A forgotten triumph that reshaped regional power—and still echoes today.
