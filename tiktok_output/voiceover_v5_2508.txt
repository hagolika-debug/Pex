In 1904, the Russian fleet mistook peaceful trawlers for enemy torpedo boats. Under Admiral Rozhestvensky, they sailed the North Sea, nerves on high alert. Fog rolled in; searchlights pierced the darkness, illuminating tiny fishing vessels. The crew shouted torpedo boat sightings.[1s] Panic spread across decks. Cannons roared[0.5s], shells slammed into the trawlers, killing two fishermen instantly. Meanwhile Russian ships fired on each other, mistaking allies for Japanese warships. The chaos lasted ten minutes before a blue signal ordered ceasefire. Diplomacy erupted[0.8s]; Britain demanded answers, fearing a war with Russia. Russia paid £66,000 compensation, and the incident faded into a forgotten tale.
