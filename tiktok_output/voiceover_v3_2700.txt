In 1832, a tiny New England valley declared itself a nation. Caught between the United States and British Canada, borders were a nightmare. The Treaty of Paris left the river’s source ambiguous.[1s] Sparking endless disputes. Local settlers convened June 11, 1832, drafting a constitution for freedom. Fifty‐six of fifty‐nine voted,[0.5s] birthing the Republic of Indian Stream. Its charter granted religious freedom, taxes, and a militia of a few hundred. New Hampshire’s sheriff tried collecting debts[1s] the republic resisted fiercely. In August 1835, militia threatened[0.8s] Indian Stream chose annexation. The land finally settled by the Webster‐Ashburton Treaty of 1842. Today, the town of Pittsburg, New Hampshire, whispers its forgotten sovereignty.
