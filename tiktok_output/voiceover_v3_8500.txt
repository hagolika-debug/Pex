In the wilds of the Amur River, a gold rush sparked a hidden republic. By 1884, twelve thousand prospectors from Russia and China built a lawless city. They called it 'California on the Amur'.[1s]Gold glittered, chaos roared. A black‐and‐yellow flag waved over bustling hotels, theaters, and a Monte Carlo casino. Women were forbidden, yet a thriving sex trade blossomed across the river. Miners settled disputes with whips and lashes.[0.8s]Law was brutal. In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, giving settlers just eight days. When the deadline passed, Chinese troops returned, torching the ghost town. The Zheltuga Republic vanished, but its legend lives as a forgotten[0.5s] gold fever.
