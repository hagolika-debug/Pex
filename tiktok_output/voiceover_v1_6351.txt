In the 1830s, two American regions almost went to war over a strip of land. A misdrawn map placed Lake Michigan farther north, sparking a boundary clash. The 'Toledo Strip'—468 square miles—became the prize both claimed. Twenty‐four‐year‐old Stevens T. Mason, the 'Boy Governor,' rallied militia. Ohio sent 600 militiamen; Michigan mustered a thousand, tension rose. A survey party was ambushed; shots rang out over heads.[1s]Only a penknife wound survived. President Jackson sided with Ohio, threatening Michigan statehood. Congress traded Michigan's claim for the Upper Peninsula, a 'barren wasteland' then.[0.8s]But fortunes changed. Michigan finally entered Union in 1837, richer thanks to copper and iron. A bloodless war that gave Michigan its mineral wealth—history's hidden bargain.
