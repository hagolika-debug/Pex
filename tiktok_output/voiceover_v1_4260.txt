In 1919, Boston's North End faced a sticky disaster. A 2.3‐million‐gallon molasses tank exploded without warning. The sugary wave surged at 35 miles per hour, crushing everything.[1s] Twenty‐one lives were lost, dozens more trapped in the goo. Rescuers waded through knee‐deep syrup, pulling victims to safety.[0.5s] The city sued the company, forcing new construction safety laws. Even a century later, the scent of molasses lingers on hot days.[2s] A hidden concrete slab still marks the tank’s buried foundation. Imagine a tsunami of treacle—Boston’s sweet, tragic reminder.
