In the 1630s, Europe raced to claim the New World. Swedish trader Peter Minuit, dismissed by the Dutch, sought revenge. He led two ships to the Delaware River[0.5s], landing secretly in 1638. The settlers founded Fort Christina, the first permanent European outpost in the region. For 17 years they lived peacefully[1s], trading furs and teaching log cabin building. But in 1655, Dutch Governor Peter Stuyvesant arrived with seven warships. The Swedes surrendered without a shot, ending America’s only covert colony. [2s]Today, hidden plaques mark the forgotten Swedish footprints across Delaware. The log cabins they built still stand, echoing a vanished Swedish legacy.
