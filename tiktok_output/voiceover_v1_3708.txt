In the early 1830s, a tiny nation sprang up on a contested border. A vague line from the 1783 Treaty of Paris left the land unclaimed.[0.5s] Both the United States and British Canada sent tax collectors, double‐taxing locals. On July 9, 1832, they drafted a constitution and declared independence. Luther Parker served as justice, while a modest assembly governed the 300 residents. But neighboring New Hampshire wasn't pleased with their autonomy.[1s] The sheriff summoned militia; troops gathered, ready to invade the fledgling republic. By August 5, 1835, Indian Stream capitulated and was annexed by New Hampshire.[2s] Today, the town of Pittsburg remembers this forgotten micro‐nation of freedom.
