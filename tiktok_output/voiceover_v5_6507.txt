In the 1630s, Sweden secretly eyed the New World. Peter Minuit, ex‐Dutch governor, sold the plan to King Gustavus. They sailed aboard the Kalmar Nyckel, landing in Delaware.[1s] 1638. Fort Christina rose on present‐day Wilmington, the first permanent settlement. Swedish settlers introduced the iconic log cabin, shaping frontier architecture. Peaceful trade with the Lenape flourished, unlike neighboring colonies.[0.8s] Governor Johan Printz expanded forts, but supplies dwindled, population stalled. In 1655, Dutch warships forced surrender, ending New Sweden. Yet its legacy lives on—in American cabins, place names, and culture.[1.2s]
