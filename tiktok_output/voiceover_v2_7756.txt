Deep in the Amur River, a secret gold city rose. In 1883, miners struck glittering veins, sparking a wild frenzy. They named it Zheltuga, the ‘California on the Amur.’[1s] Rough lawmen drafted a twenty‐clause code, lashing violators mercilessly. Russians, Chinese, Jews, even Koreans mingled in bustling streets. Gold flowed, casinos glittered, and a makeshift theater performed nightly. But the Qing Empire demanded evacuation, sending troops in winter. [2s]The Chinese stormed Zheltuga, burning it to ash. Survivors scattered, yet the legend of a borderless republic lives.
