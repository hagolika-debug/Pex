In 1814, London’s streets turned into a frothy nightmare. At Meux’s Horse Shoe Brewery, a massive vat began to groan. A cracked iron hoop gave way, unnoticed by the busy workers.[1s] Pressure built, and at five thirty, an explosion shattered the night. Over three hundred thousand gallons of porter surged onto cobblestones, flooding homes. Neighbors scrambled for barrels, some drinking the disaster’s free bounty.[0.5s] Eight women and children drowned, their lives lost in the sudsy tide. The city declared it an act of God, sparing the brewery from blame. Today, a small ale commemorates the tragedy, reminding us of history’s strange spills.[2s]
