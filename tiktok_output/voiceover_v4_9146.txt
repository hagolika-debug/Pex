In 897, Rome staged the strangest trial ever recorded. Pope Stephen VI ordered Pope Formosus' corpse exhumed from his tomb. The dead pontiff was propped onto a throne, silent witness. Clergy accused him of perjury, illegal papal accession, and dual bishoprics. Stephen stripped the corpse, then sliced off its blessing fingers. The body, re‐clothed as a layman, was tossed into the Tiber.[1s] Crowds erupted, decrying the macabre spectacle, and Stephen faced revolt. Months later, new popes nullified the trial, restoring Formosus' honor.[0.8s] History still shudders at the day a dead pope was judged.
