Boston's North End, January 1919, a massive steel tank loomed. Inside, 2.3 million gallons of sweet molasses waited for fermentation. A sudden temperature rise heated the viscous liquid, pressure built. The old tank cracked, steel groaned, then burst like a thunderclap.[0.5s] A 15‐foot wave of sticky syrup surged through the streets. People were swept away, horses drowned, cars toppled like toys. Rescue teams waded through the thick goo, pulling survivors out. The disaster claimed 21 lives, sparked nationwide safety reforms.[1s] Even a century later, Boston still smells sweet on hot days.[2s]
