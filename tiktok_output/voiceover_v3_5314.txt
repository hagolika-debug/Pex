July 24, 1915: The Eastland capsized, killing over 800 lives. A festive excursion for Western Electric workers turned into a nightmare. The overloaded ship listed, then rolled like a turtle on water.[1s] Lifeboats were packed on deck, making the vessel dangerously top‐heavy. Within minutes, passengers were hurled into the cold Chicago River.[0.5s] Rescue boats scrambled, but the hull lay twisted, trapping many. Over 10,000 onlookers gathered, turning tragedy into a macabre spectacle. The disaster sank deeper into memory than Titanic or Lusitania.[2s] Today, a memorial stands, reminding us of lives lost too soon.
