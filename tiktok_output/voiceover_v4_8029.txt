In July 1184, nobles gathered for a royal court. King Henry VI presided, hoping to settle a fierce territorial dispute. They met above ancient cesspits[0.5s], wooden floor hiding rot and moisture. Armored knights and heavy robes added unbearable weight to the fragile planks. Suddenly, the timbers snapped and the floor gave way.[1s] Screams echoed. Nobles plummeted into foul sewage, drowning amidst rotting waste. Chronicles report dozens dead, including counts and bishops, shocking the empire. King Henry survived, perched on a stone alcove that never crumbled. The Erfurt latrine disaster reminds us: even power can collapse beneath filth.
