In 1638, Sweden secretly planted a colony on the Delaware River. Peter Minuit, ex‐Dutch governor, guided the Swedes to hidden waters. They named it New Sweden, or Nya Sverige, a tiny frontier. [1s]The settlers built Fort Christina, the first permanent European structure in Delaware. Swedish log cabins introduced a design that would become America’s iconic pioneer home. [0.5s]In 1655, the Dutch warship fleet arrived, ending Sweden’s short reign. The colony surrendered without a shot, becoming a 'Swedish Nation' under Dutch rule. [0.8s]Yet its legacy lives on—log cabins, Lutheran churches, and place names. Next time you see a cabin, remember the hidden Swedes who started it.
