At the turn of the 20th century, Milwaukee's ice trade boiled over. Two rival companies fought for dominance on the frozen Milwaukee River. Wisconsin Lakes Ice owned 300 workers, 500 horses, and endless ice. North Lakes opened nearby, threatening the monopoly with fresh lake ice. The conflict erupted when North Lakes needed a narrow strip of land. Enter the Julius Goll, a steam‐powered ice‐breaker with a brass band.[1s] Armed icemen clashed with barbed‐wire defenses, hurling spiked timbers. On January 20, 1901, the boat smashed through, crowds cheering wildly. The next night, the Goll struck a thick ice shelf, sinking the war.[0.8s]
