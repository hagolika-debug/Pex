In 1898, New Zealand erupted over a humble dog tax. Hokianga County demanded two shillings per dog, sparking outrage.[0.2s] Māori leader Hone Toia declared, 'If dogs are taxed, men follow.' Armed men gathered, stripped for war, awaiting the police.[1s] Police arrived with a cannon, but fled without firing a shot. Parliamentarian Hone Heke Ngapua negotiated peace.[0.5s] Violence ended quickly. Toia and followers were arrested, sentenced to hard labour. The Dog Tax War remains a quirky footnote in New Zealand history. Imagine a war sparked by a tax on your pet.
