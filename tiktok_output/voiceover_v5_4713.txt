In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced a courtroom. Pope Stephen VI exhumed Formosus, propping his corpse on a throne. The dead pontiff was accused of perjury and illegal papacy. A deacon answered for the corpse, repeating the accusations aloud. Judges stripped his papal vestments, cutting off the blessing fingers. The verdict: guilty. All of Formosus' acts declared null. [2s]His corpse was thrown into the Tiber, floating like a ghost. [1s]Public outrage erupted, leading to Stephen's brutal downfall. Later synods nullified the trial, banning posthumous prosecutions forever. The Cadaver Synod remains history's most macabre courtroom drama.
