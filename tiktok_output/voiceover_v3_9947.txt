In 1855 a quiet river turned into a flashpoint of war. The American schooner Water Witch drifted the Paraná[0.8s], mapping uncharted waters. Paraguayan troops, wary of foreign eyes, warned the crew to retreat. Captain Page ignored the warning, pushing deeper into disputed currents. Suddenly, gunfire ripped the calm[1s]; a sailor fell, blood staining the river. Paraguayan forces suffered too, as return fire claimed several soldiers. The incident sparked a diplomatic storm[1.2s], demanding apologies and reparations. In 1859 the US sent a naval squadron, forcing Paraguay to concede. Paraguay paid $10,000, but the river's memory lingered forever.
