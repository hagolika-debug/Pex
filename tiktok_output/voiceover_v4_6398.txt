In 1920, Europe hid a secret war of borders. Polish General Lucjan Żeligowski plotted a daring, staged mutiny. He pretended to betray his army.[1s]The world held its breath. On October 8, his troops stormed Vilnius without a shot. Four days later, he declared the Republic of Central Lithuania. A puppet state born overnight.[0.8s]International diplomats scrambled. Elections followed, yet true power stayed with the mutineer. By 1922, Poland quietly absorbed the fleeting republic. Today, Żeligowski's mutiny remains a forgotten flash[0.5s] in Eastern Europe.
