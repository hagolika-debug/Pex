In 1441, a tiny strip of Italy became a sovereign state. A cartographic mistake left a village unclaimed by Florence or the Papacy. On February 25, locals declared freedom, embracing tax‐free life. No army, no prisons, just endless barter and smuggled salt.[1s] They turned into Italy’s first tobacco growers, defying papal bans. When the Pope threatened excommunication, Cospaia laughed.[0.8s] For centuries, merchants crossed the border freely, never paying duties. In 1826, the Papal State and Tuscany split the land, ending freedom. A tiny hamlet remains, still celebrating a lost republic.[1.2s]
