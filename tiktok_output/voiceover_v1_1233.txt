January 897, Rome: a dead pope is dragged from his tomb. Pope Stephen VI orders the corpse to sit on a throne. A deacon answers for Formosus, pleading with a silent, rotting witness.[0.8s] He is accused of perjury, illegal papacy, and holding multiple sees. The corpse is stripped, three blessing fingers cut off, then shamed. Stephen declares Formosus guilty, annulling all his ordinations. The dead pope's body is dumped into the Tiber River.[1s] Public outrage erupts; Stephen is deposed, imprisoned, and later strangled. Later that year, another synod restores Formosus, banning corpse trials forever.[0.5s] History remembers this macabre courtroom as the strangest trial ever held.
