At the turn of the 20th century, Milwaukee's ice was priceless. Two rival companies fought over a narrow strip of frozen land. The North Lakes crew hired a steam‐powered icebreaker with a marching brass band. The vessel, Julius Goll, smashed ice fields while music echoed across the river. Police patrolled the riverbank, fearing the clash would turn deadly.[1s] Tension rose. On January 20th, crews hurled barbed wire and spiked timbers onto the ice. The icebreaker broke through, but a massive ice shelf tore its hull.[0.8s] It sank. After six weeks, the war ended; both companies lost profit and pride. Milwaukee's residents watched the frosty showdown, cheering the bizarre spectacle. Today, the Ice War remains a chilly footnote in American industrial history.
