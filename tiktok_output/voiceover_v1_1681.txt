In the 1880s, miners forged a wild gold kingdom on the Amur. Dubbed the 'California on the Amur', it attracted thousands of prospectors. Russians and Chinese lived side[1s] by side, speaking a mixed pidgin tongue. No women were allowed; a hidden red‐light district thrived across the river. They built hotels, theaters, even a casino called 'Monte Carlo of the Far East'. Taxes funded a hospital[0.5s], a bathhouse, and a modest public school. In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, but miners ignored the warning. Chinese troops returned in early 1886[2s], razed the town, and forced exile. The ghost of Zheltuga fades, yet its gold rush echoes today.
