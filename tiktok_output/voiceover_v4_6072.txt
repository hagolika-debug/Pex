In the early 1830s, a tiny nation sprang up unnoticed. Nestled between New Hampshire and British Canada, residents faced double taxation. Frustrated, they declared independence on July 9, 1832, forming the Indian Stream Republic. Luther Parker drafted a constitution, granting citizens self‐rule and justice. The fledgling state held elections, [0.5s] appointed sheriffs, and even issued its own stamps. But neighboring New Hampshire threatened invasion, demanding the territory's surrender. [2s]On August 5, 1835, the Republic quietly dissolved, joining New Hampshire. [1s]The land dispute lingered until the Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally settled the border in 1842. Today, the town of Pittsburg remembers its brief sovereignty with a historic marker.
