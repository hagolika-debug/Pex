In 1441 a simple map error[0.5s] created a country no one claimed. The forgotten strip spanned just two kilometers, nestled between Florence and the Papacy. On February 25, the locals proclaimed themselves free, tax‐free and ungoverned. The republic had no army[1s], no taxes, and became a bustling duty‐free market. In 1574 they started growing tobacco, becoming Italy’s first free‐smoke zone. The Pope threatened excommunication for smoking[0.8s], yet Cospaia’s fields thrived unabated. For 385 years the tiny enclave survived wars, plagues, and empire shifts. In 1826 Pope Leo XII dissolved Cospaia, handing it to Tuscany and the Papal State. Today a small hamlet remembers the republic each June with a lively festival. Imagine living tax‐free for centuries[1s]—just because a surveyor misplaced a river.
