In 1272, a stolen cow sparked a regional war. A peasant stole a bovine during a jousting tournament in Ciney. Authorities executed the thief anyway, igniting fury among his lord.[0.5s] Namur, Brabant, and Luxembourg rallied, invading neighboring lands. On April 17, 1276, 2,400 men clashed under sunrise.[1s] Half the army fell; the city burned, its church consumed. King Philip III of France forced a status quo, ending bloodshed. The War of the Cow left a route marker in modern Belgium. All over a single cow—history repeats when small sparks ignite.[0.8s]
