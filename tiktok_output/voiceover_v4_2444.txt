In 1902, a German warship chased a Haitian rebel cruiser. It all began when rebels boarded the German steamer Markomannia.[0.5s] They seized weapons, angering Germany and sparking an international showdown. Germany dispatched SMS Panther, a sleek cruiser, to Haiti.[1s] The rebel gunboat Crete-à-Pierrot fought fiercely, its deck ablaze. Admiral Killick chose a desperate act—blowing up his own ship.[0.8s] The explosion sank the cruiser, killing Killick and his crew. The world watched as Germany claimed its right to protect citizens. The incident strained US Monroe Doctrine debates, echoing across continents. Today, Haiti commemorates Admiral Killick on a rare stamp, remembering sacrifice.
