In the Appalachian foothills, a bustling iron town once thrived. Cedar Grove sat where Virginia Route 39 meets the Maury River. Between 1820 and 1860, sawmills, forges, and boatyards buzzed. Local forges produced thousands of pounds of bar iron for Richmond. Flat‐bottomed batteaux ferried iron downstream,[1s] crewed by free and enslaved men. The Civil War shattered markets,[0.8s] and new railroads bypassed the river. As ore veins depleted, the forges shut,[0.5s] and families left. By the early 1900s, Cedar Grove vanished from every map. Today, a highway marker tells the ghost town’s forgotten story.
