Deep in Siberia, a gold fever birthed a hidden republic. In 1883 miners from Russia and China swarmed the Amur banks. They called it Zheltuga[0.5s], the 'California on the Amur'. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors built hotels, theaters, and casinos. A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered over streets where languages mixed. Law was harsh: whips for thieves, lashes for drunkenness.[1s] When Qing forces arrived[0.8s], they demanded evacuation within eight days. The settlement burned, leaving only legends of a gold‐filled ghost town.
