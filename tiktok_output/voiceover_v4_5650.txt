In the remote Amur basin, a gold rush birthed a rogue nation. By 1884, 12,000 miners from Russia and China converged. They called it Zheltuga – the 'California on the Amur'.[0.5s] A makeshift flag flew, black and yellow, symbolizing dirt and gold.[0.8s] Law and order emerged: a twenty‐clause code, lashings for dissent. The town boasted hotels, a theater, even a casino dubbed 'Monte Carlo'. But the Qing dynasty could not ignore the illegal gold empire. In January 1886, Chinese troops razed Zheltuga, ending the dream.[1s] Today, only stories remain of this fleeting frontier republic.
