In 1835, two states prepared for war over a narrow strip. The 'Toledo Strip' [0.5s] spanned 468 square miles between Ohio and Michigan. A faulty 18th‐century map misplaced Lake Michigan, sparking the conflict. Both sides raised militias; Ohio sent six hundred men to Toledo. Michigan’s 'Boy Governor' Stevens Mason declared the strip a crime zone. Only one shot rang out, wounding a Michigan militiaman. [1s] The skirmish ended without casualties, but pride burned hotter. [0.5s] President Jackson sided with Ohio, offering Michigan the Upper Peninsula. Michigan accepted, gaining copper and iron riches for generations.
