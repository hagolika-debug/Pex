Imagine thousands of sheep fleeing the night sky, screaming into darkness. November 3, 1888, Oxfordshire farms erupted into a wild, frantic stampede. Sheep broke fences, vaulted stone walls, and charged straight through sleepy villages. The panic swept across 200 square miles, leaving farmers bewildered and terrified.[0.5s] Scientists proposed thunder, foxes, meteors—none explained the simultaneous terror. A witness whispered: darkness swallowed the moon, blanketing fields in silence.[1s] The same eerie panic returned in 1889 and again in 1893. Modern researchers cite herd instinct: one scared sheep triggers a domino effect. The Great Sheep Panic remains an unsolved mystery, echoing through history.[0.8s]
