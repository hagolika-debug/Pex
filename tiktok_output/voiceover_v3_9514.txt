In July 1910, a quiet Texas town erupted in violence.[1s] A dispute over a promissory note between a Black businessman and a white farmer. Rumors spread like wildfire, accusing Black residents of planning an uprising.[0.8s] White mobs gathered, armed with rifles, torches, and drunken fury. They stormed homes, beating, shooting, and dragging people into the night.[1.5s] Over 16 hours, the massacre claimed dozens of lives, some buried in hidden pits. The town silenced the tragedy; newspapers buried the story for generations. It wasn't acknowledged until a century later, when a marker finally rose.[2s] Remembering Slocum reminds us: hidden histories shape our present.[1s]
