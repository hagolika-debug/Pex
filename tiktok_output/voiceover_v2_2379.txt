In 1910, a British warship turned Dubai into a battlefield. HMS Hyacinth arrived, demanding to search a merchant’s house for guns. The locals resisted, leading to a tense standoff at sunrise. Shots rang out.[1s]The town erupted in chaos. British marines fired back, and HMS Hyacinth unleashed its six‐inch cannons. Civilians fled.[0.5s]Smoke choked the narrow souks. When the bombardment ceased,[0.8s] 37 townspeople lay dead, four British wounded. The incident sparked outrage, driving merchants to abandon Dubai's port. Yet it forced the British to rethink Gulf policy, shaping the region forever.
