In 1832, a tiny border dispute birthed a forgotten nation. Nestled between New Hampshire and British Canada, settlers faced double taxation. They proclaimed the Republic of Indian Stream[0.5s], drafting a constitution. Luther Parker became justice, overseeing law in the fledgling state. But neighboring sheriffs demanded taxes, sparking armed standoffs. [1s] In August 1835, militia threatened to occupy the territory. Faced with force, the assembly capitulated, agreeing to annexation. The dispute lingered until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally settled borders. [0.8s] Today, the story lives on in Pittsburg’s historic marker.
