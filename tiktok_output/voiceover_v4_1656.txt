January 15, 1919: Boston’s North End awoke to a sticky nightmare. A massive 2.5‐million‐gallon tank burst[0.5s], releasing a sugary tidal wave. Molasses surged fifteen feet high, roaring through streets at thirty‐five miles per hour. Buildings crumbled, horses drowned, and twenty‐one souls vanished in the syrupy chaos. [2s]Rescue crews struggled, the thickened molasses hardening like cement. The city smelled of molasses for years, a lingering, haunting reminder. [1s]Legal battles followed, holding the Purity Distilling Company accountable. Today, the tragedy inspires museums, songs, and cautionary engineering lessons. A sweet disaster that still sticks in history’s memory.
