Imagine a courtroom where the defendant is already dead. In January 897, Rome’s basilica became a macabre courtroom.[1s] Pope Stephen VI ordered Pope Formosus’ corpse to be unearthed. The rotting body sat on a throne, facing a silent jury. He was charged with perjury, illegal papacy, and simony. The dead pope was found guilty; his fingers were sliced off.[0.8s] Crowds rioted; Stephen was seized, strangled, and thrown into the Tiber.[1.2s] The gruesome trial sparked a ban on posthumous executions.
