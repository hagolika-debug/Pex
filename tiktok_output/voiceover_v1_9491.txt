In 1883, gold glittered on the remote Amur River. Russian and Chinese miners flooded the banks, forming a wild settlement.[0.5s] They named it Zheltuga, the 'California on the Amur'. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived under a black‐and‐yellow flag. Law courts, hospitals, even a casino called Monte Carlo thrived. But the Qing Empire soon learned of the illegal gold rush.[1s] Chinese soldiers arrived, demanding evacuation; miners vanished into the wilderness. By early 1886, Zheltuga was burned, its gold scattered.[2s] Today, the ghost town lives only in faded photographs and legends.
