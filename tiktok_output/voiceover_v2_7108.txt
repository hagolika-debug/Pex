In 1919, Boston’s streets turned into a sticky nightmare. A massive 2.5‐million‐gallon molasses tank loomed over the North End. At 12:30 pm, the steel walls gave way, unleashing chaos.[0.8s] A 30‐foot wave of syrup surged, crushing buildings and cars. People were swept away.[1s]The city froze in sticky silence. Twenty‐one souls perished, many suffocated by the thick, sweet mass.[1.2s] Rescue crews struggled as the syrup hardened, turning the scene into a battlefield. Boston smelled of molasses for years, a lingering reminder of tragedy. A sweet disaster that still sticks in history’s sticky corners.
