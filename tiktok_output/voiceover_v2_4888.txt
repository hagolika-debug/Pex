In July 1918, a German U‐boat surfaced off Cape Cod. It opened fire on a tug and four barges, shocking locals. Shells splintered the beach, leaving craters and terrified onlookers. Coast Guard surfmen raced to rescue sailors amidst exploding shrapnel.[1s] The nearby naval air station scrambled planes, marking the only Atlantic air‐combat of the war. [0.8s]The raid lasted minutes, yet its echo lingered for generations. Orleans residents still tell the story of shells kissing the shore. Today, silent dunes guard the memory of that lone American battle. [1.2s]A plaque now marks Nauset Beach, honoring the forgotten heroes.
