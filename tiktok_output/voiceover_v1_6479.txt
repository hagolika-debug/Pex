1917: Oklahoma farmers defied the draft, sparking an unlikely uprising. Tenant farmers, Seminoles, and Black workers united under the Working Class Union.[1s] They planned to march east, living off green corn along the way. A secret informer betrayed them, and a posse of townsmen gathered.[0.8s] Shots rang out near Little River; the rebellion fizzled within hours. Three rebels died, and over 450 were arrested by federal authorities. Most were released, but many received years in prison, fueling the Red Scare. The incident crippled Oklahoma's Socialist Party, ending a hopeful movement.[1.5s] Today, the Green Corn Rebellion remains a hidden chapter of American resistance.
