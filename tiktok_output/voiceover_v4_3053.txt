April 24, 1865[1s], a steamboat named Sultana set off. It was supposed to carry 376, but held over 2,300 souls. Most were newly freed Union prisoners, desperate for home. Midnight[0.5s], a boiler burst, fire roared, the vessel shattered. Nearly 1,800 perished, making it deadlier than Titanic. The nation, exhausted by war[1s], barely noticed the tragedy. Confederate soldiers saved Union men, showing unexpected compassion. Marion, Arkansas built a museum, keeping memory alive. Today, Sultana remains a forgotten chapter of American history.
