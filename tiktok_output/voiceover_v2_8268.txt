In 1883, miners struck gold along the Amur’s remote banks. Russians and Chinese fled lawless wilderness, building a bustling gold town. At its height, twelve thousand prospectors called it “California on the Amur.” A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered over streets lined with hotels, theatres, and casinos. Law was iron:[0.5s] lashes for drunkenness, whips for stealing gold. Women were banned, yet a hidden red‐light district thrived across the river. In 1885, Qing forces demanded evacuation, giving settlers only eight days. The miners vanished,[1s] and Chinese troops burned the settlement to ash. Today, the ghost of Zheltuga lies beneath the silent Amur, a lost republic.
