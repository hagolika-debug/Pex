Imagine a secret Swedish kingdom[0.8s] hidden in America’s Delaware Valley. In 1638, Peter Minuit led 25 settlers aboard the Kalmar Nyckel. They built Fort Christina, the first permanent European settlement on the Delaware. Swedes introduced America’s iconic log cabin[1s] a symbol of frontier independence. They lived peacefully with the Lenape, trading furs and knowledge. In 1655, Dutch commander Stuyvesant[1s] arrived with seven armed ships. Outnumbered, the Swedes surrendered without a shot, ending New Sweden. Descendants later voted for independence; Old Swedes’ Church still stands. Today, Wilmington’s historic markers[0.5s] and a replica ship keep memory alive.
