On New Year's Day 1915, a picnic train set off for Silverton. Two Afghan‐Australian gunmen emerged from a trench, waving an Ottoman flag. They opened fire, riddling open ore trucks with bullets. Four civilians fell, seven more were wounded before chaos erupted. Police and soldiers rushed in, turning the line into a battlefield.[1s] The gunmen fought on Cable Hill, a jagged quartz outcrop.[0.8s] Both attackers died, their bodies buried in unknown graves. The incident sparked anti‐enemy riots, arrests, and wartime internments. Today, the site is a heritage landmark, rarely taught in schools. A forgotten battle, echoing the shadows of global conflict.[0.5s]
