In the 1880s,[1s] a golden utopia rose on the Amur River. Russian and Chinese miners built a bustling town called Zheltuga. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived there, forming a wild republic. They minted their own flag[0.8s]—black and yellow, symbolizing soil and gold. Law was strict: a single lash meant death, keeping order. But women were banned[1.2s], creating a secretive red‐light district across the river. In 1885, Qing forces finally discovered the gold haven and demanded evacuation. By January 1886, soldiers razed Zheltuga, erasing the fleeting kingdom. Today, only legends and ghost stories linger where gold once glittered.
