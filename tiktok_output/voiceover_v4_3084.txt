London summer 1858 turned the Thames into a toxic cloud. Heatwave dried the water, exposing raw sewage on riverbanks. Parliament's windows fogged with foul air; lawmakers clutched handkerchiefs. Even Queen Victoria aborted a royal boat ride after seconds.[1s] The stench forced Parliament to consider moving its chambers elsewhere. Engineers Joseph Bazalgette proposed a massive underground sewer network. Lawmakers approved £3 million funding, launching the Victorian sewer revolution.[0.8s] Those massive sewers still protect London, saving millions of lives today. All because a summer stink forced a city to change forever.[1s]
