In the 1880s, miners forged a hidden kingdom on the Amur. On Chinese soil, Russian and Chinese prospectors built a bustling frontier town. At its peak, twelve thousand hopefuls swarmed the gold‐rich banks. They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, elected leaders, and wrote a law code.[1s] Saloon music, circus acts, and a casino echoed through wooden streets. Qing officials finally discovered the republic and demanded its evacuation.[0.5s] In January 1886, Chinese troops stormed Zheltuga, burning it to ashes.[2s] The ghost of the Amur gold rush lives on in forgotten maps.
