In 1883, gold fever sparked a hidden city on the Amur. Russian and Chinese miners built a bustling town overnight, naming it Zheltuga. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived like a wild frontier.[1s] They elected leaders, raised taxes, and even built a hospital. A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered, symbolizing gold and law. But the Qing empire soon demanded the illegal settlement's surrender.[0.8s] Russian Cossacks warned them, yet miners refused to abandon their riches. In 1886, Chinese forces burned Zheltuga, erasing its brief glory.[1s] Today, only legends remain of the ‘California on the Amur.’
