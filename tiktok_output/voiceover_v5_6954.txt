In 1900, a small Michigan Indian village faced unseen danger. Sheriff Ming and deputies arrived, orders clutched in their hands. Land speculator John McGinn claimed tax titles, demanding the homes gone. He set fire to each cabin, flames devouring lives instantly.[2s] Only the 1838 St. Mary's Church survived the inferno's wrath. Nineteen families were forced onto the road, their homes reduced to ashes. The Burn-Out sparked legal battles that lasted decades, yet justice remained distant.[1s] Today, the tragedy lives on in whispered stories and forgotten plaques. Remember the Burt Lake Burn-Out, a dark chapter America chose to ignore.
