August 5, 1896: Arkansas' rail boom lured Black workers north. White laborers feared competition[0.5s], tensions simmered in the small town of Mena. On that scorching summer day, a mob assembled at the railway depot. They shouted threats, brandished clubs, and demanded the Black men leave. Shots rang out.[1s] Chaos erupted as bodies fell on the tracks. By evening, three Black workers lay dead, eight wounded, silent witnesses. Local newspapers hushed the story, but the community felt the bruise. The massacre marked a dark chapter in Arkansas' labor and racial history. Remembering the forgotten victims reminds us that justice must never sleep.
