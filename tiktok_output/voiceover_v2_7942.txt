In 897, Rome witnessed a trial unlike any other. Pope Stephen VI exhumed the dead Pope Formosus. The corpse sat on a throne, silent yet judged. He was accused of perjury[1s], illegal papacy. Stephen stripped the dead pope's vestments, cutting his fingers. The corpse was declared guilty[0.5s], nullifying his deeds. They tossed his body into the Tiber, where it floated. Romans rioted[2s], toppling Stephen, who soon met a grisly end. The synod was later annulled, restoring Formosus's legacy. A macabre reminder: power can turn even the dead into weapons.
