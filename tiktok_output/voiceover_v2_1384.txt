In Brazil’s far‐south, German settlers whispered of a prophetic healer. Jacobina Mentz Maurer claimed divine visions[0.5s], drawing crowds to her farmhouse. Authorities branded her a heretic; she called herself the new Messiah. She rallied the 'Muckers', promising protection against the hostile 'Spotters'. Violence erupted: homes torched, families massacred, fear spread across the valley. [1s] The government dispatched troops, crushing the rebellion in a bloody siege. On July 19, 1874, Jacobina’s house burned; she and her child vanished. [0.8s] Survivors reported her mouth slit, bodies left in a common grave. Today, the Mucker revolt remains a haunting reminder of faith turned violent.
