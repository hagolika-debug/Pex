In 1906[0.5s], a bizarre war erupted over pork. Serbia exported most of its pork to Austria‐Hungary, feeding empires. Vienna responded by closing borders to Serbian pork, sparking outrage. Believing in independence, Serbia turned to French and German markets. Austria‐Hungary declared a customs blockade—dubbed the Pig War.[1s] Trade numbers plunged, yet both sides refused to back down. Negotiations in 1908 finally lifted the embargo, ending hostilities. The settlement restored pork flow, but left lingering resentment. Today, few recall a war fought over pork.[0.8s]
