In 1663, Virginia's rivers hid a secret uprising brewing. Nine indentured servants, led by William Bell, plotted freedom against Governor Berkeley. They united Africans, Europeans, and Native Americans—an early multi‐ethnic resistance. The plan: raid plantations, gather weapons, demand land rights. But a servant named John Berkenhead betrayed them, seeking a reward. On September 13, soldiers ambushed the conspirators; four leaders faced the gallows. [1s]The hanging echoed across the river, silencing a nascent revolution. [0.5s]History forgot, yet this rebellion foreshadowed the American quest for liberty.
