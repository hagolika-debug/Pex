In 1883, miners struck gold along the Amur River’s remote banks. They founded Zheltuga, a makeshift republic nicknamed the 'California of the East'.[0.8s] At its peak, twelve thousand Russians, Chinese, Jews, and others lived there. Miners elected leaders, collected taxes, and even built a hospital and theater. Harsh punishments ruled: lashes for theft, whips for drunken fights. The Qing empire finally noticed.[1s]They demanded evacuation within eight days. In January 1886, Chinese soldiers stormed the camp, burning everything. Survivors fled, but the ghost of Zheltuga vanished beneath the Amur's icy waters.
