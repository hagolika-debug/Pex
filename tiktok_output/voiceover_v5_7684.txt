In 1883, gold glittered along the Amur’s remote banks. Prospectors christened it “California on the Amur,” a wild frontier. At its peak, twelve thousand miners from Russia, China, and beyond gathered. They forged a republic: black‐yellow flag, taxes, elected council. Law was brutal—whips and lashes punished theft, drunkenness, and dissent. The town boasted hotels, theaters, even a Monte Carlo‐style casino. Then the Qing empire demanded evacuation.[2s] Chinese troops stormed Zheltuga, setting the gold city ablaze. The flames erased the republic, leaving only whispered legends today.[1s]
