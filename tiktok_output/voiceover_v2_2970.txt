In 1610, a massive Portuguese carrack faced a samurai siege. The ship, loaded with silk and silver, left Macau bound for Nagasaki. Japanese samurai, angry from a Macau brawl, pursued the vessel. On January 3, they surrounded the ship in Nagasaki Bay. Captain André Pessoa ignited the gunpowder magazine, refusing surrender.[1s] Flames roared, the deck exploded, and the great ship vanished.[2s] Hundreds of samurai drowned, while a few Portuguese survived. The loss forced Japan to favor Dutch traders over the Portuguese. Legends of the black ship echo in Japanese folklore to this day.
