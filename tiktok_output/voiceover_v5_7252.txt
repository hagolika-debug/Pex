A tiny Italian republic lived tax‐free for nearly four centuries. In 1441 a mapping mistake[1s] split Florence and the Papal State. Three hundred fifty peasants declared independence on the unnoticed strip. No army, no prisons; elders governed from the Annunziata church. Duty‐free trade flourished, first with salt, then with tobacco. While the Pope banned smoking[0.8s] Cospaia became Italy’s tobacco haven. For 385 years the republic outlasted surrounding empires[1s] despite its size. In 1826 Pope Leo XII forced its surrender and division. Today Cospaia is a quiet hamlet, its flag still remembered.
