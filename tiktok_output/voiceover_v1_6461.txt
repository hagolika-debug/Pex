In 1900, a quiet Michigan village vanished in flames. Nineteen Native families lived peacefully on Burt Lake’s Indian Point. A land speculator claimed taxes owed[1s] and summoned the sheriff. Sheriff Ming and deputies arrived, demanding they vacate immediately. One by one, homes were set ablaze[0.5s], smoke filling the sky. Only the 1838 St. Mary's Church survived the inferno. The burned village left survivors scattered[1s], their future uncertain. Decades later, the tragedy faded from textbooks, barely remembered. Today, the land whispers of a forgotten injustice.
