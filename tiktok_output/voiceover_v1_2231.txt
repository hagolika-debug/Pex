In the wild 1880s, a gold rush sparked an outlaw republic. Prospectors struck glittering gold along the remote Amur tributary. They named it Zheltuga, the ‘California on the Amur.’[1s] Russians, Chinese, Koreans, Jews, and even French mingled in bustling streets. A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered; miners voted leaders and harsh laws. Whipping posts loomed; stealing or drunkenness earned hundreds of painful lashes. At its height, a casino called Monte Carlo drew fortunes and ruin. Empress Dowager Cixi demanded evacuation.[0.8s] Chinese troops arrived, burning the town. By 1886, Zheltuga vanished, leaving only ash and legend. Today, explorers hear whispers of this forgotten frontier in the forest.
