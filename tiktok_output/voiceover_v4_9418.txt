Imagine a wave of beer flooding London streets. On October 17, 1814, Meux's Horse Shoe Brewery exploded. A wooden vat's iron hoops slipped, bursting a massive porter tank. The liquid surged outward.[1s]Towers of foam crashed. A 15‐foot beer wave swept through the St Giles slums. Eight souls perished, including children at a family wake. Spectators paid to watch the ruined brewery.[0.8s]But the silence lingered. The disaster forced breweries to replace wooden vats with concrete. A forgotten tsunami of ale still haunts London's history.[1.5s]
