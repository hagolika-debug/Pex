In 1887, Black sugarcane workers demanded fair wages in Louisiana. The Knights of Labor organized them, a rare interracial union of the era. Plantation owners threatened violence, refusing to pay in cash. On November 23, white militia descended on the town's black neighborhood.[1s] Gunfire erupted; families were dragged from homes and shot. Estimates of deaths range from thirty to over three hundred. The massacre was erased from textbooks, silencing generations.[0.8s] Centuries later, descendants uncover mass graves, demanding recognition. In 2025, a memorial was finally erected in Thibodaux. Remembering this hidden tragedy reminds us labor rights still cost lives.[0.5s]
