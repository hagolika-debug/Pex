In 1842, Rhode Island erupted into a little-known civil war. A royal charter from 1663 still dictated who could vote. Only men owning 40 pounds of property could cast ballots. Reformers drafted the People’s Constitution, demanding universal male suffrage. Two rival governments formed, each with its own elected governor. Thomas Dorr seized cannons,[1s] but misfired, leaving only smoke. The rebel army never met the charter troops;[0.5s] one civilian died. The rebellion forced a new constitution, finally ending property voting. Today, Dorr’s fight reminds us that democracy isn’t guaranteed.[0.8s]
