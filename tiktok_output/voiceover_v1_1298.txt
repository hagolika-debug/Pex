In 1835, a tiny strip sparked a near‐civil war. A mistaken map placed Lake Michigan far north, confusing state borders. The Toledo Strip: 468 square miles of contested land between Ohio and Michigan. Both sides mustered militiamen, ready for battle over a line on paper. Shots rang out at Philips Corners, the war's only bloodshed.[1s] President Jackson sided with Ohio[0.5s], denying Michigan statehood until the dispute ended. Michigan traded the Toledo Strip for the Upper Peninsula, gaining minerals later. The 'Frostbitten Convention' sealed the deal, shaping two states forever. Only a horse died, making this war the most bloodless in US history. A map error sparked a war, proving geography can be deadly.
