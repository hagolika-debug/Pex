In 1830, escaped Australian convicts sailed into forbidden Japanese waters. They hijacked the brig Cyprus after a mutiny in Hobart. Led by William Swallow, they drifted across the Pacific, desperate for supplies. They anchored at Mugi Cove.[1s] Shrouded by samurai suspicion. Samurai artist Makita Hamaguchi sketched the foreign brig, noting its foul stench. Japanese commanders ordered cannon fire.[0.5s] But the ship stubbornly refused to leave. Negotiations turned to sign language: the convicts begged for water and firewood. After days of tension,[1s] the samurai let them depart, their sails catching wind. The Cyprus became the first Australian‐built vessel ever to set foot in Japan. A daring escape turned into a forgotten chapter of East‐West encounter.
