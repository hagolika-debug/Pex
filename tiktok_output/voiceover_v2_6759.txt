December 13, 1867, a massive explosion rattled Victorian London. Irish Republicans aimed to free two imprisoned comrades from Clerkenwell Prison. They packed a wall with gunpowder, expecting a modest blast. The charge misfired,[1s] detonating with ferocious force, shattering nearby homes. Hundreds were injured, the city gasped in shock and horror. The Times called it a crime of unexampled atrocity. Public outcry fueled anti‐Irish sentiment, shaping British politics. [2s]The explosion echoed beyond bricks, whispering lessons of misguided rebellion. [1s]Today, the site is quiet, a reminder of forgotten fury. History remembers the Outrage, urging vigilance against extremist desperation.
