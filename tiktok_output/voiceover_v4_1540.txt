January 15, 1919, Boston awoke to an unimaginable sweet disaster. A 50‐foot steel tank held 2.3 million gallons of molasses. Warm weather expanded the thick syrup, cracking the aging steel shell[0.8s]. At noon, the tank burst[1s], releasing a 35‐mph wave of brown fury. Streets turned into waist‐deep molasses[0.5s], sweeping people, horses, and cars away. Cadets from the USS Nantucket dove in, pulling survivors from the sticky nightmare. Twenty‐one died, dozens injured, and the city smelled syrup for decades. Victims sued the company, sparking America's first massive class‐action lawsuit. Today, a plaque marks the spot, reminding us of Boston's sticky tragedy.
