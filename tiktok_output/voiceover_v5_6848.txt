In 1883, gold glittered on the remote Amur River banks. Prospectors from Russia and China rushed there, birthing a wild settlement.[0.5s] They named it Zheltuga, the 'California on the Amur'. At its height, 12,000 miners lived under a black‐and‐yellow flag. A bustling casino, theater, and two orchestras thrived among the gold pits.[1s] Lawmakers drafted twenty‐clause statutes, punishing thieves with whips and lashes. Women were banned, sparking a shadowy red‐light district across the river. [0.8s]In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, but miners vanished. January 1886: Chinese troops burned Zheltuga, erasing the golden dream.
