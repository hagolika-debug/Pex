A tiny nation existed between the US and Canada—no one remembers it. Born from a confusing 1783 treaty line, locals declared independence. July 9, 1832: Indian Stream Republic forms its own constitution. Three hundred residents elected a council, taxed themselves, and minted hopes. Both British sheriffs and American tax collectors demanded payments—double trouble. Tensions rose.[1s] Militias gathered on both sides. In August 1835, New Hampshire militia marched in, forcing surrender. The Republic dissolved; the land became Pittsburg, New Hampshire. The border dispute finally settled by the 1842 Webster–Ashburton Treaty. A forgotten nation, remembered only in a lone historic marker.[0.8s]
