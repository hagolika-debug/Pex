January 15, 1919, Boston's North End faced an unexpected wave. A 50‐foot steel tank, filled with two million gallons of molasses, loomed. At 12:30 pm, the tank burst, unleashing a sticky tsunami. The syrup surged 15‐feet high, roaring through streets at 35 mph. Buildings shattered, horses toppled, 21 lives vanished in sweet darkness. [2s]Rescue crews fought, but hardened molasses turned into concrete. [1s]The city smelled of syrup for years, a lingering reminder. Lawsuits followed, forcing stricter construction codes across the nation. [0.5s]Today, the tragedy inspires museums and sticky souvenirs. A sugary disaster that still drips into Boston’s history.
