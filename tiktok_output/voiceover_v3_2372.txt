In 1902, a German steamer sparked an international naval showdown. The Markomannia, a Hamburg line vessel, sailed toward Haiti’s coast. Haitian rebels, fearing arms shipments, boarded the ship at Cap‐Haïtien.[0.5s] German captain protested, but the rebels seized contraband anyway. Germany responded fast, dispatching warship SMS Panther to the Caribbean. Panther chased the rebel gunboat Crête‐à‐Pierrot across the harbor. Admiral Killick set his own ship ablaze, choosing death over capture.[1s] The explosion echoed worldwide, a rare clash of colonial powers and rebels.[0.8s] The incident forced the US to reconsider the Monroe Doctrine. Today, Haiti commemorates Admiral Killick, but the story fades from history.
