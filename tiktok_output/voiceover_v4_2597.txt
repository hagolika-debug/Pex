April 27, 1865, a steamboat called Sultana set sail. It was legally allowed to carry only 376 passengers. Corrupt officials bribed the captain, packing over 2,300 soldiers aboard. A hastily repaired boiler burst in the early morning darkness.[1s] The explosion tore the hull, sending flames into the Mississippi. Thousands plunged into icy water, fighting for breath and hope.[0.8s] Rescue boats arrived too late; only 963 survived the disaster. The tragedy killed more lives than the Titanic, yet faded away.[1.2s] Today, we remember Sultana, a silent reminder of greed.
