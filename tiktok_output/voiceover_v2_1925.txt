In 1883, gold fever birthed a wild frontier on the Amur. Russian and Chinese miners built a bustling town, nicknamed 'California on the Amur'. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived under a black‐and‐yellow flag. Lawmakers met in the Eagle Field, drafting a strict twenty‐clause code. Whispers of vice spread; a hidden brothel emerged across the river. The Qing dynasty finally learned of Zheltuga, demanding evacuation.[1s]The miners vanished. In January 1886, Chinese troops razed the settlement, erasing its streets. Survivors scattered, but the legend of a borderless gold republic lives on. A forgotten chapter reminding us how greed can birth fleeting kingdoms.[0.5s]
