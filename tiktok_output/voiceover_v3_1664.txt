In 1741, Travancore's king faced a Dutch siege at Colachel. The Dutch East India Company coveted pepper, demanding monopoly over the coast. King Marthanda Varma rallied fishermen, turning simple boats[0.5s] into guerrilla weapons. They forged fake cannons from palm trunks, deceiving the Dutch fleet. A lucky Travancore cannonball hit a powder barrel, exploding the enemy stockade[1s]. Defeated, the Dutch surrendered, ending their foothold on the Indian coast. Marthanda Varma captured commander De Lannoy, offering him a new purpose. Surprisingly, De Lannoy became Travancore's admiral, modernizing its army. His reforms built forts that later resisted Tipu Sultan's forces.[0.8s] The battle remains hidden, yet it reshaped colonial power in South India.
