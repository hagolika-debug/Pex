In the 1880s, gold fever sparked a hidden empire on the Amur. Russian and Chinese prospectors built Zheltuga, a bustling town of thousands. Lawless streets roared with gambling, whisky, and secret pacts.[1s] No one left untouched. A black‐and‐yellow flag waved, symbolizing gold and the rebels' defiance. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived without women[0.5s], creating a dangerous underground market. The Qing dynasty finally noticed, demanding evacuation and threatening force. Chinese troops arrived in winter, torchlights flickering.[0.8s] The town burned. Survivors fled, scattering across Siberia, while the ashes marked the end of a frontier republic. Today, Zheltuga lives only in forgotten archives, a glittering myth of lawless gold.
