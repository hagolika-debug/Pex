In a remote New England forest, a tiny nation emerged. Border confusion after the 1783 Treaty left settlers stateless. On July 9, 1832, they declared independence as the Indian Stream Republic.[0.8s]It shocked neighbors. They drafted a constitution, elected officials, and even a justice of peace. Both British sheriffs and American tax collectors demanded money, sparking outrage. The tiny state stood firm.[1s]But war clouds gathered. New Hampshire militia marched in, demanding surrender; the republic capitulated. By August 5, 1835, Indian Stream officially became part of New Hampshire. Today, a historic marker reminds us of this lost, borderland nation.
