In 1902[1s], a tiny German steamer sparked a deadly Haitian showdown. Haiti's civil war raged, rebel gunboat Crête-à-Pierrot guarded Cap‐Haïtien. The German liner Markomannia was boarded, suspected of smuggling weapons. German consul protested, but rebels transferred cargo onto their own vessel. Kaiser dispatched warship SMS Panther to hunt the rebel cruiser. Killick ordered his crew to ignite the ship's magazine[0.8s], sacrificing himself. The Panther sank Crête-à-Pierrot, sparking international outcry[0.5s]. U.S. State Department oddly approved Germany's aggressive move. Haiti later honored Killick on a stamp, remembering his bravery. A forgotten flashpoint that reshaped Caribbean power dynamics.
