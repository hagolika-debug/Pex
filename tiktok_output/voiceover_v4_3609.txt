Before Philadelphia's fame, a secret Swedish settlement hid on the Delaware. From 1638 to 1655, New Sweden existed—just 17 fleeting years. Peter Minuit, ex‐Dutch governor, led the Kalmar Nyckel across the Atlantic. They anchored near present‐day Wilmington, quietly claiming a 67‐mile river stretch. Fort Christina rose, named for Sweden’s teenage queen, marking America’s first permanent settlement. The colony introduced log cabins—America’s iconic pioneer homes.[1s]That legacy lives today. In 1655, Peter Stuyvesant’s fleet forced surrender without a single shot. Swedish descendants still celebrate Midsommar, and their flags flutter over historic churches.
