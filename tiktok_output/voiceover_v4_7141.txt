In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced a courtroom. Pope Stephen VI ordered Formosus' corpse exhumed for trial. The body was propped on a throne, silent witness. Clergy shouted accusations, demanding justice from the dead. Stephen cut off the pope's blessing fingers, a gruesome verdict.[1s] The corpse was stripped, dressed as a layman, then thrown.[0.5s] It sank in the Tiber, only to wash ashore later.[1.2s] Romans rioted, deposed Stephen, who met a brutal end. The Cadaver Synod remains history’s strangest posthumous trial.
