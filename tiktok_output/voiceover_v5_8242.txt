April 24, 1932, a thousand walkers stormed Kinder Scout. They marched from three valleys, demanding the right to roam. Wealthy landowners guarded the moor[0.5s] with gamekeepers and dogs. A fierce scuffle erupted near Ashop Head, blood on the heather. Five were arrested[1s], sentenced to months in prison for trespassing. Public outrage surged, turning walkers into national heroes overnight. The protest sparked the 1949 National Parks Act, protecting open lands. Today[0.8s], the right to roam is guaranteed, thanks to that daring day. Every April, ramblers retrace the steps, honoring the rebellious walk.
