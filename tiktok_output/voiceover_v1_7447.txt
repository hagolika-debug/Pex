In March 1906, American forces stormed the volcanic crater of Bud Dajo. Moro villagers sought refuge, protecting families and crops atop the sacred mountain. The U.S. imposed a poll tax, sparking fierce resentment among the Moros. Seven hundred soldiers advanced, unleashing artillery into the crater's rim.[1s] Over 800 men, women, and children were killed in minutes. Newspapers blasted the massacre, dubbing it a barbaric tragedy.[0.8s] For decades, the tragedy faded from American memory. Today, memorials in Jolo honor the victims of Bud Dajo. The massacre reminds us how imperial ambition silences native voices. Remember Bud Dajo, lest history repeat its darkest chapters.
