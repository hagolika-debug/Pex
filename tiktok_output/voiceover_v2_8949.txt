In 1883, a gold rush birthed a hidden empire on the Amur. Russian and Chinese prospectors built a bustling town overnight, called Zheltuga. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived like a wild frontier city. They even raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, proclaiming a self‐governed republic. Law and order? Harsh whips punished thieves, drunkenness, even unauthorized women. Gold flowed, casinos opened, and a theater echoed[1s] with foreign accents. But neighboring Qing officials finally learned of this illegal gold haven. In 1885, Chinese troops demanded evacuation[0.8s]; the miners fled into the wilderness. By January 1886, the settlement was burned, erased from history. Zheltuga remains a forgotten chapter of lawless ambition and fleeting riches.
