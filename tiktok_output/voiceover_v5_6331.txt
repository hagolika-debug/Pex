In 1900, the Ashanti rose against British greed. The Golden Stool wasn't a chair; it held the soul of a nation.[1s] Colonial officer Hodgson demanded the stool, believing power lay in possession. Queen Mother Yaa Asantewaa answered, calling women to defend their heritage. British forces holed up in a stockade, surrounded by Ashanti warriors. Days of gunfire faded.[2s]The forest held its breath. In June, a thousand‐strong relief column finally broke the siege. Yaa Asantewaa captured, exiled to Seychelles, yet the stool remained hidden. The Golden Stool survived, becoming Ghana's symbol of freedom and unity.
