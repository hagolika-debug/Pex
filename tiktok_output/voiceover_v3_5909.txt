In 1910, British warships prowled the Persian Gulf, eyes on Dubai. Dubai thrived as a free‐port, attracting traders and illicit gun runners. British demanded a search; locals hesitated, tension simmered.[1s] When the Royal Navy landed, gunfire erupted from rooftops. Captain Dick ordered HMS Hyacinth to unleash six‐inch shells. Explosions shattered the market, scattering civilians and echoing across the creek.[0.8s] Thirty‐seven townspeople died, while four British sailors fell. The incident forced Dubai’s ruler to surrender rifles and pay reparations. The world forgot this clash, but its legacy shaped Gulf politics.[1.2s] Today, Dubai rises as a global city, built on shadows of that day.
