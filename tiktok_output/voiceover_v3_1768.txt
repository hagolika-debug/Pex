In 1908, Montana's wilderness hid a deadly secret. Two Pend d’Oreille families followed treaty hunting rights, unaware of danger. State game warden Charles Peyton demanded illegal permits, escalating tension. Peyton returned armed, shouting orders; the camp trembled in fear.[1s] He shot leader Atwen and his wife, silencing voices instantly. The wounded son, Peh‐lah‐so‐weh, grabbed a rifle, returning fire.[0.8s] Peyton fell, but his deputy shot the brave boy dead. Clarice, pregnant, seized a rifle, ending Peyton’s reign of terror. The massacre sparked outrage, yet no one was ever prosecuted. Today, the Swan Valley whispers a forgotten tale of resistance.
