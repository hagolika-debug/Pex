On December 24, 1910,[1s] a British warship opened fire. The HMS Hyacinth arrived to crush a thriving Gulf gun‐running network. British officers demanded to search a Dubai merchant’s house for hidden arms. The ruler stalled,[0.5s] buying time as locals gathered, tension simmered. At sunrise, 100 marines stormed the house, gunfire erupted instantly. Citizens fired from rooftops, turning the quiet market into a battlefield. The ship unleashed six‐inch shells, demolishing homes and killing dozens.[2s] Thirty‐seven townspeople died; the British suffered four dead, five wounded. The incident soured Gulf‐British relations, forcing Britain to rethink Gulf policy.
