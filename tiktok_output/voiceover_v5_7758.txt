January 1919, Boston’s North End faced an impossible wave. A 2.3‐million‐gallon molasses tank burst, unleashing sticky devastation. A 15‐foot wave of brown syrup roared through streets. People were trapped, suffocated, and dragged by the viscous tide. Twenty‐one souls perished, dozens more suffered broken bones. Rescue crews fought the hardened syrup,[2s] tools biting steel. Lawyers blamed anarchists, but the judge saw shoddy construction. [1s]The disaster reshaped building codes across America. Today, a small plaque reminds us of the sweet tragedy.
