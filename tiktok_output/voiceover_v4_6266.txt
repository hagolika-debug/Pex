In 1862, a British trader met a deadly Japanese procession. He ignored the warning to dismount[0.5s], riding straight through the samurai's path. The lord's guards drew swords, believing he insulted their honor. Richardson fell, cut ten times, his blood staining the road.[1s] The murder ignited a diplomatic firestorm between Britain and Satsuma. Britain demanded reparations, the shogunate hesitated, tensions rose. [0.8s]When talks failed, the Royal Navy bombarded Kagoshima, sparks of war. The clash cost lives, but forced Japan to modernize its defenses. A single traveler’s death reshaped East‐West relations forever.
