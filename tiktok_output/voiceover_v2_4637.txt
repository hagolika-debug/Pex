In 1904, Russian warships mistook British trawlers for enemy vessels. Fog rolled over the North Sea[0.5s], tension crackled aboard the fleet. Suddenly, searchlights blinded innocent fishermen[1s], sparking chaos. Gunfire erupted, sinking the trawler Crane, killing its captain. Russian ships turned on each other, fearing Japanese torpedo boats. Two British fishermen died, six wounded; a tragedy unseen[1s]. Britain prepared its navy; war with Russia loomed. International commission intervened, Russia paid £66,000 compensation[0.8s]. The Dogger Bank Incident almost sparked a world war[1s].
