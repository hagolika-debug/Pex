In 1184, a royal council met beneath Erfurt’s ancient cathedral. Nobles from across the Holy Roman Empire gathered, fearing a looming feud. King Henry VI[0.5s] summoned them to resolve a bitter land dispute. The wooden floor, centuries old, creaked under the weight of dozens. Then—[1s]—the floor gave way, plunging everyone into darkness. They fell into a foul cesspit, the church’s hidden latrine. Nobles drowned, were crushed, or suffocated by the stench. Miraculously, King Henry clung to iron rails, surviving the nightmare.[2s] His escape paved the way for becoming Holy Roman Emperor later. A royal council turned into a toilet tragedy—history’s most foul meeting.
