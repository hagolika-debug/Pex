In 1904, a Russian fleet fled toward the Far East. They passed the North Sea, paranoid of Japanese torpedo boats. Night fell, and the fleet mistook British trawlers for enemy vessels. Searchlights blazed, guns roared—harmless fishermen became targets. The trawler Crane sank, its captain dead.[1s] Panic erupted. Russian ships even fired on each other, fearing Japanese attacks. [0.5s]Only minutes later, they realized their grave mistake. Diplomacy teetered; Britain prepared its fleet for war. Russia paid £66,000, and a memorial stands in Hull.[0.8s]
