In the wild Far East, gold sparked a secret republic. Between 1883 and 1886, miners built a bustling town on Chinese soil. They named it Zheltuga, the ‘Yellow River’ gold haven. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors from Russia, China, Europe gathered. Lawless parties roared, whips cracked.[1s]Order emerged quickly. They elected a president, collected taxes, built a theater and casino. Women were banned, yet a hidden red‐light district thrived. The Qing dynasty finally noticed.[0.8s]Soldiers arrived, flames rose. By early 1886, Zheltuga burned, its gold legends vanished.
