In 1883, miners forged a secret gold city on the Amur River. Russian and Chinese prospectors built a bustling town of up to twelve thousand souls. They called it Zheltuga, nicknamed ‘California on the Amur’. A black‐and‐yellow flag waved over a makeshift casino, theater, and hospital.[0.8s] Lawmakers wrote twenty clauses; punishments included brutal whippings for minor offenses. Women were forbidden, spawning a thriving illicit sex trade across the river.[1s] By 1885 the settlement peaked, but Chinese forces began to stir. In January 1886, Qing soldiers razed Zheltuga, erasing it from maps. The ghost of the Amur's gold city still haunts historians today.
