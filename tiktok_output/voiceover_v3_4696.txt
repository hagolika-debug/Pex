In 1861, Assamese farmers rose against British taxes. New levies on betel leaf and areca nuts crushed livelihoods. Thousands assembled at Phulaguri’s raijmel, demanding fair treatment. The British ignored pleas, sending armed police.[1s] Violent confrontation erupted, Lieutenant Singer was killed.[0.5s] British retaliation killed over fifty farmers, imprisoning many. It marked Assam’s first organized anti‐colonial peasant movement. Today, Phulaguri’s martyrs are remembered in a modest memorial. Ordinary villagers proved courage can challenge empires.[0.8s] Remember forgotten histories; they shape our future.
