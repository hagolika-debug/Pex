In 897 Rome, a dead pope rose from his tomb.[2s] Stephen VI ordered the exhumation, placing Formosus on a throne. A deacon answered for the corpse, echoing ancient accusations. The dead pontiff was found guilty, his fingers cut off.[1s] His corpse was stripped, dressed as a layman, and buried. Later, the body was tossed into the Tiber, floating downstream.[0.5s] The public erupted, deposing Stephen VI and ending the macabre trial. Future synods annulled the trial, forbidding any corpse’s judgment. The Cadaver Synod remains a chilling reminder of power’s darkest games.
