In 897, Rome witnessed a trial unlike any other. Pope Stephen VI demanded the dead[1s] Pope Formosus to appear. The corpse was exhumed, robed, and placed on a throne. A deacon answered for the silent pontiff as onlookers gasped. Stephen accused Formosus of perjury, ambition, and illegal bishoprics. The dead pope was found guilty[0.5s] his fingers severed. His body stripped, dressed as a layman, then tossed into the Tiber. The public erupted[1s] deposing Stephen, who later died in prison. Later popes nullified the trial, banning posthumous judgments forever.
