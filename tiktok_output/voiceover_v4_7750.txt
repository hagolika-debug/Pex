In 1902, a German steamer sparked a deadly Haitian showdown. Rebel gunboat Crête‐à‐Pierrot seized the Markomannia near Cap‐Haïtien. Admiral Killick believed the ship hid weapons for the government.[0.5s] German consul protested, but the rebels transferred supplies anyway. Germany dispatched warship SMS Panther to exact revenge. Under fire, Killick ordered his crew to blow up the cruiser.[1s] The explosion sank Crête‐à‐Pierrot, killing Killick and four sailors. The U.S. watched nervously, weighing Monroe Doctrine implications.[0.8s] Today, Haiti commemorates Killick on a stamp, yet the clash fades.
