In 1184, King Henry VI called nobles to Erfurt's grand hall. A heated land dispute between Thuringia and Mainz set the stage. Hundreds of armored lords crowded the wooden floor above a cesspit. Rotten beams gave way under their combined weight.[1s] The floor shattered. Nobles plunged into darkness, drowning in foul, swirling sewage. [2s] King Henry survived, perched in a stone alcove untouched. Around sixty aristocrats perished, altering the Holy Roman power balance. The tragedy became a grim legend, warning of medieval sanitation. Even the mightiest can fall through a rotten floor.
