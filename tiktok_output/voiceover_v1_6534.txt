March 1876, Bath County, Kentucky: a strange sky phenomenon began. Chunks of red meat fell, covering a hundred‐by‐fifty yard field.[0.5s] Farmers shouted, animals fled, and the air smelled of decay. Scientists called it 'meat rain', but the truth stayed hidden.[1s] Vulture regurgitation emerged as the leading theory, backed by anatomy. Some suggested cyanobacteria Nostoc, turning rain into gelatinous flesh. Modern DNA tests in 2004 confirmed only old tissue, inconclusive. Every March, locals celebrate the bizarre 'Meat Shower Festival' with jokes. A reminder that history can be stranger than any fiction.[0.8s] What would you do if meat rained from the clouds?
