In 1932, Australian soldiers faced an unexpected opponent: emus. Farmers begged the government to halt crop devastation by the flightless birds. The Defence Minister deployed machine guns, believing birds were easy targets. First assault on November 2: guns jammed, emus scattered like bullets.[1s] Soldiers chased birds across fields, only a dozen fell. By December, 2,500 rounds fired[0.8s], yet only hundreds of emus died. The emus proved tougher than tanks, outrunning every gun. Public mocked the 'war', and the army withdrew in defeat. Today, the Emu War reminds us: nature can outsmart even soldiers.
