February 9, 1913, a strange fireball parade lit the night sky. Over 40 bright meteors swept from horizon to horizon across North America. No radiant point—just a silent line of glowing giants.[1s]Unsettling. Witnesses described a slow, five‐minute procession, each fireball lingering like lanterns. In some towns, the sky thundered—people feared the world was ending. Scientists later argued the meteors were a temporary satellite, orbiting Earth briefly.[0.8s]Mystery. Astronomer Clarence Chant documented hundreds of reports, proving it spanned 11,000 km. Today, the event remains a rare example of a fireball procession, still puzzling researchers.[1s] Next time you see a meteor, remember 1913—when the heavens marched together.
