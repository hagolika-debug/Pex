In July 1806, Indian sepoys rose against the British. They were angry about a new hat that erased their identity.[1s] At Vellore Fort, the mutineers seized the walls before sunrise. Hundreds of British soldiers fell asleep, unaware of the looming bloodshed. The rebels raised the banner of Tipu Sultan's sons, demanding freedom. Within hours, a cavalry from Arcot thundered back, breaking the siege.[0.5s] General Gillespie climbed the walls with a rope, leading a desperate bayonet charge. The mutiny was crushed; over three hundred sepoys died that day. Its memory faded, yet it foreshadowed India's massive 1857 rebellion.[0.8s]
