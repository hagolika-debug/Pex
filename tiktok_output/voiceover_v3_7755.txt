In 1910, British warships prowled the Persian Gulf. Dubai thrived as a free port, thriving on trade. Gun‐running flooded the town, angering the British Empire. When a British patrol demanded a search, delays sparked conflict.[1s] Captain Dick led 100 marines onto Dubai’s streets at dawn. Gunfire erupted; locals defended their homes against foreign steel. HMS Hyacinth unleashed six‐inch shells, turning alleys into firestorms.[0.8s] The bombardment killed thirty‐seven townspeople, a tragedy forgotten. British demands followed, forcing reparations and a reluctant peace.[1.2s] Today, Dubai rises from the ashes, its secret buried beneath skyscrapers.
