1883, gold fever erupted on the remote Amur River. Russian and Chinese prospectors converged, building a lawless town.[1s]Chaos thrived. They called it the 'California of the East'—gold glittered everywhere. At its peak, 12,000 souls—Russians, Chinese, Jews, even Koreans—coexisted. They elected leaders, raised taxes, even flew a black‐yellow flag.[0.8s]Order emerged. Casinos, theaters, and a circus thrived; whiskey flowed nightly. Qing officials finally learned of Zheltuga, demanding evacuation. Chinese troops returned, burned the settlement, and scattered its survivors.[1.2s]Silence fell. Today, Zheltuga lives only in faded photographs and forgotten legends.
