October 21, 1904, the Russian Baltic Fleet sails toward the Far East. Fog blankets the North Sea, visibility drops to a few meters.[1s] Admiral Rozhestvensky orders lights on, fearing Japanese torpedo boats. Russian gunners spot unlit silhouettes and mistake them for enemy vessels. Searchlights blaze, cannons roar—British trawlers are struck dead.[0.8s] Chaos erupts as Russian ships fire on each other, thinking they're Japanese. Two fishermen die, six wounded; the world watches in disbelief. Britain demands answers; war looms over the North Sea.[1.2s] Russia pays £66,000 compensation, and a monument stands in Hull today.
