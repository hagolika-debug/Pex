In 1902, a German steamer met Haitian rebels in a Caribbean port. The rebel gunboat Crête-à-Pierrot seized the Markomannia, accusing it of smuggling arms. Captain Nansen protested, but the Germans demanded the ship’s release. Germany dispatched the cruiser SMS Panther, sparking an international showdown. [2s]The Panther opened fire, and Killick chose a desperate sacrifice. He detonated his own ship’s magazines, sending the vessel into a fiery inferno. [1s]The flames illuminated a diplomatic crisis between Germany, Haiti, and the United States. The incident sparked protests, yet the US ultimately accepted Germany’s aggressive action. [0.5s]Today, Haiti commemorates Admiral Killick on a rare postage stamp. A forgotten flashpoint that shaped early 20th‐century Caribbean geopolitics.
