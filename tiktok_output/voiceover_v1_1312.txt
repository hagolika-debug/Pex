In 897 Rome, a dead pope[1s] faced a courtroom. Pope Stephen VI ordered the corpse exhumed, placed on a throne. A deacon was forced to answer for the silent saint. Formosus was accused of perjury, illegal papacy, and treason. The dead pontiff could not defend himself, only silence[0.5s] answered. Judges cut off his blessing fingers, stripping papal vestments. His body was cast into the Tiber, drifting like a ghost. The public erupted, deposing Stephen, who later met a grim end. Soon after, the synod was annulled, restoring Formosus' honor. A macabre trial[2s] that still haunts history's darkest chapters.
