In 1900, a quiet Michigan village vanished in flames. The Burt Lake Band had lived there for centuries, thriving peacefully. Treaties promised their land forever, but greed ignored the promise. They bought their own parcels with treaty annuity money, securing ownership. On October 15, sheriff and deputies set fire to every cabin.[1s] Only the 1838 St. Mary's church survived the inferno. Nineteen families fled, their homes reduced to ashes and memory. Decades of lawsuits followed,[0.8s] yet justice remained out of reach. Today, the burn‐out whispers,[1s] urging us to remember forgotten truths.
