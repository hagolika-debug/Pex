Deep in the 1880s, miners forged a rogue nation on the Amur. Gold glimmered along the Albazikha river, drawing thousands of dreamers. They called it Zheltuga, the 'California on the Amur'. A black‐and‐yellow flag rose above a bustling town of twelve thousand.[0.5s] Miners elected leaders, drafted twenty‐clause statutes, and enforced brutal punishments. Hotels, theaters, and a Monte Carlo‐style casino thrived amid the gold rush. Cixi’s imperial letter finally forced both China and Russia to act. In 1886, Chinese troops burned Zheltuga down.[1s]The gold vanished. Today, the ghost of Zheltuga haunts legends of a lost frontier.
