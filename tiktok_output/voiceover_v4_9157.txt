In 1883, gold glittered along the Amur River's banks. Prospectors from Russia and China built a wild frontier town. It became known as the Zheltuga Republic, the 'California on the Amur'. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived under a black‐and‐yellow flag. A makeshift court handed out whips for crimes, keeping order. The settlement boasted hotels, a theater, and a casino dubbed Monte Carlo. [1s]In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, threatening force. When the soldiers arrived, Zheltuga was set ablaze, disappearing forever. [0.5s]The ghost of this gold rush lives only in faded maps.
