In 1832, a tiny nation declared independence amid border confusion. Nestled between New Hampshire and Quebec, settlers faced double taxation. They formed the Republic of Indian Stream.[0.8s] Drafting a constitution. Luther Parker became justice of the peace, leading the fledgling government. British and American sheriffs demanded taxes, sparking a tense standoff. In 1835, New Hampshire militia marched.[1s] Demanding surrender. The Republic capitulated, becoming part of Pittsburg, New Hampshire. The dispute lingered until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally settled borders. Today, a quiet marker remembers this lost nation.[0.5s] A reminder of forgotten borders.
