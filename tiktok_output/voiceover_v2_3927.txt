In July 1184, nobles gathered in Erfurt to settle a feud. They convened in a grand stone church beneath a hidden latrine. Suddenly, the floor gave way, plunging them into a cesspit below. Wood shattered, bodies fell.[1s] The stench rose like a poison cloud. Sixty to a hundred aristocrats perished, crushed, drowned, or asphyxiated. King Henry VI and Archbishop Conrad clung to iron rails, surviving the horror. The tragedy failed to resolve the feud, but boosted Henry’s rise to emperor.
