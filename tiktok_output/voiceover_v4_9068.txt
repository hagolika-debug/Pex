On the remote Amur River, gold sparked an illegal boom. In 1883 miners founded the Zheltuga Republic, a frontier gold town. At its height, 12,000 prospectors lived there, called “California on the Amur.” Russians, Chinese, Koreans, Jews, and others mingled, sharing a unique pidgin. Miners drafted twenty statutes, enforced by brutal whips and public courts. The town boasted theaters, circuses, and a casino where fortunes vanished.[1s] By 1885 Qing officials demanded evacuation, fearing the gold‐filled rebellion. In January 1886 Chinese troops razed Zheltuga, [0.5s] ending its glittering dream. Today, its ghost whispers across the Amur, a lost gold myth.
