In 1272, a stolen cow sparked a six‐year war. A peasant took a cow at a tournament, then was executed mistakenly. The lord demanded compensation, prompting Namur to invade neighboring lands. In 1276, 2,400 men clashed at Ciney, half cavalry half infantry. The fight left 500 dead, including the mayor who led the defense. Soon after, the city was stormed, its church set ablaze.[1s] King Philip III of France ordered a return to the status quo. By 1278, peace was restored and the cow returned to its owner. The conflict is remembered as the ‘War of the Cow’[0.8s], a quirky history. Today, a historic route marks the battlefields for curious travelers.
