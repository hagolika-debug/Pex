In 1859, a single pig sparked an international standoff. The Oregon Treaty left the San Juan Islands' border ambiguous. American farmer Lyman Cutlar shot a roaming pig on his garden. The pig’s owner, a Hudson's Bay employee, demanded $100 compensation. Both nations sent troops—66 Americans and several British warships—to the island. Soldiers faced off at Griffin Bay, cannons pointed, yet no shots fired.[1s] Presidents Buchanan and Queen Victoria negotiated, avoiding a bloodless war. The island remained jointly garrisoned until 1872 arbitration settled the dispute.[0.5s] Today, the Pig War reminds us how tiny sparks can ignite nations.
