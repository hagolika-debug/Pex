Imagine a tsunami of beer flooding a city's streets. October 17, 1814, Meux’s Horse Shoe Brewery in London. A wooden vat, twenty‐two feet tall, brimmed with fermenting porter. Iron hoops slipped; the vat burst[1s] unleashing a roaring wave. Over three hundred thousand gallons surged, flooding the St Giles slum. Eight neighbors drowned, including a child sipping tea. Spectators paid to view the ruined brewery, a macabre attraction. The disaster forced breweries to abandon wooden vats forever. Today the Dominion Theatre stands, oblivious to the foamy tragedy. A forgotten beer tsunami still foams beneath London’s streets.[0.8s]
