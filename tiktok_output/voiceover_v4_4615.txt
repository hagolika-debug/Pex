January 15, 1919: Boston's North End faced an impossible wave. A 50‐foot steel tank held 2.3 million gallons of molasses. Warm winter days and rushed filling stressed the thin steel walls. The tank ruptured.[1s]Molasses roared through the streets at 35 mph. A 15‐foot sticky wave crushed buildings, overturned streetcars, and swept horses. Twenty‐one people died, and over a hundred were injured.[0.5s]Many never recovered. Navy cadets dived into the syrup, pulling survivors from the brown tide. The tragedy sparked the first major U.S. class‐action lawsuit. Boston smelled of molasses for decades, especially on hot summer days. Today, a marker remembers the flood, and engineers study its lessons.
