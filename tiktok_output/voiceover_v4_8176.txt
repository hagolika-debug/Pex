England’s rugged northeast coast hid a deadly secret in the 1860s. Between the Wear and Tyne, rocks claimed over a hundred vessels. Fishermen whispered of phantom lanterns guiding ships onto the jagged shore. On November 24, 1865, the schooner Test followed false lights.[1s]Tragedy struck. Survivors petitioned Parliament, demanding the government investigate the eerie phenomenon. In December, a Board of Trade commission arrived, interviewing coastguards and pilots.[0.8s] The inquiry concluded human error, not malicious lanterns, caused the wrecks. 1871 saw Souter Point lighthouse rise, ending the phantom light era. Today, the mystery fades, but the cliffs still whisper.[0.5s]
