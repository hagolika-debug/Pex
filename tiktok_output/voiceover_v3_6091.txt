In 1883, gold fever ignited a hidden city on the Amur River. Russian and Chinese miners built Zheltuga, a lawless 'California' far east. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors roamed bustling streets and saloons. They minted their own flag, black and yellow, symbolizing earth and gold.[1s] Law was harsh: 500 lashes for stealing a gold nugget. Women were banned, yet a shadow market thrived across the river.[0.8s] The Republic's wealth attracted Qing attention, prompting a diplomatic showdown. In early 1886, Chinese troops burned Zheltuga to ashes[0.5s], ending the gold rush. Today, the ruins whisper a forgotten chapter of frontier ambition.
