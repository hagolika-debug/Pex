In 1840,[0.5s] a silent war brewed over a humble mineral. Sicily produced most of the world’s sulphur, vital for industry. Britain’s booming factories demanded sulphur, secured by an 1816 treaty. King Ferdinand II handed a monopoly to French merchants, angering Britain. Palmerston threatened blockade; British navy seized Neapolitan ships without firing. Tensions escalated.[1s] The Mediterranean hummed with unease. Metternich warned Europe: a war over sulphur is absurd. After months of pressure, Ferdinand cancelled the contract on July 21. The crisis ended, but it revealed how a single element could spark diplomacy.
