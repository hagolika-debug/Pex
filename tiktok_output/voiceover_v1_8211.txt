In 1657, a whispered rumor sparked a deadly crackdown. A farmer named Hyōsaku mentioned a miracle‐performing boy.[0.5s] Authorities heard the tale, and the shogunate’s eyes opened. The domain dispatched inspectors.[1s] Night fell over Kōri. Six hundred hidden Christians were seized, families torn apart. Four hundred and fifty were beheaded, many died in prison. An eleven‐year‐old girl endured sixty‐four years of solitary confinement.[1.5s] She never left. The crackdown revealed Japan’s most sophisticated religious surveillance system. Yet hidden faith survived, whispered through generations for centuries.
