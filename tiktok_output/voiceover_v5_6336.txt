In the 1880s, gold fever birthed a wild frontier city. Nestled on the Amur River[0.5s], it was nicknamed California on the Amur. Russian and Chinese miners built Zheltuga, a bustling republic of twelve thousand. They forged their own flag, law, and even a circus troupe. Women were banned, yet a secret red-light district thrived nearby. Gold poured in, fortunes rose.[1s]Then the Qing empire struck back. In 1886, Chinese soldiers torched the settlement, erasing its glitter. Survivors scattered, but the legend of Zheltuga lives in whispers. A forgotten gold rush, hidden in the far‐east's icy rivers.
