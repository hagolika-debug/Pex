London, 1814: a quiet night turned into a frothy disaster. At Meux Brewery, a rusty iron hoop finally gave way. Seventy‐six thousand gallons of porter burst, flooding the streets. Neighbors fled upwards, [0.5s] climbing furniture as beer surged like a wave. Eight lives were lost, including a mother and her toddler. The city’s poor alleys turned into amber rivers, choking homes. Courts declared it an act of God, absolving the brewery. [1s] Yet the barrels never rose again, leaving a damp legend. Now we remember the night London drowned in beer.
