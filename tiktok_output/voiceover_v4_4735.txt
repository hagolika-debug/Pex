In the remote Amur River, a golden kingdom rose. In 1883, miners struck glittering veins, sparking a feverish rush. They built Zheltuga, the 'California on the Amur', a lawless city. Twelve thousand prospectors, from Russia, China, even Europe, converged. A black‐and‐yellow flag flew over hotels, theaters, and bustling markets.[1s] Miners voted rules; disobedient souls faced brutal lashes.[0.5s] By 1885, Chinese troops demanded evacuation; miners hid, then fled. In January 1886, Qing soldiers burned Zheltuga to ashes.[0.8s] The ghost town vanished, but its story whispers across history.
