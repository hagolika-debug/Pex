In 1184, German nobles gathered for a crucial council. They met inside Erfurt’s ancient cathedral, unaware of danger below. The wooden floor, weakened by time, could not bear their weight. Suddenly[0.8s] it gave way, plunging the assembly into darkness. They fell into a foul cesspit, a medieval toilet nightmare. Sixty to a hundred nobles drowned[0.5s], crushed, or suffocated. Miraculously, King Henry and Archbishop Conrad clung to iron rails. The tragedy halted peace talks[1s] but the emperor rose to power. Today, this macabre disaster reminds us power can crumble under a floor.
