In 1962, a Tanzanian school erupted in uncontrollable laughter. It started with three girls giggling during math class. Within days, half the 159 pupils were laughing nonstop.[1s] They cried, fainted, and couldn't attend lessons. The outbreak lasted 48 days before the school finally closed.[2s] When it reopened, another wave hit, affecting dozens more. Fourteen schools shut down, over a thousand people were affected. Scientists label it mass psychogenic illness, stress after independence.[1.5s] The Tanganyika laughter epidemic still haunts psychologists today.
