January 15, 1919, Boston's North End faced an unimaginable wave. A 50‐foot steel tank held 2.3 million gallons of molasses. Rising temperatures warmed the syrup, increasing pressure inside the tank. The tank burst,[0.5s] releasing a 35‐mph wave of sticky fury. Streets flooded waist‐deep, crushing buildings and toppling streetcars. Twenty‐one souls perished, dozens injured, horses tangled in the mess. Cadets plunged into the syrup, pulling survivors out.[1.5s] Time raced. Laws changed forever, demanding stronger engineering oversight for massive tanks. Even a century later, summer breezes still carried sweet molasses scent.[0.8s]
