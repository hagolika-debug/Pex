In 1755, a tiny Silesian village feared real vampires.[0.5s] Villagers claimed the dead rose, attacked sleepers, and drained blood. Maria Theresa ordered doctors to investigate the supernatural claims.[1s] Physicians exhumed twenty‐nine bodies, finding many mysteriously unrotted. The council voted to burn the suspected vampires alive.[0.8s] On January thirty‐first, the executioner led a torch‐lit procession. Nineteen corpses turned to ash, yet some bones survived.[1.2s] The tragedy forced an imperial decree banning clergy from such cases. Today, Hermersdorf’s vampire burnings warn how fear sparks cruelty.
