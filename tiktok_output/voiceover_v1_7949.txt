In 1886, a British cargo ship vanished off Japan's coast. The Normanton, laden with goods, set sail from Yokohama to Kobe. A violent storm drove it onto hidden reefs, sealing its fate.[1s] Captain Drake escaped in lifeboats, leaving 25 Japanese passengers behind.[0.8s] All Asian crew and passengers perished, while the crew survived. Outrage erupted across Japan, demanding justice for the dead.[1.2s] British courts exonerated the captain, citing extraterritoriality. Public fury forced a historic trial, convicting Drake to three months.[0.5s] The tragedy sparked anti‐Western sentiment and accelerated treaty reforms.
