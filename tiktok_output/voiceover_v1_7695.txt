In 1610, a massive Portuguese carrack clashed with Japanese samurai. The ship, loaded with silk and silver, was called the 'black ship'. A violent brawl in Macau left Japanese samurai dead, sparking revenge. Pessoa anchored in Nagasaki Bay, hoping to trade,[1s] unaware of looming attack. Samurai junks approached silently; gunfire erupted, lighting the night sky.[0.5s] Captain Pessoa ignited the powder magazine,[0.8s] choosing fire over surrender. The ship exploded twice,[1.2s] tearing the great vessel apart in flames. Japanese survivors looted treasure, while Portuguese trade in Japan waned. The battle echoed for centuries, shaping Japan's isolationist policies.
