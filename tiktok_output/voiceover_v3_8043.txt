In 1919, Boston's North End faced a sweet catastrophe. A massive steel tank held millions of gallons of molasses above the neighborhood. Neglected construction and rising temperature built deadly pressure inside. At noon, the tank burst[1s], releasing a 40‐foot wave of syrup. The sticky torrent smashed buildings, overturned carts, and trapped residents. Twenty‐one people died, many more injured, the city coated in brown. Courts held United States Industrial Alcohol liable[1.5s], reshaping safety regulations. A small plaque marks the spot, but few remember the tragedy. Imagine a wave of molasses crushing your street[0.8s]—history’s sweetest disaster.
