In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced a courtroom.[1s] Pope Stephen VI ordered the corpse of Formosus exhumed. The rotting body was placed on a throne, surrounded by bishops. A deacon answered for the dead, while crowds gasped in horror. Formosus was accused of perjury, illegal papacy, and simony.[0.5s] Stephen VI declared him guilty, cutting off his blessing fingers. The corpse was stripped, dressed as a layman, then thrown into the Tiber. The river carried his body, yet miracles were still reported. Public outrage toppled Stephen, ending the macabre trial forever.[1s]
