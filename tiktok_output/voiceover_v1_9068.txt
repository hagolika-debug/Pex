In 1849, Indigenous chiefs gathered to challenge a mining empire. Shingwaukonse, Nebenaigoching, and Oshawano led the bold resistance. They seized a small cannon from the Crown Lands Agent's lawn. On November 1, a schooner carried them toward Mica Bay. [1s] They demanded the mine shut down or pay for the land. Mine manager John Bonner refused, so the operation was abandoned. [0.5s] Rumors of a massacre spread across Detroit and Toronto. The government dispatched 87 soldiers, but harsh weather halted them. [1s] The clash forced 1850 treaties, securing Indigenous rights over minerals.
