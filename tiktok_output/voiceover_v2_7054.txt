September 25, 1904, the Cunene River became a battlefield of blood. Portugal pushed south[0.5s], claiming lands beyond their grasp, eyes on Angola. But the Ovambo, fierce and united, guarded their river home. A 500‐strong Portuguese column, led by Captain Luís Almeida, marched forward. They crossed the shallow waters—[1s]—then gunfire erupted from hidden cliffs. Over 300 men fell, including 114 Europeans, in minutes. The Ovambo claimed a crushing victory, defending their homeland. Portugal retaliated with brutal expeditions in 1905 and 1907, yet resistance lingered. Forgotten by many, this ambush reshaped Angola’s colonial history[0.8s] forever.
