In 1832, a tiny nation sprang up between New Hampshire and Canada. A confusing 1783 treaty left the land claimed by both sides. Residents faced taxes from U.S. sheriffs and British officials simultaneously. On July 9, they declared independence, drafting a constitution.[0.8s] Luther Parker became justice while citizens elected a council. Sheriff of Coos County demanded militia, threatening invasion.[1s] By August 5, 1835, New Hampshire forces occupied the territory. The border finally settled by the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty. Today, a historic marker in Pittsburg commemorates this forgotten republic.
