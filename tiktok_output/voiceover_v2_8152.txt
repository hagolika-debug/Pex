In 1863, a bizarre war erupted over seabird eggs. Two rival companies fought for exclusive rights[0.8s] to harvest murre eggs. The Pacific Egg Company claimed the South East Farallon islands as theirs. But independent harvesters, led by David Batchelder, refused to back down. On June 4, 1863, gunfire cracked the morning sea.[1s] Two men died, and the Egg Company secured total control. The triumph was fleeting; the government reclaimed the islands a decade later. By 1881, all egging operations were banned, ending the bloodied saga. Today, the Farallons protect wildlife, their violent past forgotten.[1s]
