In 1900, a quiet Michigan village vanished in flames. The Burt Lake Band lived on treaty‐protected land for generations. A land speculator claimed the land was delinquent and called the sheriff. October 15, deputies arrived, forced families out[1s], and set houses ablaze. Only St. Mary's stone church survived, later turned into a pig barn. Families scattered, some rebuilt nearby; the village never returned. The band sued for their land, but courts dismissed their claim. Today, descendants remember the burn‐out[0.8s] as a scarred chapter. Learn this hidden history, honor the lost voices.
