In 1932, a lone Korean hurled a grenade at Japan’s Emperor.[1s] Lee Bong‐chang, a Korean activist, blended into Tokyo under a Japanese name. He secured a lightweight Chinese grenade, planning a swift, daring strike. The imperial procession marched through Yoyogi Park, crowds cheering in unison. At Sakuradamon gate, Lee tossed the grenade toward the royal carriage.[0.8s] The explosion sputtered, damaging only a side panel; the Emperor lived. Courageously, Lee raised his hands, proclaiming his patriotic deed. Japan’s prime minister resigned, fearing the empire’s shaken image.[1s] Today, Lee is celebrated as a martyr, with statues honoring his sacrifice.
