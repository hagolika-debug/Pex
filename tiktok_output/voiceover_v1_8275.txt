July 24, 1915: Chicago's river turned into a deadly trap. The Eastland, a 275‐foot excursion steamer, was meant for fun. Over 2,500 workers, women, and children boarded, far beyond safe limits. The ship listed, then lurched violently...[1s]It flipped like a turtle. Eight hundred forty‐four souls perished, many under twenty years old.[0.5s] Rescuers scrambled, pulling survivors from the hull's exposed side. The tragedy sparked new safety laws for passenger vessels nationwide. Today, few remember, eclipsed by Titanic and Lusitania. A memorial stands in Chicago, honoring those lost that day.
