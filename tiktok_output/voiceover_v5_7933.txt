In 1184, a royal council turned into a deadly bathroom disaster. They gathered beneath Erfurt's towering cathedral, unaware of rotten floors below. A feud between the Archbishop of Mainz and Thuringia's landgrave sparked the meeting. When nobles crowded the stone floor, it gave way with a deafening crack.[0.5s] They plunged into a foul cesspit, drowning in filth and foul fumes. Sixty to a hundred aristocrats vanished forever.[1s]The rest barely escaped. King Henry clung to iron rails, surviving the stench and chaos. He rose to Holy Roman Emperor, his reign shaped by this near‐death. The Erfurt Latrine Disaster reminds us: even kings can fall into the toilet.
