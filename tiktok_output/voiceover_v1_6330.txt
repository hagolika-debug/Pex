In 1184, nobles met to settle a feud, but the floor vanished. They gathered in Erfurt’s ancient church, unaware of the hidden pit below.[1s] The wooden floor creaked, then shattered under the weight of dozens of aristocrats. Nobles plunged into a fetid cesspit, drowning in liquid excrement[0.5s] and despair. Some were crushed by falling beams; others suffocated from the noxious fumes. King Henry VI and Archbishop Conrad miraculously clung to iron railings, surviving.[1s] Estimates say sixty to a hundred nobles perished that dreadful summer day. The disaster halted the peace talks[0.8s], but Henry’s rise to emperor continued unabated. A medieval cautionary tale: even the mightiest can be sunk by a toilet.[1s]
