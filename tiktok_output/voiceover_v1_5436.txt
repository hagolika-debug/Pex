January 1, 1915, a mining town prepares for a holiday picnic. Thousands board an open‐carriage train heading toward Silverton. Two Afghan cameleers, Gool Mohammed and Mullah Abdullah, lie in wait.[1s] At 10:10 am, gunfire erupts, shattering the peaceful ride.[0.8s] Ten passengers are hit; three of them die instantly. The locals rally, grabbing rifles from a nearby gun club. A chaotic shootout ensues on the rocky outcrop, echoing through the desert. The attackers are wounded; one dies on the field, the other later. The incident sparks anti‐Afghan sentiment, burning the German Club.[0.5s] Today, this battle remains a forgotten chapter of World War I history.
