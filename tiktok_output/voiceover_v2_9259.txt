In 1915, Chicago’s river turned into a deadly ballroom. The excursion steamer Eastland was meant for a festive picnic. Over 2,500 workers and families crammed aboard,[0.5s] ignoring safety limits. Extra lifeboats added weight, reducing stability to dangerous levels. At 7:23 a.m., the ship listed sharply, water rushed in. Within minutes, Eastland capsized like a turtle,[2s] shocking onlookers. More than 800 souls perished, many were children under twenty‐five. The tragedy faded quickly; even newspapers gave it brief coverage. Today, the Eastland rests beneath Chicago’s waters,[1s] a silent reminder.
