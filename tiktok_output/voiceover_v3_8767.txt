In 1883, gold sparked a rogue city on the Amur. Russians, Chinese, and wanderers swarmed, forming a multiethnic gold rush. They declared a republic, hoisted a black‐yellow flag, and elected leaders. A strict code punished thieves with whips.[1s]Violence vanished quickly. Gold flowed to taverns, theaters, and even a Monte Carlo casino. Women forbidden, yet a shadow market blossomed across the river. Qing forces demanded evacuation; miners fled as soldiers approached. The settlement burned to ash.[2s]History erased, memory flickers. Zheltuga remains a vanished ‘California’ on the Amur, forgotten today.
