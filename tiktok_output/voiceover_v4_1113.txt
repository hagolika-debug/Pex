In 1835, New York's Sun newspaper spun a sensational lunar tale. Six articles claimed Sir John Herschel discovered moon beasts and towering temples.[1s] Readers gasped at bison, unicorns, and bat‐like humanoids on the moon. The newspaper advertised it as a reprint from the Edinburgh Courant. Sales skyrocketed, proving sensationalism sells more than sober facts. But on September 16, The Sun finally confessed it was all fiction.[1.5s] The hoax sparked debates, influencing future journalism ethics and public trust. Even famed author Edgar Allan Poe later mocked the lunatic lunar craze. Today, the Great Moon Hoax reminds us how easily truth can be eclipsed.
