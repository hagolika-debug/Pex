A hidden gold rush birthed a fleeting republic on the Amur River.[0.5s] In 1883, prospectors unearthed glittering veins along the Albazikha River. Russians and Chinese built a lawless city, nicknamed 'California on the Amur'.[1s] At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived without women, under a black‐yellow flag. A self‐made council taxed gold, ran a hospital, and even a circus. Violence surged; whips and lashes enforced order in the wild frontier.[0.8s] The Qing dynasty finally intervened, demanding evacuation in August 1885. By January 1886 Chinese troops razed the settlement, ending the republic. Its ashes scattered, but the legend of Zheltuga still glitters today.
