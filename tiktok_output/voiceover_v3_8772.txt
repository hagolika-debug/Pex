In 1904, Russian warships fired on innocent British fishermen. The Baltic Fleet raced to the Pacific, fearing Japanese torpedo boats. Foggy night, they spotted tiny lights and thought enemy vessels. Searchlights blinded trawlers; cannon fire erupted[0.8s], killing two fishermen. Chaos grew as Russian ships mistakenly fired on each other. Britain nearly declared war[1s]; the world held its breath. An international commission met in Paris, demanding accountability. Russia paid £66,000, a rare apology for a naval blunder. The Dogger Bank memorial still stands, honoring fallen trawlers. A war that never happened[0.5s], sparked by a tragic case of mistaken identity.
