In early 1900s Kentucky, tobacco farmers faced a ruthless monopoly. The American Tobacco Company forced prices down, driving growers to desperation. Farmers formed the Planters' Protective Association, vowing to withhold their crops. When pooling failed, masked Night Riders took the law into their own hands.[1s] [0.5s] They rode silently through the night, brandishing torches and dynamite. December 1906, Princeton, Kentucky burned as warehouses of tobacco turned to flames. The terror spread; Hopkinsville prepared defenses, only to be fooled by a hoax. [1s] By 1908 the Night Riders were finally stopped by the state guard. Their legacy remains a hidden chapter of American labor resistance.
