Imagine a tidal wave of beer flooding London streets. On October 17, 1814, St Giles turned into chaos. A massive fermentation vat burst, releasing over three thousand barrels. The wooden tank shattered like a cannonball.[1s] A 15‐metre high wave of porter rushed through alleys. Eight women and children drowned, their lives lost to beer.[0.8s] Locals scooped the foam, some even drank it on the spot. The disaster forced breweries to replace wooden vats with concrete. Today, a small ale commemorates the flood in historic London.[0.5s]
