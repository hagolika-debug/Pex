Imagine a whole town vanished overnight, exiled by a mining corporation.[0.5s] In 1917, Bisbee, Arizona, thrummed with copper mines and tension. The IWW demanded fair wages, safety, and an end to abuse. Company boss Phelps Dodge hired a 2,000‐strong posse to crush dissent. At dawn, deputies rounded up 1,300 miners.[1s]No warning, no mercy. They were crammed into filthy cattle cars, traveling 200 miles under scorching sun. Arriving in New Mexico, the deportees faced empty camps and hostile locals. The government sent troops, yet no one was ever convicted.[0.8s] Bisbee's dark chapter reshaped labor law, warning of corporate tyranny.
