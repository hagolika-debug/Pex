In 1780, a forgotten rebellion erupted on India's east coast.[1s] Vizagapatam, a bustling port under British East India Company control. Sepoys, unpaid and ordered to fight Hyder Ali, refused to board ships.[0.5s] On October 3, they turned guns on their officers, killing many. Governor Casmajor and civilians were seized, a French spy freed.[1s] The mutineers marched inland, hoping to join Hyder Ali's forces. British troops pursued, engaging in a brief skirmish; the mutiny fell.[0.8s] History erased it, yet scholars call it India's first sepoy uprising. Remember Vizagapatam's 1780 mutiny—an early echo of India's freedom fight.[1.2s]
