In 1730, a British slaver set sail from Guinea. Onboard, ninety-six Africans were shackled, destined for Rhode Island markets. Five days at sea, whispers turned into a daring plan. At 4 a.m., captives broke their iron chains and stormed the deck.[1s] They seized weapons, killed three crew members, and threatened a bomb. Realizing the danger, the crew surrendered, fearing the ship's destruction. Without navigation skills, the rebels turned the vessel back toward Africa.[0.5s] They reached Sierra Leone River, abandoned the ship, and escaped to freedom. Their story survived through Captain Scott's journal, a rare record of resistance.
