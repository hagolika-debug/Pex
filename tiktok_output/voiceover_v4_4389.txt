Imagine a tiny nation sprouting between the US and Canada, 1832.[1s] Confusing 1783 treaty left a wedge of land double‐taxed. July 9, 1832, locals voted for independence, drafting a constitution. A president, judges, and militia guarded the fledgling republic.[0.5s] Three hundred settlers farmed, hunted, and paid taxes to nobody. New Hampshire sheriffs demanded taxes; militia gathered, tension rose. After a showdown, the republic surrendered, becoming part of New Hampshire.[2s] The border dispute lingered until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty settled it. Today, a historic marker whispers the story of this lost nation.
