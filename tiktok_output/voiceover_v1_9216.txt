In 1904, a Russian fleet mistook fishermen for enemy torpedoes. The Baltic fleet sailed through the misty North Sea at night. Suddenly, searchlights cut through darkness, illuminating trembling trawlers.[1s] Russian guns roared, riddling the humble boats with shells. Two fishermen died, their cries lost to the waves.[0.8s] Britain demanded answers, fearing a war with Russia. An international commission forced Russia to pay £66,000 compensation. The tragedy sparked the first modern international arbitration case. A near‐war averted, all because of mistaken torpedo boats.[0.5s]
