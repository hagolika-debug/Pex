In 1883, gold glittered along the Amur's remote banks. Prospectors from Russia and China built a lawless town overnight.[0.5s] At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived under a black‐yellow flag. Women were forbidden, spawning a thriving cross‐river red‐light district. The settlement boasted a casino dubbed ‘Monte Carlo of the Far East’.[0.8s] Miners voted laws, punished crimes with brutal whips and lashes. The Qing dynasty finally noticed, demanding evacuation within eight days. Chinese troops returned, razed the town, and scattered its survivors.[1s] Today, Zheltuga lives only in faded photographs and whispered legends.
