In 1184, noblemen gathered in Erfurt for a high‐stakes council. They aimed to settle a bitter feud between Archbishop Conrad and Landgrave Ludwig. The meeting took place inside the ancient Petersberg church, built on wooden floors. Suddenly, the floor gave way, dropping the assembly into a foul cesspit below.[1s] Sixty to a hundred nobles drowned, were crushed, or suffocated by toxic fumes. Only a handful escaped, clinging to iron window rails as chaos erupted.[0.5s] King Henry survived, later becoming Holy Roman Emperor, shaping Europe's destiny. The Erfurt Latrine Disaster remains a macabre reminder of medieval sanitation hazards.[0.8s] Even the most powerful can be swallowed by a toilet.
