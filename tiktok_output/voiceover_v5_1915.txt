Imagine a dead pope, perched upon a marble throne. In 897, Pope Stephen VI ordered the corpse exhumed. They propped the rotting body[1s], crown placed, and began the trial. A deacon spoke for the dead, accusing him of perjury. The crowd jeered, while the pope’s corpse remained silent. Stephen declared Formosus guilty[0.5s], stripping him of papal vestments. The dead pontiff was thrown into the Tiber River, never to rise. Public outrage sparked riots; Stephen was later imprisoned and strangled. The Cadaver Synod remains history’s strangest courtroom drama, forgotten by many.
