A daring German airship test turned into a fiery catastrophe. On October 17, 1913, LZ 18 lifted from Berlin's outskirts.[0.5s] Hundred‐meter‐high, the massive zeppelin carried twenty‐eight souls. Spectators watched as hydrogen hissed, then an engine sparked. The flame devoured the hull, [1s] and the sky turned black. All twenty‐eight onboard perished; only a wounded officer survived briefly. The disaster shocked a nation, [1s] halting zeppelin ambitions temporarily. Investigators blamed vented hydrogen sucked into the engine, igniting instantly. Today few remember the Johannisthal tragedy, a forgotten lesson in daring.
