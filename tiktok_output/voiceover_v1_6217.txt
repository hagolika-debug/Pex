December 13, 1867, a quiet London street was about to erupt. Irish Republicans aimed to free two comrades imprisoned in Clerkenwell House. They loaded a massive barrel of gunpowder against the prison wall. The fuse misfired, then detonated[1s]—shattering walls and nearby homes. Three people died instantly, dozens more injured by flying bricks. Victims ranged from a 47‐year‐old laborer to a seven‐year‐old girl. The blast sparked public outrage[0.8s], prompting a massive relief fund. Only one Fenian, Michael Barrett, was convicted and publicly hanged[1.2s]. The event marked Britain’s first Irish bomb attack, fading into history. Today, the ruined wall lies silent, a reminder of miscalculated rebellion.
