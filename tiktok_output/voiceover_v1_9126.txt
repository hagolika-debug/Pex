In the early 1800s, a tiny nation emerged between the US and Canada. A vague 1783 treaty left the land unclaimed, sparking confusion. On July 9, 1832, locals declared independence, drafting their own constitution. They elected a council, justice, and even a militia to protect their borders. Both British and American sheriffs demanded taxes.[1s] Citizens chose sovereignty. New Hampshire militia marched,[0.5s] forcing the republic to surrender. The border dispute lingered until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally resolved it. Today, the town of Pittsburg, New Hampshire, marks the site of this lost nation. Imagine living in a country that existed for just three years—[2s] forgotten by history.
