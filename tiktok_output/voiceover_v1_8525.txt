At the turn of the 20th century, Kentucky’s farms boiled with anger. Big tobacco monopoly crushed prices, pushing small growers toward desperation. In 1904, farmers formed the Planters’ Protective Association, seeking justice. When negotiations failed,[1s] masked Night Riders emerged, riding silently through moonlit hills. December 1906: They seized Princeton,[0.8s] igniting warehouses and terrorizing townsfolk. Their message? ‘Sell only to the Association,’ or face violent retribution. The raids sparked statewide outrage, forcing the Kentucky militia into action. By 1909, federal courts dismantled the monopoly,[0.5s] ending the Night Rider terror. Today, the Black Patch War remains a forgotten chapter of American resistance.
