In 1926, a fake radio broadcast sparked nationwide panic. Father Ronald Knox disguised a satirical play as breaking news. On 16 January, listeners heard rumors of a London[1s] revolution. Crowds imagined barricades, soldiers[0.5s], even Big Ben exploding. Calls flooded newspapers, but a snowstorm delayed their arrival. The BBC interrupted the program[1.5s] to announce it was a satire. Thousands felt terror, proving the power of imagined sound. This hoax inspired Orson Welles' 1938 'War of the Worlds'. Even today, a single broadcast can ignite panic. History reminds us: listen critically, question the noise.
