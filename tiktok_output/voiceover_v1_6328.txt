In 1902, a German steamer sparked a deadly Haitian showdown. The Markomannia, bound for Hamburg, docked near Cap‐Haïtien. Rebel gunboat Crête‐à‐Pierrot boarded, accusing it of smuggling arms. German captain protested, but the rebels seized the cargo anyway.[1s] Germany dispatched warship SMS Panther, demanding restitution and punishment. Admiral Killick chose destruction, blowing up his own ship's magazines.[0.8s] The explosion sank Crête‐à‐Pierrot, killing Killick and four crew. The US watched nervously, fearing a breach of the Monroe Doctrine.[0.5s] Despite outrage, the incident cemented German naval influence in Haiti. Today, a Haitian stamp commemorates Admiral Killick’s tragic sacrifice.
