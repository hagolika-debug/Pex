In the 1880s, gold fever birthed a wild frontier town. Nestled on the Amur River, miners forged the Zheltuga Republic. Roughly twelve thousand prospectors, Russians and Chinese, lived without law. They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, calling it ‘California on the Amur’. A makeshift court met daily, punishing thieves with brutal whips.[1s] Despite chaos, the settlement boasted hotels, a theater, and a casino. Women were barred; a hidden red‐light district thrived across the river.[0.8s] In 1886, Qing troops stormed the town, burning it to ashes. Today, the ghost of Zheltuga whispers a forgotten gold rush tale.
