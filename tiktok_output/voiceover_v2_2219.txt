In 1902, a German steamer sparked a deadly clash in Haiti. Haitian rebels seized the Markomannia,[0.5s] suspecting hidden weapons. Berlin dispatched the warship SMS Panther to retaliate. The Panther pursued the rebel gunboat Crête-à‐Pierrot across turbulent waters. Killick detonated his ship's magazine, sinking it with his crew.[1s] Germany claimed rights, while the US quietly watched the Monroe Doctrine unfold. The incident faded, yet Haiti honored Killick on a 1943 stamp. A forgotten naval showdown that shaped early 20th‐century geopolitics.[0.8s]
