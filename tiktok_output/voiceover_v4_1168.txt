In 1919, Boston’s streets turned into a sticky nightmare. A 50‐foot steel tank burst[0.5s], releasing 2.3 million gallons. Molasses surged at 35 mph, a wave twenty‐five feet high. People screamed, buildings crumbled.[1s]The city was engulfed. Twenty‐one lives lost, including horses trapped in the brown torrent. Rescuers waded through syrup, pulling survivors from waist‐deep goo. The sticky flood lingered; Boston smelled of molasses for decades. The tragedy reshaped building codes.[0.8s]Safer tanks followed. Today, a plaque marks the site where the tank once stood. A sweet disaster reminding us how ordinary things can become deadly.
