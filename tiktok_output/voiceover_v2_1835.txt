Imagine a wave of sticky syrup roaring through city streets. On January 15, 1919, Boston's North End faced an unimaginable nightmare. A massive 2.5‐million‐gallon molasses tank burst[1s], unleashing chaos. Molasses surged up to 40 feet high, racing at 35 miles per hour. Twenty‐one people perished, many suffocated[0.8s], and horses met a sticky fate. The city scraped streets for weeks, still smelling of molasses years later. Lawsuits forced stricter tank regulations, reshaping American industrial safety. Today, the sweet disaster inspires tours, memes, and lingering curiosity.
