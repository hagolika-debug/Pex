In 1900, a quiet Michigan village vanished in flames.[1s] The Burt Lake Band of Chippewa and Ottawa called it home. A land speculator claimed the land was tax‐delinquent. Sheriff Ming and deputies arrived with a writ of assistance. One by one, they set fire to each family cabin.[0.5s] Only the 1838 St. Mary’s Church survived the inferno. The community was forced onto neighboring lands, their homes reduced to ash.[1s] Decades later, courts denied them justice, calling the land 'taxable'. Today, the story remains a scar on Michigan’s hidden history.
