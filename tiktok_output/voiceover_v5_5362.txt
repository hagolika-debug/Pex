In the early 1800s, a tiny nation vanished into history. Nestled between New Hampshire and Canada, residents faced double taxation[0.5s]. Ambiguous 1783 Treaty lines left the land unclaimed. July 9, 1832, they declared independence, forming the Indian Stream Republic. A modest constitution granted them courts, militia, and a president. But peace was fragile[1s]. Neighboring powers eyed the border. In 1835, New Hampshire militia marched, demanding surrender. The Republic capitulated, becoming Pittsburg, New Hampshire, yet its story lingered. A forgotten nation, erased by treaties, lives on in legend[0.8s].
