In 1900, a football rivalry turned deadly in San Francisco. Thousands swarmed the makeshift stands, hoping to watch Stanford vs. Cal. Teenagers climbed the glassworks roof for a free view, ignoring warnings. The fragile roof collapsed[1s], crushing spectators beneath scorching furnace heat. Screams filled the air as bodies fell into the inferno. Rescue teams fought the blaze, but many lives were already lost.[2s] Thirteen children died, dozens more injured, the city mourned in silence. Newspapers splashed the tragedy on front pages while the game played on. Today, the disaster is hidden, forgotten beneath modern streets and stadiums.[0.5s] Remember the 1900 Big Game tragedy—a haunting reminder of crowd safety.
