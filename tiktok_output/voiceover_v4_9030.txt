A tiny Italian republic survived centuries, hidden between rival borders. In 1441 a cartographic error carved out a 330‐hectare no‐man's land. Local farmers seized the chance, declaring themselves free from any taxes. The republic had no army, no prisons; neighbors settled disputes. They cultivated tobacco without papal permission, becoming Italy's first legal growers.[0.8s] In 1642 the Pope threatened excommunication for smoking, yet Cospaia ignored.[1s] Merchants flooded the borderless market, swapping salt, silk, and secret deals. By 1826 the Vatican cut supplies, forcing the tiny republic's surrender. Fourteen representatives signed the act, receiving papal coins as compensation. Today, Cospaia's stone church still whispers ‘Perpetual liberty’ to visitors.[0.5s]
