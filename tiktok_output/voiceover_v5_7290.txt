In 1883, gold glittered along the Amur's remote banks. Prospectors from Russia and China swarmed, birthing a wild frontier town.[1s] Within months, population surged to twelve thousand, a true gold rush. They named it Zheltuga, the 'California on the Amur'.[0.8s] A black‐and‐yellow flag flew over makeshift banks, hotels, and theaters. Miners drafted laws, punished thieves with whips, and taxed gold. The grand casino 'Monte Carlo' roared, losing thousands in a night. In 1885, Qing forces arrived, demanding evacuation within eight days. When they returned, flames devoured Zheltuga, erasing its fleeting empire.[1.2s]
