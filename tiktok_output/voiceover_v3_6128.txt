A tiny strip of land in Italy became a nation by mistake. In 1441, two surveyors drew different streams as the border. The mistake left 330 hectares unclaimed, a no‐man’s land.[0.8s] The locals declared independence, enjoying freedom from taxes and armies. Cospaia thrived as a tax‐free hub, smuggling salt and goods. In 1574 they planted tobacco, becoming Italy’s first legal growers. When the Pope banned smoking, Cospaia remained exempt, thriving.[1s] After 385 years, Pope Leo XII forced its surrender in 1826. The village today remembers its liberty with an annual festival.[1s] A cartographic error birthed a republic that outlived empires.
