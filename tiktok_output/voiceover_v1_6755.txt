On September 3, 1878, the paddle steamer Princess Alice set sail. Hundreds of working‐class families boarded, seeking a day out at Gravesend. Near Woolwich, she collided with the massive Bywell Castle[1s] tearing her hull. Water flooded decks, Victorian dresses weighed passengers down[0.5s] panic erupted. Nearby boats threw ropes, but sewage‐filled water claimed many lives. Between six hundred and seven hundred souls vanished into the Thames. The disaster sparked stricter river safety laws[0.8s] and public swimming pools. Today, the mass grave at Woolwich Cemetery whispers this lost tragedy.
