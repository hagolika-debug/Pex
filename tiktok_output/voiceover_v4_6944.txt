In 1904, a midnight misfire turned fishing boats into war targets. The Russian Baltic Fleet, racing to the Far East, roamed the North Sea. Foggy night, they saw tiny lights and assumed Japanese torpedo boats. Searchlights blinded trawlers; cannon fire ripped through the quiet waters.[1s] British fishermen screamed as shells exploded, two lives lost instantly. Russian ships, confused, turned guns on each other, causing more destruction.[1s] Britain demanded answers; war between empires seemed inevitable.[1s] An international commission investigated, Russia paid £66,000 compensation to fishermen. Today, a memorial in Hull honors the forgotten tragedy of Dogger Bank.
