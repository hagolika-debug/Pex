In August 1930, the Amazon sky roared with an unknown fireball. Villagers near the Curuçá River heard thunderous booms that shook the jungle. A mysterious blaze lit the canopy, leaving scorched trees and stunned silence.[1s] A Franciscan friar, Fedele d'Alviano, documented terrified witnesses five days later. He sent a detailed letter to the Vatican Observatory, sparking intrigue.[0.5s] Decades later, astronomer Mark Bailey uncovered the report in Vatican archives. Scientists debated: meteor air burst or myth? Energy estimates ranged from kilotons to megatons.[0.8s] Expeditions in the 1990s searched for a crater, finding only a circular river island. The Curuçá event remains Brazil's unsolved cosmic mystery, echoing Tunguska's legacy.
