In July 1518, Strasbourg streets filled with unstoppable dancers. A woman named Frau Troffea started moving, no music, no reason.[1s] Within days, dozens joined; by month, four hundred twirled together. City council declared it a fever of hot blood, prescribing more dancing. They hired musicians, built a stage, and paid drums to worsen mania.[0.8s] Physicians watched dancers collapse, some dying from exhaustion or stroke. Modern scholars argue ergot fungus or mass hysteria sparked the frenzy. By September, authorities sent dancers to St. Vitus shrine for absolution. The mystery endures; no one knows why the city danced to death. A bizarre chapter reminding us how fear can move bodies.
