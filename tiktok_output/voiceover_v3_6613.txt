In the 1880s, a gold rush birthed a hidden frontier. On the Amur River's banks, miners built a bustling makeshift city.[0.5s] Russians, Chinese, Koreans, Jews—diverse crowds lived under one flag. They raised hotels, a theater, and a casino dubbed Monte Carlo. A miner‐written code imposed brutal punishments—whipping, exile, even death. Chinese officials ignored them at first, then demanded immediate evacuation.[1s] In 1886, Qing troops attacked, razing the town to ruins.[0.8s] The settlement vanished, but its story survives in forgotten maps. Zheltuga, meaning 'gold' in Russian, remains a lost gold rush legend.
