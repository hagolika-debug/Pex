In 1835, two states nearly went to war over a strip The contested land was a 468‐square‐mile ribbon called the Toledo Strip Ohio claimed the border based on its 1802 constitution, Michigan disagreed Both sides sent militias; the only bloodshed was a single stabbing[0.5s] March 31, 1835, Ohio troops gathered at Perrysburg, ready to march Michigan's General Brown ambushed surveyors at Phillips Corners, firing warning shots President Andrew Jackson intervened, favoring Ohio to protect a crucial swing vote[1s] Congress settled the dispute: Ohio kept the Strip, Michigan gained the Upper Peninsula[0.5s] Today, the 'Toledo War' lives only in rivalry jokes and quirky road lines
