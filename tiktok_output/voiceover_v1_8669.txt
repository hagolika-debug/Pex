In 1902, New York's Lower East Side erupted over meat prices. The massive Beef Trust hiked kosher beef costs, starving immigrant families. Jewish housewives formed the Ladies Anti‐Beef Trust Association, demanding fairness.[0.5s] They organized a citywide boycott, refusing to buy any kosher meat. Violent clashes erupted when stubborn butchers sold meat to 'scabs'.[1s] Police battered protesters, yet the women persisted, chanting for lower prices. President Roosevelt ordered investigations, forcing the Trust to lower prices. By June, kosher beef prices fell, and the boycott ended triumphantly.[0.8s] The forgotten war proved consumer power could challenge corporate greed.
