In January 1919, Boston's North End faced an unimaginable disaster. A massive steel tank, holding 2.3 million gallons of molasses, burst. The brown wave surged fifteen feet high[0.5s], crushing everything in its path. Residents were trapped, suffocating under the sticky torrent. The flood claimed twenty‐one lives.[1s]The city mourned. Investigations revealed shoddy steel—same material that sank the Titanic. Lawyers blamed anarchists, but the judge saw negligence.[0.8s]Justice prevailed. The tragedy forced stricter building codes, reshaping American engineering. Today, a modest plaque marks the site, reminding us of the sweet catastrophe.
