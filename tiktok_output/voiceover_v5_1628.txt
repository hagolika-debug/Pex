In the wilds of Siberia, a gold fever birthed a republic. By 1883, 12,000 prospectors flooded the Amur's banks. They named it Zheltuga, the ‘California on the Amur’. A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered over makeshift saloons and a circus. Law was written in twenty clauses,[0.5s] enforced with brutal whips. Gold glittered, greed grew.[1s]The settlement spiraled into chaos. Women were banned; a shadow market thrived across the river. In 1886, Qing forces stormed the town, burning it to ash. The gold vanished.[0.8s]History forgot Zheltuga’s wild dream.
