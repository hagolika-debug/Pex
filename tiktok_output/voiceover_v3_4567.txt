In the 1880s, a gold fever birthed a hidden republic. On the Amur's banks, miners built Zheltuga, a lawless city.[0.8s] At its peak, 12,000 prospectors from dozens of nations mingled. They elected leaders, raised taxes, and flew a black‐yellow flag. Punishments were brutal—whips for thieves, lashes for drunkenness.[1s] Women were banned, spawning a notorious vice district across the river. In 1885, Chinese troops demanded evacuation; the miners scoffed. January 1886, the settlement burned, erased from maps forever.[1.2s] Now, Zheltuga lives only in ghost stories of lost gold.
