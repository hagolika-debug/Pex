In the 1830s, a forgotten republic blossomed in New Hampshire's woods. Treaty of Paris left a vague river head, sparking a jurisdictional nightmare. Residents paid taxes to both America and Canada, living in legal limbo. On July 9, 1832, locals declared the Republic of Indian Stream. They drafted a full constitution, electing a House and a magistrate.[1s] A tiny militia kept peace while families logged, farmed, and traded. A constable's arrest of a Canadian sheriff sparked a diplomatic standoff. By 1836, they voted to join New Hampshire, ending the experiment.[0.8s] Today a marker stands, remembering a three‐year fight for self‐governance.[0.5s]
