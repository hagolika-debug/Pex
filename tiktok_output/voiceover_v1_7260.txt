In 1883, gold fever sparked a rogue city on the Amur. Russian and Chinese miners built a makeshift 'California' without borders. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived under a black‐yellow flag. Law was written in twenty clauses, enforced by brutal whips. Women were banned, yet a hidden red‐light district thrived. [1s] The settlement boasted theaters, orchestras, and a casino dubbed Monte Carlo. [0.5s] But the Qing dynasty finally demanded evacuation, threatening force. In January 1886, Chinese troops burned Zheltuga, ending its wild era. Today, the ghost of the Amur's gold republic fades into forgotten history.
