Imagine a wild gold rush, but on the Chinese border. In 1883, miners forged a makeshift city called Zheltuga. At its peak, twelve thousand hopefuls lived like a lawless California. They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, declaring their own republic. No women, no official government—just miners voting in dusty streets.[1s] Crime thrived: escaped convicts, gambling dens, and secret vodka trades. The Qing dynasty finally noticed, demanding evacuation within eight days.[0.5s] When soldiers returned in 1886, they burned Zheltuga to ash.[1.5s] Survivors scattered, but the legend of the ‘California on the Amur’ lives on. History's forgotten frontier reminds us: fortunes rise and vanish in seconds.
