May 24, 1815: A forgotten clash erupts on Missouri’s frontier. While the War of 1812 had ended, news hadn't reached the West. Missouri Rangers, fifty strong, pursued Black Hawk’s Sauk warriors near the Cuivre River. The Native fighters fled into a natural sinkhole—[0.5s] a limestone cage. Rangers tried shooting down, but the deep walls swallowed their bullets. They built a moving wooden shield, a makeshift battery, to approach. [1s] The shield failed; Sauk warriors fired through, killing Lieutenant Spears. Night fell, Black Hawk slipped out, leaving the battlefield unresolved. This tiny battle proved peace treaties ignored Indigenous resistance on the frontier.
