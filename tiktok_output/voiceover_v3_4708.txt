In 1861, Assam's quiet fields erupted into rebellion. British imposed new taxes on betel leaf and areca nut. Farmers gathered at a raijmel, demanding fair treatment. The British officer refused to listen.[1s] Anger boiled. Police arrived, but the thousand-strong crowd stood their ground. Lieutenant Singer tried to disarm a farmer.[0.5s] Violence erupted. Singer was beaten, thrown into the Kolong River, and killed. British reprisals followed, killing over fifty peasants in brutal sweeps. The Phulaguri Uprising faded, but its legacy whispers in Assam today.
