In 1868, a tiny war erupted over a fragrant wood. Britain craved camphor, the prized scent used in medicine and explosives. The Qing dynasty guarded a monopoly on Taiwan’s camphor trade. When diplomatic talks failed, the British fleet sailed into Anping harbor.[1s] They bombarded the town, shattering windows and igniting panic.[0.5s] The clash lasted only weeks, but its echoes lingered. Negotiated camphor regulations briefly broke the monopoly, reshaping trade. Yet the war faded, hidden behind larger imperial conflicts.[0.8s] A scent that sparked a forgotten battle, still whispers through history.
