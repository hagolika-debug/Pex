In 1883, gold glimmered along the Amur's remote banks. Russians and Chinese rushed there, building a lawless frontier town. Within months, 12,000 miners lived together, calling it Zheltuga. They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, dubbing it the 'California on the Amur'. Gold flowed, but so did violence, whips, and secret pacts. In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, but the miners ignored.[1s] Soon the Chinese army returned, torch in hand, to crush the dream. The settlement burned, its gold vanished, leaving only ghostly stories.[0.8s] Today, Zheltuga lives only in faded photos and whispered legends.
