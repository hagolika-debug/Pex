In the early 1800s, a tiny nation sprang between the US and Britain. Its borders were defined by a vague line from the 1783 Treaty of Paris. Disputed territory became Indian Stream, where farmers faced double taxes from two governments. In 1832, locals drafted a constitution and declared independence[0.5s], forming the Indian Stream Republic. They elected a council, held elections, and even printed their own paper. Both British sheriffs and New Hampshire tax collectors demanded payment.[1s] When militia threatened invasion, the Republic surrendered, becoming part of New Hampshire in 1835. The dispute lingered until the Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally settled the border in 1842. Today, the town of Pittsburg hides the ghost of a forgotten nation.
