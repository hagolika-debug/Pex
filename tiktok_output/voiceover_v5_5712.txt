1918, Australia’s skies buzzed with phantom aircraft that terrified a nation. From March to May, towns reported mysterious planes slicing through clouds.[0.8s] Panic spread quickly. People feared a German bombing raid, recalling London’s 1917 air raids. Police logged dozens of reports, launching frantic investigations across states. The truth? No planes existed.[1.5s] It was mass hysteria. Rumors crossed the Tasman, sparking similar panic in New Zealand’s coastal towns. Officials warned of enemy aircraft, yet offered no concrete evidence. By June, sightings vanished, leaving a baffling chapter in wartime folklore. Today, historians view the panic as a striking example of collective imagination.
