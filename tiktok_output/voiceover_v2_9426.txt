In 1441 a tiny strip of Italy became a nation by mistake.[0.5s] Two surveyors chose different rivers, leaving 330 hectares unclaimed. The locals declared freedom, refusing taxes to Florence or the Papacy. Cospaia thrived as a duty‐free zone, trading salt and goods. In 1574 they began cultivating tobacco, Europe’s newest addiction.[1s] When the Pope banned smoking, Cospaia’s tobacco flourished unchecked. For centuries it existed without army, prisons, or even laws. In 1826 the Papal State finally dissolved the republic, ending freedom.[0.8s] Today, Cospaia is a quiet hamlet, but its legacy lives on.
