A harmless pig ignited a border crisis between the United States and Britain. The San Juan Islands sat on a vague line drawn by the 1846 Oregon Treaty. Both nations claimed the islands, each insisting their maps showed rightful ownership. [0.5s] On June 15, 1859, farmer Lyman Cutlar shot a roaming pig in his garden. The pig belonged to a Hudson’s Bay Company employee, who demanded $100 compensation. Britain sent naval warships; the U.S. dispatched troops under Captain George Pickett. [1s] Cannons pointed across the bay, yet no shots were ever fired. Negotiations led to joint occupation, with each side keeping a small garrison. [0.5s] In 1872 German arbitration awarded the islands to the United States, ending the standoff. Today visitors see both flags waving—a reminder of a war fought over a pig.
