In 1814, London's bustling streets hid a brewing catastrophe waiting. Meux & Co's Horse Shoe Brewery stored massive wooden vats of fermenting porter. One vat's iron hoops slipped, but supervisors dismissed the danger.[1s] Minutes later, the 22‐foot vat burst, unleashing a roaring wave. Porter surged fifteen feet high, crashing into the St Giles rookery. Eight souls perished—children, mourners, and a teenage servant caught. Spectators flocked, paying to view the foamy wreckage and ruins. The disaster forced breweries worldwide to replace wooden vats with steel.[0.5s] Today, a theater stands where the torrent of beer once raged.
