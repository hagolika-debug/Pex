Off San Francisco, the Farallon Islands host millions of seabird nests. In the 1850s, gull and murre eggs became a booming[0.5s] cash crop. The Pacific Egg Company claimed exclusive rights, filing legal papers and building sheds. Rival fishermen ignored the claim, daring to harvest eggs on neighboring islets. June 1863, armed men clashed on South East Farallon—blood spilled.[1s] The gunfire echoed. One man died on each side; dozens more were wounded in minutes. The Egg Company emerged victorious, yet their monopoly lasted only a decade. Federal authorities finally banned egging in 1881, ending the savage scramble.[0.8s] The islands fell silent. Today, the Farallons protect wildlife, but the Egg War remains a bizarre footnote.
