In 1883, miners struck gold along the remote Amur River. They founded Zheltuga, a lawless town called the 'California of the East'. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived under a black‐and‐yellow flag. Miners elected leaders, collected taxes, and built a hospital, theater, and casino. [1s]Violence and crime ran rampant, with whips delivering brutal punishments. The Qing dynasty finally noticed, demanding evacuation after years of secrecy. [0.5s]In January 1886, Chinese troops razed the settlement to ashes. Survivors scattered, but the legend of Zheltuga lives on in forgotten history. [2s]A fleeting gold empire, erased but never truly silent.
