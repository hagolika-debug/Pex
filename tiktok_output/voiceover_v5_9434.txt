In 1849, Belgium launched its first overseas colonial adventure. Joint French‐Belgian warships sailed up Guinea’s Nunez River. They aimed to secure trade, backing King Lamina’s claim. But local rival Mayoré resisted, sparking a violent standoff. [1s] On March 24, cannons roared, burning the village of Boké. British merchants watched helplessly, their goods reduced to ash. [0.5s] France and Belgium covered up the attack, denying responsibility. The incident fueled the Scramble for Africa, shaping future empires. [2s] Today, the Rio Nunez affair remains a forgotten flashpoint.
