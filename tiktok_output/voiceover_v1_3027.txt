In 1930, Taiwan's mountains echoed with a silent fury. The Seediq people, oppressed for decades, finally rose against Japanese rule. At a school gathering, Mona Rudao led 300 warriors into a deadly ambush. [1s]Shots rang, children screamed, and 134 Japanese lives vanished instantly. Japanese forces responded with overwhelming force, deploying thousands of troops into the forest. [0.5s]The air turned toxic as mustard gas bombs rained down on hidden rebels. Over weeks, half the Seediq fighters perished, many choosing death to preserve honor. The brutal crackdown forced Japan to rethink its colonial policies toward indigenous peoples. [2s]Today, the Musha Incident remains a silent scar on Taiwan's history. Remembering the fallen reminds us that resistance can echo through centuries.
