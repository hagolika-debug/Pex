In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced an impossible trial. Pope Stephen VI ordered the exhumation of Formosus’ corpse. The rotting body was propped on a throne before the cardinals.[1s] A deacon spoke for the silent pope, answering accusations of treason. Stephen sliced off Formosus’ blessing fingers, stripping his papal power. The corpse was dressed as a layman and cast into the Tiber River.[0.8s] Public outrage erupted; riots forced Stephen to flee Rome. Later that year, a new synod nullified the macabre verdict. The Cadaver Synod remains a haunting reminder of power's darkest games.
