In the 1880s, a secret gold town sprang on the Amur. Prospectors from Russia, China, even America flooded the frozen valley.[0.5s] They named it Zheltuga, the ‘Yellow River’ republic of lawlessness. Makeshift saloons, opium dens, and gambling halls roared day and night. At its peak, ten thousand souls lived in tents and shacks. The Chinese Qing government declared the settlement illegal.[1s]They sent troops. In 1885, soldiers stormed Zheltuga, burning its wooden streets. Within weeks, the gold rush vanished, leaving only frost‐bitten ruins. Today, the ghost of Zheltuga whispers across the Amur’s icy silence.[1s]
