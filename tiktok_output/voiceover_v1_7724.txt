In 1872, Huntington, New York simmered with quiet, genteel tension. Charles G. Kelsey, a Sunday‐school teacher, fell for young Julia Smith. Julia chose wealthy Royal Sammis, sparking bitter jealousy in Kelsey. One night Julia’s aunt swapped rooms, catching Kelsey at the window. Masked townsfolk descended, stripping, tarring, feathering Kelsey in a barn. He vanished.[1s] Later fishermen found his tattered legs, still tarred. Huntington split: ‘Tars’ defended the mob, ‘Anti‐Tars’ condemned it. The town earned the nickname ‘Tar Town,’ phrase echoing forever. A dark reminder of vigilantism.[2s] History still whispers warning.
