In 1883, gold glittered on the Amur River's banks. Rogue miners erected a self‐styled republic, calling it Zheltuga. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived like a wild frontier. A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered over makeshift hotels and bustling casinos. Law was simple: twenty clauses, punishable by brutal whips. Multilingual traders bartered vodka for gold, speaking a strange pidgin. In 1885, Qing forces demanded evacuation, giving eight days.[1s] When the deadline passed, soldiers razed the town to ashes. Today, the ghost of Zheltuga whispers across the frozen Amur.[0.8s]
