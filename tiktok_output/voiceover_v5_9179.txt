In 1835, two American regions prepared to battle over a strip. The ‘Toledo Strip’ measured 468 square miles of coveted land. A faulty map misplaced Lake Michigan, sparking the border chaos. Ohio claimed the land, Michigan defended it with militia. [1s]Shots rang over surveyors' heads; only a horse was killed. President Jackson sided with Ohio, offering Michigan the Upper Peninsula. [0.5s]Michigan accepted, gaining mineral riches years later. The conflict ended without major bloodshed, but left lasting rivalry. Today, the Toledo War lives as a quirky footnote of U.S. history.
