A tiny Italian republic existed for centuries—born by a map mistake. In 1441, a narrow strip of land fell between Florence and the Papal State. Surveyors chose different rivers, leaving 330 hectares unclaimed—no one owned it. On February 25, locals declared freedom, becoming the Republic of Cospaia. [0.5s]No army, no taxes, no courts—just a council meeting in a church. Cospaia thrived on tobacco, smuggling salt—its economy flourished tax‐free. [2s]When the Pope banned smoking, Cospaia stayed the only exempt sanctuary. In 1826, Pope Leo XII forced the republic’s surrender with a single coin. [1s]The tiny hamlet remains, its flag still fluttering over a forgotten freedom.
