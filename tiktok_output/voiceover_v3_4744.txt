In Iowa’s heart, a hidden town once thrived. Founded around 1900 by Consolidation Coal Company’s ambitious plan. Black miners and European immigrants lived side by side, defying norms. Children of every hue learned together in integrated schools. The town’s YMCA boasted a roller‐skating rink, dancing nights. Hattie Hutchinson, Iowa’s only Black pharmacist, served the whole community. Nearby Klan threats never entered Buxton, thanks to company protection.[1s] When the coal seams dried, the town vanished almost overnight. Today, only foundations remain, whispering a forgotten utopia.[0.8s]
