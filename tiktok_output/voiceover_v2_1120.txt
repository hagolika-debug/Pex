In 1832, a tiny borderland declared its own independence. The dispute stemmed from the 1783 Treaty of Paris ambiguity. Three hundred settlers formed a constitution, electing Luther Parker as justice. Both Britain and the United States sent tax collectors,[1s] causing double taxation. Frustrated, locals proclaimed the Republic of Indian Stream on July nine. The fledgling nation lasted only three years[0.8s] before New Hampshire's militia arrived. A hardware‐store debt sparked a dramatic armed standoff near the border. In 1836, the republic dissolved, becoming part of New Hampshire forever. Today, only a plaque[1s] reminds us of this vanished nation.
