In the remote Amur River, a gold fever birthed a hidden republic. Between 1883 and 1886, thousands of Russian and Chinese prospectors built Zheltuga. Nicknamed the 'California on the Amur,' it thrived like a frontier town. Lawless, yet organized.[1s]They even drafted their own statutes. A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered, symbolizing the earth and the gold. Population swelled to twelve thousand, with cafés, theaters, even a casino. But women were banned; a secret brothel rose across the river. Tensions rose.[0.8s]The Qing Empire demanded immediate evacuation. In early 1886, Chinese troops razed the settlement, ending the gold haven. Today, Zheltuga lives only in dust and forgotten stories.
