October 21, 1904: The Russian Baltic Fleet sails toward the Far East. Fog blankets the North Sea.[1s] nerves already on edge. Russian officers hear distant lights, think Japanese torpedo boats approach. Captain Rozhestvensky orders fire,[0.5s] believing civilians are enemy vessels. British trawlers illuminated, helpless as shells rain down. Two fishermen die, several wounded; the sea turns crimson. Amid chaos, Russian ships mistakenly fire on each other. The incident nearly sparks war between Britain and Russia. An international commission forces Russia to pay £66,000 compensation.[0.8s] History remembers the tragedy.
