In 1900, a quiet Michigan village vanished in flames. The Burt Lake Band of Chippewa and Ottawa called it home. Treaties promised them perpetual land rights, but greed ignored the promises. Land speculator John McGinn[0.5s] secured a dubious writ, summoning sheriffs. On October 15, deputies set fire to nineteen cabins.[1s] Only the 1838 St. Mary's church survived, a lone witness. Families fled, some hidden in nearby woods, others displaced forever.[0.8s] Decades later, courts denied restitution, leaving scars on the land. Today, descendants honor their ancestors, demanding recognition and justice.
