A single stolen cow sparked a six‐year war across medieval Belgium. In 1272, peasant Engorant de Jalhay was caught pilfering a prized heifer.[1s] Instead of a fine, local bailiff ordered his execution, igniting fury. Lord of Namur demanded reparations; Duke John I of Brabant answered with troops. On April 17, 1276, 2,400 men clashed in Ciney.[0.5s] Blood soaking fields. The city burned, its church aflame, while 500 villagers perished. King Philip III of France intervened,[1s] restoring the status quo ante bellum. Centuries later, the 'Route de la Vache' marks the path of this bizarre war.
