In 1863, a bizarre war erupted over seabird eggs.[0.8s] The Farallon Islands, 25 miles off San Francisco, housed millions of murres. Two rival companies fought for exclusive rights to collect those golden treasures. The Pacific Egg Company claimed the southeast island, demanding government protection. Armed men from the Egg Company met armed rivals on June 4.[1s] Shots rang, one man died on each side, blood stained cliffs. The conflict ended with the Egg Company’s sole control, but not for long. By 1881, the federal government finally evicted all eggers.[0.5s] Today, the Farallones protect wildlife, remembering a forgotten egg war.
