In the early 1830s, a tiny nation emerged between America and Canada. A vague treaty line[0.8s] left the border undefined, sparking a territorial showdown. On July 9, 1832, locals declared independence and drafted a constitution. They elected officials, collected taxes, and even printed their own stamps. Both U.S. sheriffs and British magistrates demanded taxes, igniting daily tensions. New Hampshire militia arrived,[1s] but the Republic stood firm. By August 5, 1835, the community voted to join New Hampshire. The border dispute lingered until the 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally resolved it. A stone marker today marks the spot where the Republic once stood. Three years of sovereignty—forgotten by many,[0.5s] but still fascinating.
