Deep in Oregon's Hells Canyon, thirty Chinese miners sought fortune. They braved rugged cliffs, hauling boats upriver to hidden gold.[0.5s] Late May 1887, a gang of horse thieves stalked the camp. At dawn, rifles cracked.[1s] Silence fell over the river. All thirty‐plus miners were gunned down, their bodies dumped downstream. The killers stole the flour gold, then vanished into the wilderness. News muted; no one prosecuted, the massacre sank into oblivion. [2s] Decades later, a clerk uncovered hidden court files in a dusty safe. 1995 memorial stones rose, honoring ten names finally remembered. A silent reminder that forgotten tragedies still echo through history.
