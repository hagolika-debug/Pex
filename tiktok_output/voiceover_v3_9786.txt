In the remote Amur River, a gold vein sparked a wild dream. 1883: prospectors flooded the banks, founding a makeshift town they called Zheltuga. Within months, the settlement swelled to thousands—its own frontier California.[1s] Russians, Chinese, even Europeans lived side‐by‐side, trading in a chaotic market. They forged their own laws, punishments delivered by spiked whips.[0.7s] Women were banned, yet a thriving underground vice network emerged. In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation—deadline passed, miners vanished. January 1886, Chinese troops razed the town, burning dreams to ash.[0.8s] Today, Zheltuga remains a forgotten gold ghost, whispered by historians.
