In 1835, two American neighbors nearly went to war. It was over a 468‐square‐mile strip called the Toledo Strip. A mistaken map placed Lake Michigan too far north[0.5s], sparking the dispute. Ohio claimed the land, Michigan defended it, both raised militias. The only bloodshed?[1s] A teenage scout stabbed a militiaman's arm. President Jackson sided with Ohio, refusing Michigan statehood. Michigan traded the prized Toledo Strip[0.5s] for the Upper Peninsula. Soon copper and iron turned the Upper Peninsula into a mining boom. Today, the tiny war lives on in textbooks, a forgotten border drama.
