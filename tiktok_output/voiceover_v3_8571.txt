In 1184, a royal assembly gathered beneath Erfurt's cathedral. The floor, rotted by years, couldn't bear the noble crowd. Suddenly, the timber gave way[0.8s], sending everyone crashing down. They fell through a lower floor into a massive underground cesspit. Hundreds drowned in filthy sewage[1s], a medieval nightmare. Only a few, like the king, clung to a stone alcove. The disaster claimed about sixty nobles[0.5s], reshaping regional politics. Today, the tragedy is a forgotten tale of rotten beams. Yet the story reminds us how fragile power can be. Remember the Erfurt Latrine Disaster, history's most gruesome collapse.
