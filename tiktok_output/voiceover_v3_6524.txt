On a quiet September morning, a German steamer faced Haitian rebels. The rebels seized the Markomannia, suspecting hidden weapons aboard. The German captain protested, but the Haitian gunboat pressed on. Later, Germany dispatched the cruiser SMS Panther to exact retribution.[0.5s] Panther opened fire, igniting the rebel ship's aft magazines. The explosion roared, engulfing the vessel in flames.[1s] The sea fell silent. Admiral Killick chose to stay, detonating his own ship's magazine. The United States watched, torn between doctrine and foreign aggression.[0.8s] History recorded a quiet controversy. Today, Haiti commemorates Killick, while the incident fades from textbooks.
