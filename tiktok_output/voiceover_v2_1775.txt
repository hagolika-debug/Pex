In the early 1800s, a tiny nation sprang up between US and Canada. Border confusion from the 1783 Treaty of Paris left the land unclaimed. Local settlers faced double taxes from both American sheriffs and British officials. Fed up, they declared independence on July 9, 1832,[1s]forming the Indian Stream Republic. They drafted a constitution, elected a council, and even printed their own currency. But neighboring New Hampshire soon demanded control, sending militia to the border. The militia arrived.[1s]The tiny republic faced its final showdown. After a brief standoff, the Republic capitulated and was annexed by New Hampshire. The border dispute finally settled in 1842,[0.5s]with the Webster‐Ashburton Treaty. Today, the area is Pittsburg, New Hampshire, but the story lives on.
