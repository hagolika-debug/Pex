In 1896, a war lasted less than an hour.[1s] It began when a sultan seized power without British permission. Britain demanded he step down; the deadline was 09:00 sharp. At nine o’clock, naval guns roared, lighting the palace aflame.[0.8s] The shells fell for just thirty‐eight minutes—record‐breaking brevity. Around five hundred Zanzibaris wounded or dead; only one British sailor injured. The British installed a puppet sultan, ending Zanzibar’s independence. History books label it the shortest war, but its impact lingered. A battle under a minute—proof that wars can be fleeting.[0.5s]
