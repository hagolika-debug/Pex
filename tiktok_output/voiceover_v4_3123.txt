In July 1184, nobles gathered in Erfurt for a crucial council. The meeting took place inside an ancient stone church with wooden floors. They aimed to settle a bitter feud between the Archbishop of Mainz and Ludwig III. Suddenly, the crowded floor gave way, plunging the assembly into darkness. They fell into a foul cesspit, water mixing with excrement[1s] and screams. Between sixty and a hundred nobles drowned, were crushed, or asphyxiated by stench. Miraculously, King Henry and Archbishop Conrad clung to iron rails, escaping death. The tragedy halted the council, but political power shifted dramatically afterward. History remembers the Erfurt Latrine Disaster as a grim reminder of medieval sanitation. Imagine the most powerful people of Europe meeting...[0.8s]...in a toilet.
