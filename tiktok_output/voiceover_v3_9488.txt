April 27, 1865: a river steamboat hidden tragedy ignites. Hundreds of Union prisoners, fresh from Andersonville, boarded the Sultana. The boat was designed for half that number, yet overloaded. Three boilers roared, then exploded.[1.5s] Darkness swallowed the river. Fire raced through wooden decks, trapping men in burning steel. Over 1,000 souls vanished into the cold Mississippi waters. Investigations blamed overcrowding, faulty repairs, and profit‐driven greed. The nation mourned, yet the tragedy faded.[2s] History remembered wars, not rivers. Today, the Sultana rests beneath Arkansas fields, a silent reminder.
