In 1867, an American barque met a deadly fate on Taiwan's cliffs. The Rover struck the treacherous Qixingyan reef during a violent monsoon.[1s] Only a few escaped, landing among the hostile Paiwan tribe's jungle. The Paiwan tribe slaughtered the survivors, even killing the captain's wife. U.S. consul Le Gendre rushed to China, demanding justice and a punitive expedition.[0.8s] The first American naval force was repelled by scorching heat and fierce resistance. It ignited the 1874 Mudan conflict, opening Taiwan to foreign intervention.[1.2s] Today, the Rover tragedy remains a forgotten chapter of colonial ambition and native resistance.
