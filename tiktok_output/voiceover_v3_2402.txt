On February 9, 1913, the night sky lit up unexpectedly. A line of fireballs marched across the heavens, one after another.[0.5s] Unlike typical showers, they had no radiant—no single point of origin. The procession lingered, each fireball visible for thirty seconds, lasting five minutes.[1s] Witnesses across Canada and the US feared the world was ending. Thunder-like booms rattled homes, as if the sky itself roared.[1.2s] Astronomers concluded a temporary Earth satellite broke apart, creating the fireball chain. Today, the Great Meteor Procession remains a rare celestial mystery. Look up tonight; the cosmos may still hold unseen parades. Remember 1913, when stars marched across our night.
