Eighty-three, gold glittered on the Amur, birthing a secret frontier. Russians and Chinese miners built a bustling town overnight. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors called it 'California on the Amur'[0.5s]. They elected a president, levied taxes, and raised a black‐yellow flag. Punishments were brutal—whips, lashes, even death for crimes.[1s] The town boasted hotels, theaters, a casino, and a menagerie. Chinese officials finally demanded evacuation; miners ignored the warning. Qing troops arrived, razed the settlement, and erased it from maps.[0.8s] Today, Zheltuga remains a lost gold kingdom, whispered only by historians.
