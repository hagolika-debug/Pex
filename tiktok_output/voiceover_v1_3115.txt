[1s]In 1878, a night on the Thames turned deadly. The paddle steamer Princess Alice left Sheerness, packed with hopeful families. At dusk, near Woolwich, she collided with the massive cargo ship Bywell Castle. [2s]The hull tore open, water rushed in, panic erupted among passengers. Victorian dresses weighed them down, many struggled to stay afloat. [1s]Rescue boats arrived, but sewage‐filled river turned a death trap. Between 600 and 700 souls vanished, some bodies drifted miles downstream. The tragedy forced stricter safety laws and spurred public swimming pool construction. Today, a modest memorial in Woolwich whispers the forgotten river horror.
