A tiny Italian republic existed for 385 years—by accident. In 1441, two surveyors missed a half‐kilometer strip. The forgotten land fell between Florence and the Papal State.[0.5s] Its 350 peasants declared freedom, because no one claimed taxes. Cospaia became a tax‐free hub, trading salt, silk, and ideas. When tobacco arrived in 1574, the republic profited wildly.[1s] The Pope excommunicated smokers, yet Cospaia ignored the decree.[0.8s] For centuries it survived because powerful neighbors needed its loophole. In 1826, Pope Leo XII finally dissolved the free enclave.
