In 1810, tiny Banda Islands sparked a revolt that rattled empires. These islands held the world’s only nutmeg, a priceless spice. The Dutch VOC monopolized nutmeg, crushing local communities with brutal force. By 1810, the Dutch garrison was weak[1s], soldiers unpaid and discontent. British ships arrived, offering the rebels a chance to topple the monopoly. Local leaders, Asian traders, and mutinous soldiers united[0.5s] in fierce street battles. The British seized the forts, briefly ending Dutch control over nutmeg. Yet within years, the islands reverted[1.2s] to Dutch rule, the revolt forgotten. This tiny spark showed empires that even spices could ignite wars. Remember Banda’s 1810 uprising—a forgotten chapter that shaped global trade.
