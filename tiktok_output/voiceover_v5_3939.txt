In 1835, Ohio and Michigan nearly ignited a war over a strip.[0.5s] A faulty map misplaced Lake Michigan, shifting the intended border. Both sides claimed the 468‐square‐mile Toledo Strip as theirs. Governor Lucas sent 600 militiamen; Mason mustered a thousand. Shots rang out at Philips Corners, but only a penknife wound.[1s] The only fatal casualty? A horse named Lewis E. Bailey. President Jackson declared the strip part of Ohio, ending the standoff.[0.8s] Michigan surrendered the strip, gaining the Upper Peninsula as compensation. Iron and copper later turned the so‐called wasteland into a mining boom. A quirky border fight that shaped two states forever.
