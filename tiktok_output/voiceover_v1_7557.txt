In October 1912, a hidden Tasmanian mine ignited a deadly blaze. A pump house on the 700‐foot level erupted into raging flames. Workers scrambled, smoke thickened, hope fading fast.[1s] Rescue crews rushed, bringing breathing gear aboard the SS Loongana. The ship crossed Bass Strait in thirteen hours, a record then. Inside, miners clung to darkness, letters left behind.[0.8s] Albert Gadd re‐entered the inferno, sacrificing himself for strangers. Forty‐two lives lost, many more rescued after a hundred hours. Today, the tragedy whispers through Tasmania’s hills, a forgotten fire.
