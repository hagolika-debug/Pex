In 1272, a single stolen cow sparked a regional war. A peasant stole a cow during a jousting tournament in Ciney. Caught, he paid a fine, yet bailiffs executed him anyway.[1s] His lord demanded restitution, dragging the Marquis of Namur into conflict. Bishop John of Liège allied with Namur, while Brabant and Luxembourg joined the fray. April 1276, Condroz burned, Ciney razed, flames devoured the town.[0.8s] Thousands died; even the Count scoffed, 'Three gentlemen for a villein!' King Philip III of France intervened, restoring the status quo ante bellum. The War of the Cow faded[0.5s], but its legend endures. Imagine a war over a single cow—history’s oddest feud.
