In 1883, miners struck gold along the remote Amur River They founded Zheltuga, a wild frontier nicknamed 'California on the Amur' At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors of many nations lived there A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered over makeshift banks, courts, and hospitals Gambling halls called Monte Carlo drained fortunes, while vodka fueled nightly brawls [1s]But the Qing Empire soon learned of the illegal gold haven Chinese troops demanded evacuation; miners vanished into the frozen forest [0.8s]In January 1886, Qing forces razed the town, ending the republic Today, Zheltuga remains a ghost story of lawless gold fever
