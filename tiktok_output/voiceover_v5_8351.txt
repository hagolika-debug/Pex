In 1925, a stray dog ignited a cross‐border crisis. A Greek soldier chased his dog across the Demir Kapia pass. Bulgarian sentries opened fire, killing the soldier and a captain. Greece demanded apology and reparations, then invaded the town of Petrich. [1s]The tiny flashpoint threatened a wider war in the Balkans. The League of Nations stepped in, ordering an immediate cease‐fire. [0.5s]Bulgaria received £45,000 compensation; Greece withdrew its troops. This ten‐day standoff proved the League could enforce peace—temporarily. A stray dog, a border, a lesson: small sparks can ignite nations.
