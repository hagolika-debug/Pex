In 1900, England faced a silent epidemic hidden in pints. Over 6,000 people fell ill after drinking their daily brew. The culprit? Arsenic‐tainted invert sugar added to save brewing costs. Bostock & Co. supplied the contaminated sugar, unknowingly poisoning taverns nationwide. [1s]Doctors misdiagnosed the symptoms as alcoholic neuropathy. Only after months of mystery did investigators detect arsenic in the brew. [0.8s]The Royal Commission traced the poison to sulfuric acid laced with arsenic. The scandal forced breweries to dump thousands of barrels into city sewers. Legislation tightened, but the tragedy left a silent decline in birth rates.
