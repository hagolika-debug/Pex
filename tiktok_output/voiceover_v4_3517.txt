In the remote Amur basin, gold sparked a wild experiment. Russian and Chinese prospectors founded Zheltuga, a lawless gold town. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived under a black‐yellow flag. They called it 'California on the Amur'.[0.8s] A bustling frontier city rose overnight. Law was written on wooden boards, punishments meted with whips. Women were banned, yet a secret trade persisted.[0.5s] Miners paid fines to keep it hidden. In 1885, Qing officials finally discovered the illegal gold colony. A Chinese detachment razed the town, burning it to ashes.[1s] By 1886, Zheltuga disappeared, leaving only legends of its lawless gold rush.
