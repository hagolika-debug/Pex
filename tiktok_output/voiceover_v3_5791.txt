In 1900, a remote island became home to thousands of Boer prisoners. Napoleon's exile grounds now housed enemies of the British Empire. First transport arrived on April 11, 514 men stepping ashore. They camped on Deadwood Plain, a windswept canvas city.[1s] Prisoners built tin towns, calling them Blikkiesdorp and Rag Town. Sports thrived: cricket matches echoed across the plain each weekend. A daring escape on a fishing boat ended in farcical failure.[0.5s] The war left economic boosts, higher food prices and new friendships. When the war ended, some Boers stayed, marrying locals and becoming legends.
