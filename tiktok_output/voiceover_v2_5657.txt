In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced the ultimate courtroom.[1s] Pope Stephen VI ordered Formosus' corpse exhumed from its tomb. The rotting body was propped on a throne, silent witness. A deacon answered for the dead, pleading innocence. Stephen accused him of perjury, illegal papacy, and ambition.[0.8s] The verdict? Guilty. The pope's three blessing fingers were severed. His corpse was stripped, dressed as a layman, then cast into the Tiber. Crowds rioted, Stephen was deposed and strangled months later. The Cadaver Synod was nullified, but its horror haunts history.[1.2s]
