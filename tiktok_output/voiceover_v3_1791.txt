In 1900, a forgotten massacre unfolded on Russia's Far East frontier. Russian troops forced thousands of Chinese residents into the icy Amur.[1s] Lieutenant‐General Gribskiy ordered them to drown, sparing none. Men, women, and children were shoved into the water, then shot. Over three thousand lives vanished, erasing an entire community.[0.8s] The massacre remained hushed, hidden by shifting Sino‐Russian politics. Only recent testimonies bring the tragedy back into daylight.[0.5s] Remembering the Amur pogrom reminds us: history's shadows linger.
