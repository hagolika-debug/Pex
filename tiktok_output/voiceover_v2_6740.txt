In 1932, Australia declared war on a flock of emus. Veteran farmers faced starving wheat fields, devoured by thousands of migrating birds. Desperate, they begged the government for machine guns to thin the emu hordes. The army arrived, armed with Lewis guns, ready for a feathered battle. First volley missed; emus sprinted away, scattering like tanks on the plain.[1s] Then the gun jammed, only twelve birds fell before chaos returned. After weeks, only a few hundred emus died, while soldiers lost dignity.[0.8s] The government withdrew, and emus roamed free, becoming national legends. Today, the Emu War reminds us: nature can outwit any weapon.
