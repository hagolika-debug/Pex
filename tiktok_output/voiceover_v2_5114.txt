In 1863, a bizarre war erupted over bird eggs. The remote Farallon Islands hosted the West’s largest seabird egg harvest. Pacific Egg Company claimed exclusive rights, building huts and landing docks. Rival fishermen and eggers resisted, sparking years of violent tension. President Buchanan’s 1859 order declared the islands federal, but ignored. June 4, 1863: armed men clashed on the cliffs.[1s] Blood stained the sand. Two men died, four wounded, yet the Egg Company won. The conflict ended the rivalry, but federal eviction came in 1881. Today, the faraway islands host seals, not egg‐hunters, forgotten by history.
