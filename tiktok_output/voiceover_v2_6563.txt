Off California’s coast, the Farallon Islands hide a secret. In the 1850s, entrepreneurs harvested millions of gull[0.5s] and murre eggs. The Pacific Egg Company claimed exclusive rights, sparking fierce rivalries. Competing crews stormed the islands, guns hidden among the cliffs. June 4, 1863—shots rang out across the rookeries[1s]Blood stained sand. One man fell on each side; the island’s silence returned. The Egg Company[0.8s] secured control, but nature paid a heavy price. By 1881, the federal government finally evicted all eggers. Today, the Farallons protect wildlife, remembering the bloody egg war.
