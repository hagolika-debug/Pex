In 1933, rural Virginia towns were haunted by a mysterious gas attacker. Late one Christmas night, the Huffman family smelled a foul, choking odor. Within minutes, the wife vomited, the daughter collapsed, doctors rushed in. Neighbors armed themselves, patrolling streets with shotguns, fearing an invisible killer. The press sensationalized each incident, dubbing him the ‘Mad Gasser.’[1s] Police offered a $500 reward, yet the gas attacks vanished as quickly. Some victims later admitted the fumes were likely hoaxes, sparking mass hysteria. Historians now view the saga as a 1930s panic echoing WWI gas fears.[0.8s] A chilling reminder: collective fear can create monsters out of thin air.
