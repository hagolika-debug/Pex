In the dusty town of Porum, Oklahoma, 1906, tensions boiled. Judge Thomas Hester guarded his cattle empire, while the Davis clan fought back. Accusations of rustling sparked a vendetta, igniting a brutal range war. Cicero Davis fell outside his home in 1906[1s], blood staining the frontier. Vigilante groups formed, barns burned, and the town echoed with gunfire. May 1911[0.5s], thirty masked men stormed the Starr house, guns blazing. The shootout lasted ten minutes, leaving several dead and Hester’s son wounded. Families fled, the war faded, and history buried the bloodied saga. Today[0.8s], Porum’s silent streets whisper a forgotten chapter of the Old West. Remember the Porum Range War—history’s hidden fire still burns within.
