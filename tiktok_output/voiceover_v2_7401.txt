In July 1900, a Siberian city faced a deadly crisis. The Boxer Rebellion roared across the border, igniting fear. Blagoveshchensk, perched on the Amur River, swelled with Chinese traders. Local officials panicked, ordering all Chinese residents to leave immediately. Without boats, they were forced to swim across the icy current.[1s] Cossacks fired as people flailed; dozens drowned in seconds. The massacre was reported, then silenced.[0.5s][2s] Years passed; the tragedy faded from textbooks and memory. Today, a small museum whispers the story to those who listen. Remembering hidden horrors helps us guard against future panic.
