In the 1840s, New York farmers donned calico masks to defy landlords.[1s] For centuries, patroons owned vast estates, demanding impossible rents from tenant farmers. When Stephen Van Rensselaer died, heirs demanded back payments, sparking revolt. July 4, 1839, rebels gathered atop Helderberg, declaring independence from feudal rule.[0.8s] They formed rent strikes, disguised as 'Calico Indians', tarring and feathering tax collectors. Governor Seward sent militia, but the rebels held their ground for years.[1.2s] In 1845, the Anti‐Rent Party helped pass a constitution outlawing long leases. The fight ended feudal tenures in New York, echoing today’s housing justice battles.
