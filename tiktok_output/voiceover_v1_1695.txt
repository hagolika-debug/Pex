In the remote Amur River, gold sparked a wild empire. 1883 miners from Russia and China built a lawless town.[0.5s] They named it Zheltuga, the “California on the Amur.” Population exploded to twelve thousand, a melting pot of cultures. A black‐and‐yellow flag flew over bustling streets and casinos. Miners created their own laws, punishments administered with whips. [1s]Gold fever blinded them; fortunes rose and vanished overnight. In 1885 Chinese troops demanded evacuation; the town resisted briefly. [2s]By early 1886 Zheltuga was razed, its legend lives on.
