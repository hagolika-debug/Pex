In 1657, a whispered rumor shattered Japan’s secret Christian haven. A farmer named Hyōsaku mentioned a miraculous boy to his brother‐in‐law. The story reached Nagasaki magistrate, igniting the shogunate’s anti‐Christian machine. Without proof, officials ordered a domain‐wide sweep of the Kōri district.[0.8s] Six hundred hidden believers were seized; four hundred fifty executed in brutal raids. Families were forced to trample fumi‐e images, then secretly pray behind closed doors. [1s]Among them, an eleven‐year‐old girl spent sixty‐four years in a dank cell. The crackdown exposed Japan’s most sophisticated religious surveillance, yet faith survived underground. [0.5s]The Kōri Debacle reminds us: whispers can topple empires.
