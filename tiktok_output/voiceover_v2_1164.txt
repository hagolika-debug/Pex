In March 1867, the American barque Rover vanished off Taiwan's cliffs. A sudden storm slammed her onto coral reefs, tearing the hull apart. Only a handful survived, crawling onto a remote Paiwan village. Mistaking the captain's wife for a man, the tribe executed her brutally.[0.5s] Fourteen crew members, including the captain, were slain in revenge. America demanded justice.[1s]The navy prepared a punitive expedition. Tropical heat and fierce jungle ambush crippled the Marines, forcing retreat. The incident sparked the 1874 Mudan Conflict, paving Japan's Taiwan invasion. Today, the Eluanbi Lighthouse marks the tragic waters where Rover fell.
