In 1157, a single trout sparked a deadly uprising. A shoemaker promised the prized fish, but the steward intervened.[0.5s] Steward arrested the shoemaker, igniting anger among townsfolk. Villagers stormed the regidor’s house, setting it ablaze. Knights gathered in Santa María; rebels locked them inside.[1s] The inferno consumed the clergy, sealing the town’s fate. Fleeing to Portugal, they begged King Ferdinand for pardon.[1.2s] Ferdinand granted mercy, exiling the oppressive governor. The trout’s legacy lives on in Zamora’s legends.
