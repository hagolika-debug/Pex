In the early 1800s, a tiny nation vanished unnoticed. Nestled in dense forests between New Hampshire and Quebec. A vague 1783 treaty line sparked a three‐year dispute.[1s] On July 9, 1832, locals declared the Indian Stream Republic. They drafted a constitution and elected a council of leaders. Both British and American sheriffs taxed the same settlers simultaneously. New Hampshire militia marched in, forcing the republic to surrender.[0.8s] The 1842 Webster‐Ashburton Treaty finally resolved the border dispute. A historic marker today still whispers the story of this lost nation.[1.2s]
