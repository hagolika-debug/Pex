In 1814, London’s streets turned into a beer‐filled tsunami. Meux’s massive wooden vat held 3,500 barrels of fermenting porter. A clerk spotted a slipping iron hoop, but bosses dismissed it. At 4:30 pm the vat exploded, releasing a towering wave.[1s] Fifteen‐foot beer flood smashed walls, swept through New Street. Eight neighbors died, some while attending a mournful wake. Spectators paid to watch the ruined brewery, turning disaster into spectacle.[0.5s] The catastrophe forced breweries worldwide to replace wooden vats with concrete. Today, the Dominion Theatre stands where beer once flooded the streets.[0.8s]
