In the 1880s, a gold rush birthed a secret frontier. Nestled on the Amur River, miners forged a wild city. At its peak, 12,000 prospectors from Russia and China converged. They built hotels, theaters, even a casino called Monte Carlo. A self‐made code enforced brutal punishments—whips for even minor crimes. [1s] When Qing officials finally intervened, the republic faced a ruthless siege. In early 1886, soldiers razed the town, erasing its legend. [0.8s] Women were banned, yet a hidden red‐light district thrived nearby. Today, Zheltuga remains a forgotten ‘California on the Amur’. A ghost town whispers stories of lawless gold and vanished dreams.
