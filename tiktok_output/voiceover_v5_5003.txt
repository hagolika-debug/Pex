In 1883, gold sparked a rogue kingdom on the Amur River. Russian and Chinese miners built a bustling city overnight, calling it Zheltuga. Population swelled to twelve thousand, a multicultural mix of adventurers. They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, symbolizing soil and gold.[1s] Law courts, hospitals, even a casino called Monte Carlo thrived. But the Qing dynasty soon discovered the illegal outpost.[2s] Chinese soldiers arrived, demanding evacuation within eight days. The settlement burned, scattering survivors across Siberia and Manchuria. Today, Zheltuga is a forgotten ghost, a wild gold legend.
