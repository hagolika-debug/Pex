In the wilds of Siberia, a gold rush birthed a rogue republic. By 1883, miners from Russia and China built a bustling town. They called it Zheltuga, the ‘California on the Amur’.[1s] At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived, gambling, drinking, and striking gold. Lawless? No. They drafted a twenty‐article code, even built a hospital.[0.8s] Women were banned, yet a thriving sex trade erupted across the river. The Qing dynasty finally intervened, sending troops to crush the outlaw city. In January 1886, soldiers burned Zheltuga to ashes, erasing its legend. Today, the ruins lie silent,[0.5s] a forgotten chapter of gold‐driven ambition.
