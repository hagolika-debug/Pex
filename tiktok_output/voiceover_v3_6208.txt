In 1883, gold fever sparked a wild frontier on the Amur River. Russian and Chinese miners built a bustling town they called Zheltuga. At its peak, twelve thousand prospectors lived without law[1s] or women. They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, branding their gold haven. A makeshift court enforced brutal punishments[0.5s], lashes echoing through camps. The Qing dynasty finally noticed, demanding the miners leave within eight days. When the deadline passed, Chinese troops returned, torching the town. By early 1886, Zheltuga was reduced to ashes and memory. Today, the silent river hides the story of a forgotten republic[0.8s].
