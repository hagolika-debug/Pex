On Christmas Eve 1913, miners gathered for a festive celebration. The crowded Italian Hall overflowed with families, hopes, and laughter. Suddenly, a voice shouted ‘Fire!’[0.5s]—no flames, just panic. People surged down the narrow stairs, doors jammed against frantic bodies. Seventy‐three souls, mostly children, were crushed beneath the crowd. The tragedy silenced a union strike, sparking nationwide outrage[1s]. Investigations blamed an anti‐union agitator, but the true culprit vanished. Today, only a lone arch marks the site’s haunting memory. Remember the forgotten Christmas massacre[1.2s], a warning of fear’s power.
