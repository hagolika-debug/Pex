In 897 Rome, a dead pope faced a courtroom. Pope Stephen VI ordered Formosus' corpse exhumed from its tomb. He propped the corpse on a throne[1s], like a ruler. A deacon was forced to answer for the dead pontiff. The dead pope was accused of perjury[0.5s] and illegal papacy. Judges stripped his papal vestments, cutting off his blessing fingers. The corpse was declared guilty, his acts nullified forever. The body was tossed into the Tiber[2s], floating like a cursed relic. Public outrage rose, ending Stephen’s reign and banning corpse trials.
