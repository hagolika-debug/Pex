In 897, Rome held the most bizarre trial ever. Pope Stephen VI ordered a dead pope to stand on a throne. The corpse of Pope Formosus was exhumed[0.5s], rotting, yet dignified. He was forced to answer accusations of perjury and ambition. The dead pope remained silent.[1s]The judges shouted. Stephen VI sliced off Formosus' three blessing fingers, a grotesque sentence. Finally, the corpse was tossed into the Tiber, floating like a nightmare. The public erupted, deposing Stephen, who soon met a violent end. Later, the church banned posthumous trials, sealing this macabre chapter forever.
