In 1810, tiny Banda Islands sparked a rebellion that stunned empires. They guarded the world’s only source of coveted nutmeg and mace. The Dutch East India Company enforced a brutal monopoly, wiping out locals. But the Napoleonic Wars weakened the Dutch, inviting British eyes.[1s] Asian troops and Bandanese merchants seized forts, demanding freedom. British warships arrived, allying with mutineers to topple the garrison.[0.5s] For months, Banda briefly lived under British control, trading freely. When peace returned, the Dutch reclaimed the islands, ending the revolt. Today, the forgotten uprising reminds us how tiny ports can shift empires.[0.8s]
