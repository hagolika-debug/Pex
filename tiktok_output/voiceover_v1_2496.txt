Imagine a court where the defendant[0.5s] is... dead. In 897 AD, Rome witnessed the most bizarre trial ever. Pope Stephen VI, driven by vengeance, summoned a dead pope. He ordered Formosus' corpse exhumed from the Tiber riverbed. The corpse was propped on a throne, a deacon answered. Charged with perjury and illegal papal transfers, the dead stood silent[1s]. The synod found him guilty, stripped his papal robes. Formosus' body was thrown into the river, causing public outrage[0.8s]. The Cadaver Synod shattered the Church, leading to chaotic papacies. A grim reminder: power can turn even death into a weapon.
