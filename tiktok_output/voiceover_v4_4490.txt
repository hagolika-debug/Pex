A forgotten 1871 massacre sparked Japan's first overseas war.[1s] Four Ryukyuan tribute ships left Shuri, chasing homeward winds. A typhoon shattered one vessel near Taiwan’s rugged southeast coast. Sixty‐six castaways trudged ashore, unaware of looming danger. They met Paiwan tribesmen, who first offered food, then betrayal.[0.8s] At night, the tribe turned savage—fifty‐four sailors slain. Nine escaped, rescued by a Hakka trader, and returned home months later. Japan seized the tragedy, launching the 1874 Taiwan Expedition.[0.5s] Today, the Mudan incident reminds us how small lives spark empires.
