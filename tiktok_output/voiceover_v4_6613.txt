In 897, Rome staged a trial no one expected.[0.5s] Pope Stephen VI exhumed his predecessor, Pope Formosus, from the tomb. The dead pontiff was propped on a throne, silent witness.[1s] Clergy shouted accusations of perjury and illegal papacy at the corpse. A deacon answered for the dead, mimicking a trembling voice. Stephen VI ordered Formosus’ fingers cut off, symbolically ending blessings.[0.8s] The corpse was stripped, dressed as a layman, then cast into the Tiber. The public erupted, deposing Stephen, who later died in prison. The Cadaver Synod remains history’s strangest courtroom, a macabre warning.
