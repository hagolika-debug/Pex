In 1883, gold glittered along the Amur’s remote banks. Russian and Chinese prospectors erected a lawless town, naming it Zheltuga[0.8s]. Within months, twelve thousand miners flooded the settlement, calling it “California on the Amur.” They forged a black‐and‐yellow flag, a symbol of gold and rebellion. A self‐made court met under an oak, meting out lashes for theft. [1s]The town boasted a casino, a theater, even a circus troupe. Women were banned, spawning a hidden red‐light district across the river. [0.5s]In 1885, Qing officials demanded evacuation, warning of imminent attack. When soldiers returned in 1886, they set the town ablaze, erasing its gold‐dust dreams. Today, only whispers remain of Zheltuga, the forgotten republic that glittered briefly.
