In 1897, a peaceful march turned into a deadly tragedy. Immigrant coal miners from Eastern Europe demanded fair wages. Sheriff James Martin and deputies confronted them at Lattimer. Orders were ignored, tension rose,[0.5s] and gunfire erupted. Nineteen men fell, many more wounded, the massacre shocked the nation. The press labeled the victims as foreign invaders, fueling outrage. Trials absolved the deputies,[1s] leaving families without justice. Decades passed before a modest memorial rose in 1972. Today, the forgotten Lattimer Massacre reminds us of labor's fight.[1s]
