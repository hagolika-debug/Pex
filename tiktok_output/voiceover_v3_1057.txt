In 1883, miners struck gold on the Amur River's wild banks. They built a makeshift city, calling it the Zheltuga Republic. At its peak, 12,000 prospectors from five continents gathered. Gold flowed freely.[1s]But lawless violence roamed the streets. They elected a president, Karl Fassi, earning 400 rubles monthly.[0.5s] A black‐and‐yellow flag waved over bustling hotels and a casino. The Qing dynasty finally learned of the rogue town.[0.8s]They moved in. In January 1886, Chinese troops razed Zheltuga, ending the gold dream. Survivors scattered, but legends of the 'California on the Amur' linger. Today, the river hides a forgotten republic, waiting for discovery.
