In Montana's remote Swan Valley, a tragedy unfolded in 1908. The Pend d'Oreilles, honoring a 1855 treaty, hunted freely beyond reservation lines. But Montana's new game laws ignored federal rights, demanding state permits.[0.5s] Eight family members entered Swan Valley, buying permits they didn't truly need. State warden Charles Peyton stalked them, demanding licenses and provoking tension.[1s] On a cold October morning, Peyton fired first, killing two tribal leaders. The young son grabbed a gun, striking Peyton, but was instantly shot. Clarice, pregnant and fierce, returned fire, ending the warden's reign.[0.8s] The deputy vanished; no one faced justice for the deadly massacre. The tragedy faded, yet descendants remember, preserving a painful legacy.
