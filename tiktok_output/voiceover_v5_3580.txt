In 1754, Windham’s quiet night turned into a terrifying mystery. A deafening chorus erupted, shaking shutters and waking every household. Colonists feared a French‐Indian raid, imagining enemy drums and gunfire. They sent a militia squad to the nearby pond for answers. What they found: thousands of bullfrogs battling in a flooded arena.[1s] The frogs died by sunrise, their bodies littering the water’s surface. Windham became the colonies’ laughingstock, inspiring ballads and mockery. Centuries later, copper frogs guard the Frog Bridge, honoring the legend.[0.5s] A strange war without weapons reminds us: history can sound absurd.[0.8s]
