In 1883, gold glittered along the Amur’s remote banks.[0.8s] Prospectors from Russia and China rushed to claim the glittering promise. They built Zheltuga, a lawless city nicknamed 'California on the Amur'. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived, speaking a chaotic pidgin. A black‐and‐yellow flag fluttered over hotels, theaters, and a bustling casino.[0.5s] Strict laws punished thieves with whips; even minor crimes earned brutal lashes. In 1885, Chinese officials finally discovered the hidden gold haven. They demanded evacuation; when ignored, they razed the settlement to ash.[1s] By 1886, Zheltuga vanished, a forgotten chapter of frontier ambition.
