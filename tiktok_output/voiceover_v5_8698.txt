In 1962, a small Tanzanian school erupted into unstoppable laughter. Three teenage girls giggled, and the contagion spread like wildfire.[0.8s] Within days, 95 of 159 pupils were doubled over, crying and laughing. Teachers watched helplessly as lessons turned into chaotic chorus of mirth.[1s] The school closed after 48 days, but the mania traveled beyond. Nearby villages reported 217 more victims, each bout lasting up to two weeks.[0.5s] Doctors labeled it mass psychogenic illness, sparked by post‐independence stress. The outbreak finally faded after eighteen months, leaving a baffling legacy.[1.2s] A reminder that emotions can spread faster than any virus.
