In the early 1800s, a hidden nation emerged between the US and Canada. Border ambiguity from the 1783 Treaty of Paris left the land unclaimed. Local settlers grew tired of double taxation from both American and British officials. On July 9, 1832, they declared independence, forming the Indian Stream Republic. They drafted a constitution, elected officials, and even printed their own paper.[0.5s] But tensions rose when New Hampshire militia demanded control of the territory. A standoff erupted, leading to a dramatic showdown over a hardware debt.[1s] By August 1835, the Republic surrendered, becoming part of New Hampshire. The quirky microstate vanished, yet its story reminds us borders are human constructs.[0.8s]
