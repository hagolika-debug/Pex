June 30, 1916, a hidden battlefield emerged near Richebourg. The Boar’s Head, a tiny German salient, earned its name. British 39th Division prepared a diversion, hoping to spare the Somme. At 3:05 am, artillery roared, shrouding the assault in darkness. Young volunteers charged,[1s] only minutes later gunfire turned the advance into carnage. [0.5s]The Germans, expecting them, unleashed relentless machine‐gun fire. Over a thousand men fell, marking the ‘worst day for Sussex’. [2s]The battle vanished from history books, yet its echo lingers. Remember the Boar’s Head, a forgotten sacrifice beneath the Somme’s shadow.
