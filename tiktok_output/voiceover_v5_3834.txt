Boston’s streets were about to be drenched in an unexpected tide.[1s] On January 15, 1919, a massive molasses tank catastrophically burst. Two million gallons of sugary syrup surged, towering up to forty feet. The sticky wave raced at thirty‐five miles per hour, flattening blocks. Twenty‐one people perished, many suffocated as the syrup hardened. Horses collapsed, their bodies stuck like flies on glue. Rescue crews fought the viscous nightmare.[2s]The city smelled sweet forever. Weeks of scrubbing couldn't erase the lingering molasses scent. Today, the disaster reminds us that even sweet things can destroy.
