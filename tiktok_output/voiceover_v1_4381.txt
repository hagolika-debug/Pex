Imagine a wave of beer flooding London streets, killing eight souls. On October 17, 1814, Meux's Horse Shoe Brewery erupted catastrophically. A 22‐foot wooden vat burst, releasing hundreds of thousands of gallons.[1s] A 15‐foot foam wall of porter surged through the cramped slums. Eight neighbors drowned, including a child sipping tea and mourners at a wake.[0.8s] Crowds watched the destruction, some paying to see the beer tide. The brewery survived, but wooden vats were replaced by safer concrete containers. Today, the site hosts the Dominion Theatre, remembering the forgotten beer tsunami. History’s strangest flood reminds us—sometimes disaster brews where you least expect.[1s]
