In the 1880s, gold fever birthed a hidden republic. Along the Amur River, miners forged a bustling town. At its peak, 12,000 prospectors from across the world gathered. They elected a president, collected taxes, and flew a black yellow flag. Strict punishments—whipping, exile—kept the lawless gold rush in check. The settlement boasted hotels, a casino, even a circus troupe. [1s] Qing authorities finally demanded evacuation, threatening force. In 1886, Chinese troops razed the town, ending the dream. [0.8s] The ghost of Zheltuga still whispers as 'California on the Amur'. Imagine a wild‐west city, hidden in Siberian forests—forgotten forever.
