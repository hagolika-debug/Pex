January 15, 1919, Boston’s North End awoke to an ordinary day. A massive steel tank[0.8s], brimming with two million gallons of molasses, loomed nearby. At 12:30 pm, a thunderous crack shattered the quiet harbor. Molasses surged like a wave[1s], fifteen feet high, roaring through streets. Cars were lifted, windows shattered, and horses stumbled in sticky chaos. Rescuers fought the thick syrup[0.5s], each breath a struggle for air. Twenty‐one souls perished, many suffocated beneath the sweet, suffocating tide. The city smelled of molasses for years, a lingering reminder. Lawsuits followed, forcing stricter building codes to prevent future catastrophes. Today, the tragedy lives in history, a sticky lesson never forgotten.
