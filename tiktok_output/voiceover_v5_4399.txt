January 15, 1919, Boston’s North End woke to a terrifying roar. A massive steel tank burst, unleashing two million gallons of thick molasses. The sticky wave[1s] surged 35 miles per hour, crushing everything in its path. Buildings toppled, a streetcar was thrown off the elevated tracks. Rescue crews waded through waist‐deep syrup, pulling victims from the brown nightmare. Twenty‐one lives lost, including horses, while the city smelled of molasses for years. The disaster sparked the nation’s first massive class‐action lawsuit[0.8s] against a corporation. New safety codes required licensed engineers, changing how industrial tanks were built forever. Today, a small park marks the site, reminding us that sweet can be deadly[2s].
