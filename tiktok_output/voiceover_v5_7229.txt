A tiny hamlet sparked a legal battle no one saw coming. Boston Corners sat at the crossroads of three states[0.5s]. In 1853, 3,000 fans gathered for a bare‐knuckle bout. John Morrissey faced James ‘Yankee’ Sullivan in a 37‐round marathon. The fight erupted into chaos[1s]; a riot broke out in round thirty‐seven. Morrissey won, and his victory reshaped the town’s destiny. Residents petitioned Congress[0.8s], demanding to join New York for better law enforcement. In 1855, Congress approved the transfer, redrawing the state line forever. Today, a modest marker hints at the fight that moved a border.
