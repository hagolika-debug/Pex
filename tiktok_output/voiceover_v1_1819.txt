A summer night in 1900, Hoboken's docks turned to inferno. Cotton bales ignited, feeding flames that devoured massive ocean liners. Explosions of turpentine and oil sent fire racing across the water. More than 300 souls perished, many trapped behind sealed portholes.[1s] Rescue boats struggled; some survivors crawled from coal bunkers after hours.[0.5s] The tragedy forced new safety rules: larger portholes for emergency escapes. A granite monument now marks the mass grave in North Bergen. Forgotten by most, this fire reshaped maritime regulations forever. Imagine standing on those burning piers—heat, smoke, panic, silence.[0.8s]
