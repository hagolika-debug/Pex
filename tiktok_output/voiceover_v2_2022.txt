Deep in the Amur, a secret gold city blossomed overnight. In 1883, prospectors struck gold and declared their own republic. Russians, Chinese, Jews, even Koreans lived side by side. They raised a black‐and‐yellow flag, symbolizing earth and gold[0.8s]. Miners drafted twenty clauses, punishments included whips and public lashes. The Casino Chita, nicknamed ‘Monte Carlo’, drained fortunes in minutes. Empress Cixi demanded evacuation; Chinese troops arrived, warning the gold rush[1s]. In January 1886, soldiers burned the settlement, erasing its streets forever. Today, the ghost of Zheltuga lives only in forgotten maps.
