July 24, 1915, Chicago River turned into a deadly playground. The SS Eastland, a 275‐foot excursion steamer, bristled with excitement. Over 2,500 workers, wives, and children squeezed onto its decks. Extra lifeboats and rafts added weight, making the hull dangerously top‐heavy.[1s] Within minutes, the vessel listed, then tipped like a turtle. Water rushed in; passengers screamed,[0.8s] panic erupted instantly. Rescue boats arrived, pulling survivors from the tilted hull. Nearly 850 lives lost, yet the tragedy faded from memory. Chicago buried the Eastland disaster, focusing instead on war headlines. A reminder: safety regulations can’t replace sound engineering and oversight.[2s]
