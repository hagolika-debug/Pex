In 1883, gold glimmered along the Amur's icy banks. Prospectors from Russia and China built a lawless[0.5s] frontier town. At its peak, twelve thousand miners lived under a black‐and‐yellow flag. They forged a unique pidgin language mixing Russian and Chinese tongues. A self‐imposed court met daily, meting out lashes for crimes. The settlement boasted hotels, theaters, even a casino dubbed 'Monte Carlo'. But Qing officials grew angry, demanding the illegal colony's evacuation. Chinese troops arrived, set fire[1s], and the town vanished in ash. Today[0.8s], the 'California on the Amur' is a forgotten legend.
