London 1858—summer heat turned the Thames into a toxic soup. Sewers dumped raw waste directly into the river, creating a foul cloud. The smell grew so unbearable that Parliament's windows were soaked in lime.[1s] [0.5s]MPs fled meetings, handkerchiefs pressed to noses, fearing suffocation. Queen Victoria’s royal cruise aborted within minutes—thousands fled the deck.[1s] Public outcry forced the government to fund massive sewer overhaul. Engineer Joseph Bazalgette designed an underground network that still runs today. The project saved millions of lives, ending cholera outbreaks forever. [0.8s]All because a summer stink forced London to clean its river.
