In 1919 Boston, a sweet nightmare erupted from a steel tank. 2.3 million gallons of molasses burst, flooding streets at thirty‐five miles per hour. People were swept away like toys, horses trapped in the sticky tide. The roar sounded like a train crash.[1s]The city froze in horror. Rescue cadets from USS Nantucket waded through syrup, pulling victims to safety. The sticky mess lingered months.[0.8s]Even summer air smelled of sugar. The disaster spurred new building codes, demanding engineers certify tank designs. Today, a plaque marks the site, reminding Boston of its sticky tragedy.
