October 21, 1904: Russian warships drifted into the North Sea. They feared invisible Japanese torpedo boats stalking their fleet. Mistaking humble trawlers for enemy vessels, they opened fire. Lights flashed.[1s]Sparks rose over terrified fishermen in darkness. Two men died, six wounded; the trawler Crane sank. Confusion grew as Russian ships began firing on each other. The world held its breath.[0.8s]War loomed on. Britain demanded answers; an international commission was formed. Russia paid £66,000, and a memorial still stands in Hull. A forgotten flashpoint that almost sparked a world war.
