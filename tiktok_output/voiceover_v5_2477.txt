In 1810, tiny Banda Islands sparked a forgotten spice revolt.[1s] The Dutch East India Company held a ruthless nutmeg monopoly for centuries. Bandanese farmers chafed under strict price controls and forced deliveries. Napoleon's wars weakened the Netherlands, inviting British interest in the archipelago. In mid‐1810, Asian troops and locals rose against Dutch forts.[0.8s] British forces landed, allied with mutineers, quickly overpowering the weak garrison. For a few years, Banda tasted a softer trade policy under British rule. After the Napoleonic peace, the Dutch reclaimed Banda, re‐imposing monopoly. The 1810 uprising reminds us small islands can shift empires.[1.2s]
