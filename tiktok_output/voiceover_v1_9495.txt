In the wild Amur River, a secret gold city rose. 1883 miners from Russia and China struck glittering veins, sparking frenzy. Within months, twelve thousand prospectors built a bustling frontier town. They named it Zheltuga, the 'California on the Amur'.[0.5s] Lawmakers drafted twenty clauses, whipping lawbreakers with brutal spiked whips. A black‐yellow flag fluttered over a casino where fortunes vanished. Silence fell when Qing forces demanded evacuation, giving eight days.[1s] In January 1886, Chinese troops razed the town, burning everything.[0.8s] Survivors scattered, but Zheltuga’s legend inspired future border gold rushes. A fleeting republic, now a ghost story etched in history.
